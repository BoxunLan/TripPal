Three Things a Fake Guangzhou Travel Agency Cannot Produce

A convincing fake travel agency is cheap to build. English website, testimonials, a stock photo of an office, a WeChat account that answers within a minute. None of that is evidence.
Three things cannot be faked, and checking all three takes about ten minutes: a licence number that decodes correctly, an entry in the national registry, and a contract generated by the government platform with a verification code printed on the first page. If an agency gives you all three, the rest is negotiation. If it balks at any one of them, stop.
Read the licence number
A Chinese travel-agency licence is not a random string. It is L-[province]-[scope]-[serial], and the scope letters tell you what the company is actually allowed to do.
Segment

Means

L

Travel agency

GD

Registered in Guangdong. A genuine Guangzhou agency starts L-GD-

GN

Domestic tours only (国内). Legal, but the lowest tier

GJ

International and inbound-qualified. This is the one you want as a foreign visitor

CJ

Outbound (出境) — authority to take Chinese citizens abroad. Not the same as inbound capability

That GJ/CJ distinction is where most guides get it wrong, including ones that tell you CJ is the mark of an agency equipped for foreigners. CJ is about sending Chinese nationals out. For an inbound itinerary, GJ is the broader authority.
If the province code is not GD, you are talking to an agency registered somewhere else that runs a branch in Guangzhou. That can be fine — verify the branch separately rather than assuming.

Check the national registry, not just the provincial one
China runs one public, free registry covering every licensed agency: the National Tourism Supervision Service Platform's query portal, publicity.mr.mct.gov.cn/web/notice, operated by the Ministry of Culture and Tourism. Search by the exact registered Chinese name, the licence number, or the Unified Social Credit Code. English trade names will not match — ask for the Chinese name.
It returns licence status, approved business scope, and the thing the provincial site does not consolidate: whether the company appears in the platform's 严重失信 (serious dishonesty) or 轻微失信 (minor dishonesty) penalty announcements. Suspensions and scope changes land here too.
Guangdong's own portal at wlt.gd.gov.cn is a reasonable second check and will show the registered name, legal representative and address. Use it to confirm the address matches what you were told, then put that address into Amap. You want a commercial tower, not a flat. Established agencies cluster on Tianhe North Road near the Sports Center metro, around Huanshi East Road in Yuexiu, and near the Chen Clan Academy in Liwan.
Insist on the electronic contract
This is the strongest single signal and the least known. A compliant agency issues a platform-generated electronic tourism contract carrying a unique verification code on page one, created through the same national platform. You sign it by scanning a QR code into the 全国旅游监管服务 WeChat mini-programme, which then holds your filed contract and itinerary where you can read them back.
An agency that will not issue an electronic contract, or that wants you to pay first and sign afterwards, has told you what it is. Paper or electronic, the contract has to spell out the itinerary, an itemised fee breakdown, refund and change rules, and insurance.
Most people do not need an agent at all
Worth saying before you spend anything. If you are arriving on the 30-day visa-free scheme, on 240-hour transit , or on an ordinary tourist visa with your own hotel bookings, an agency adds cost and nothing else. The visa-free list covers which passports are which, and what a US visa actually costs is $140 flat, not the $300-plus an agency will quote to handle it for you.
You need a licensed operator for four things:
A Tibet Travel Permit. Foreigners cannot file for one independently — it has to go through an agency holding a Tibet-issued licence, and the permit comes bundled with a booked tour.

Xinjiang border zones. Though check whether you need the agency at all: for Tashkurgan the 边防证 is free and the immigration desk at Kashgar Airport issues it in 15 to 30 minutes. The Tashkurgan route guide has the detail. The ¥200–400 an agency charges buys you three to seven days of not queuing.

An invitation-letter visa, where your consulate wants a formal itinerary document.

A packaged group tour out of Baiyun — the Guilin, Zhangjiajie and Yunnan circuits are the common ones.

Paying without getting stuck
Pay the corporate account. A request to send money to a staff member's personal QR code, a personal bank account, or cash is not a quirk of local practice — it is the recognised signal.
There is a mechanical trap here that catches people on larger bookings. Alipay and WeChat Pay bound to an international card work fine, but WeChat's foreign-card route caps at ¥6,000 per transaction, ¥50,000 a month and ¥60,000 a year, and adds roughly 3% on anything over ¥200. A ¥20,000 tour balance will not go through in one payment. Agree a bank transfer to the corporate account, or instalments, before you are standing in the office trying to settle. Ask for a fapiao in the company's name either way.
A normal sequence: enquiry with passport scan and dates, a written day-by-day proposal within one to three days, revisions, the electronic contract, a 30–50% deposit to the company account, permits or letters issued, balance once you hold the documents.
When to end the conversation
No licence number on the site or the contract, or hesitation when you ask for it.

Payment to an individual.

Full payment demanded before you have seen a draft itinerary.

An address that Amap shows as residential.

A Tibet permit promised in two days, or a Xinjiang border permit with no advance booking.

Verbal agreement only, or a WeChat thread offered in place of a contract.

An unsolicited DM in a Facebook group. Real agencies do not prospect that way.

Two lower-effort routes if the verification work is more than you want to take on. A four- or five-star hotel concierge in Zhujiang New Town keeps a vetted list and has every incentive not to send guests to a bad operator — ask for the introduction. Or use a CTS or CYTS branch: state-affiliated, English-speaking counters, no ambiguity about licensing, and prices at the top of the ranges above. CTS has a branch near Guangzhou East Railway Station; CYTS has a storefront near Beijing Road.
Two things to specify in writing
"English-speaking guide" and "guide fluent in English" are different claims, and you will find out which one you bought on day one. Ask for a short voice or video recording of the proposed guide speaking English before you confirm.
And check the hotels yourself on Trip.com rather than taking the star rating in the proposal. A "four-star" in an itinerary is sometimes an older three-star property. While you are at it, get the inclusions itemised — "lunch included" often means a fixed group set menu.
FAQ
How do I check a Chinese travel agency is real?
Ask for the registered Chinese name and licence number, then search them on the national registry at publicity.mr.mct.gov.cn. It shows licence status, approved scope and any dishonesty penalties. Then require a platform-generated electronic contract with a verification code before paying anything.
What does L-GD-GJ mean on a licence?
Travel agency, registered in Guangdong, holding international and inbound-qualified scope — the tier you want as a foreign traveller. GN is domestic-only; CJ is outbound authority for taking Chinese citizens abroad, which is a different thing from being equipped to handle you.
Can I get a Tibet permit without an agency?
No. Foreign travellers cannot file independently. It has to go through an agency holding a Tibet-issued licence and comes with a booked tour attached. Budget ¥800–1,500 and 10 to 15 days.
Why did my card payment to the agency fail?
Probably the cap. WeChat Pay on an international card allows ¥6,000 per transaction, ¥50,000 a month and ¥60,000 a year. Larger balances need a bank transfer to the corporate account or splitting into instalments — agree which in advance.
