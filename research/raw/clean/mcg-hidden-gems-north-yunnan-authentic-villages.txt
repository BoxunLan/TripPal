North Yunnan hides a string of stone-and-timber villages where Bai, Naxi, and Tibetan life still runs on agricultural calendars rather than tour-bus schedules. Skip the Lijiang Old Town crowds and head to Shaxi, Nuodeng, Yuhu, Xizhou, Baisha, Shigu, and Dukezong for homestays at ¥100-300 a night and landscapes that used to line the Tea Horse Road.

Shaxi Old Town (沙溪古镇)
Shaxi sits in a wide valley between Dali and Lijiang and is the only Tea Horse Road market town still standing with its original Friday market, theatre stage, and Xingjiao Temple intact. UNESCO and a Swiss restoration program saved it from concrete in the 2000s. Bring small CNY notes — half the ¥15-40 courtyard cafes still don't take Alipay or WeChat Pay cleanly for foreign cards.
Getting there: Take a 2-hour high-speed train from Kunming to Dali (¥145-220 on 12306), then a 2-hour shared van from Dali bus station to Shaxi (¥55-80, book through the homestay).

Stay: Bai family-run courtyards along Sideng Square, ¥180-300 a night in the main season.

Don't miss: Friday morning market, Xingjiao Temple (¥30), Ouyang Courtyard woodcarving.

Nuodeng Ancient Village (诺邓古村)
Nuodeng is a 1000-year-old salt-well village in Yunlong County, west of Dali, famous for Nuodeng ham cured in local salt. The village climbs a hillside in tiers of stacked mud-brick houses and ancestor halls. It featured on the CCTV documentary "A Bite of China," which briefly made the ham famous but did not flatten the village.
Getting there: Dali bus station → Yunlong County (¥55, 3 hours), then taxi ¥40-60 for the final 7 km. Didi is unreliable in Yunlong — call your homestay for pickup.

Stay: Three family homestays inside the village, ¥100-180 a night, breakfast usually ¥15.

Don't miss: The salt-brine well, the Jade Emperor Pavilion at sunset, and a ¥60-90 ham-and-vegetables family dinner booked the day before.

Yuhu Village (玉湖村)
Yuhu sits at the foot of Jade Dragon Snow Mountain, 10 km north of Lijiang, and was the base of American-Austrian botanist Joseph Rock from 1922-1949. His stone house is now a small free museum, and the Naxi community still farms below pine-forested slopes with clear glacial streams.
Getting there: Lijiang Old Town → Yuhu by Didi (¥40-55, 25 minutes) or shared van from the Gucheng bus stop (¥15).

Stay: Naxi courtyard homestays, ¥150-280 a night, often with wood-stove heat in shoulder season.

Don't miss: Joseph Rock's former residence (¥25), horseback loop to Wenhai Lake (¥150-200 per person, negotiate at the village gate).

Xizhou (喜洲)
Xizhou is the wealthiest Bai merchant town on the Erhai Lake plain, 18 km north of Dali Old Town, known for its tri-courtyard "three houses screening a wall" mansions and morning rice-flour Xizhou baba pancakes at ¥8-12 each. It is busier than Shaxi but still a real working town rather than a theme park.
Getting there: Dali Old Town → Xizhou by city bus #4 (¥3, 35 minutes) or Didi (¥35-50, 25 minutes).

Stay: Linden Centre is the well-known restored mansion at a premium (¥1000+); 10+ family courtyards run ¥200-400.

Don't miss: Yan family compound, Sifangjie square market, and an Erhai Lake sunset from the Haishe platform.

Baisha Old Town (白沙古镇)
Baisha is the original capital of the Mu Naxi kings before Lijiang Old Town was built, and still holds the Baisha Mural halls — 15th century Ming and Qing Buddhist-Daoist-Dongba frescoes that few tour groups visit. It is 8 km north of Lijiang Old Town and is much quieter than its famous neighbor.
Getting there: Lijiang Old Town → Baisha by Didi (¥30-40) or shared van from Mao Square (¥15).

Stay: Naxi courtyards in the main lane, ¥120-250 a night, most offer bike rental included.

Don't miss: Baisha Murals (¥30), Dr Ho's Chinese medicine clinic, and the sunset view of Jade Dragon Snow Mountain from the north gate.

Shigu Town (石鼓镇)
Shigu sits at the First Bend of the Yangtze, where the river makes a dramatic hairpin turn and changes course from south to northeast. The name means "Stone Drum" after a Ming-dynasty stone stele shaped like a drum at the town square. It was also a key ferry crossing for the Red Army's Long March in 1936.
Getting there: Lijiang bus station → Shigu (¥25-35, 90 minutes) or private taxi ¥250-300 one way.

Stay: Three Yangtze-view guesthouses by the bend, ¥100-180 a night.

Don't miss: The Stone Drum stele, the Long March Memorial, and the riverside teahouses serving ¥15 cups of yak-butter tea.

Dukezong Old Town (独克宗古城)
Dukezong is the 1300-year-old Tibetan quarter of Shangri-La (Zhongdian, 3200 m altitude). A 2014 fire destroyed part of the old town but most of the upper streets, Guishan Park, and the enormous prayer wheel were rebuilt or survived. It is the best base for exploring Songzanlin Monastery and the surrounding Tibetan grasslands.
Getting there: Lijiang → Shangri-La by 4-hour bus (¥75-95) or 2.5-hour new high-speed train (¥100-145 on 12306). Both stations are 10-15 minutes from the old town by Didi (¥15-25).

Stay: Tibetan family guesthouses with wood-stove heat, ¥150-300 a night. Altitude adjustment is real — drink 2 liters of water a day and avoid alcohol on arrival.

Don't miss: Sunset at the giant prayer wheel, Songzanlin Monastery (¥115, 5 km north), and Potatso National Park (¥138, 22 km east).

What to eat in each village
North Yunnan food is regional and changes every 50 km. A short eating guide for the villages covered:
Shaxi : Bai-style wood-fired goat-cheese cake ("rushan") ¥20-30, local wheat noodles with spicy peanut sauce ¥15, tea-smoked duck at the Friday market ¥35-45 per portion. Courtyard cafés on Sideng Square serve decent ¥40-70 set lunches.

Nuodeng : Nuodeng ham is the star — ask for a "one ham, three courses" family dinner (¥60-90 per person) at any homestay: raw slices, ham fried with vegetables, and ham bone soup. Also try the cold rice noodles (凉米线) for breakfast at ¥10.

Yuhu : Naxi cold cuts platter with wild mushroom hotpot ¥60-100, yak yogurt from Wenhai Lake herders ¥15. The village has three family restaurants by the main gate.

Xizhou : The ¥8-12 Xizhou baba pancake (plum and rose varieties) at the Sifangjie market is a must. Also Erhai Lake silver fish stir-fry ¥55-75 at lakeside restaurants.

Baisha : Naxi baba bread with honey and yak butter ¥12-18, yak sha cool noodles ¥15, and ¥30-50 village breakfast sets of rice wine-soup and pickled vegetables.

Shigu : Yak-butter tea ¥15 at the riverside teahouses, simple ¥25-35 home-style stir-fries at the three guesthouses.

Dukezong (Shangri-La) : Yak meat hotpot ¥80-120 per person, Tibetan tsampa (roasted barley flour) porridge ¥15-20, and highland mushroom noodle soup ¥25.

Weather, altitude, and when to go
North Yunnan weather is controlled by altitude and the Southeast Asian monsoon.
Dali plain (Xizhou, Dali, 2000 m) : Mild year-round, 15-25°C in day, 5-15°C at night. Avoid the July-August rainy peak when paths turn to mud.

Lijiang and Baisha (2400 m) : Daytime 18-25°C spring to autumn, down to -5°C in January nights. Snow on Jade Dragon Snow Mountain is best viewed November-February, though the village stays walkable.

Shaxi valley (2100 m) : Similar to Dali but less tourist-dense. Friday market runs year round.

Shigu (1800 m) : Warmest in the loop — 20-28°C most of the year — because it sits deep in the Yangtze canyon.

Dukezong / Shangri-La (3200 m) : Sharp seasons. June-September is the grassland bloom with temps 10-20°C; October-April is cold and dry with frequent -10°C nights. Roads to Potatso close after heavy snow.

Nuodeng (1800-2200 m) : Salt-well village gets uncomfortably humid in summer monsoon. October through April is much better.

For first-time visitors, the ideal windows are April-May and September-October — mild, clear, and after or before the monsoon.
A 7-day North Yunnan slow-travel loop
Day 1 — Fly into Dali (DLU) or high-speed train Kunming → Dali; overnight Xizhou.

Day 2 — Xizhou morning market, Erhai Lake bike ride, evening in Dali Old Town.

Day 3 — Shared van Dali → Shaxi; afternoon in Sideng Square.

Day 4 — Shaxi Friday market (if timing works), transfer Shaxi → Lijiang.

Day 5 — Baisha and Yuhu half-day each; sleep in Baisha.

Day 6 — Train Lijiang → Shangri-La; overnight Dukezong.

Day 7 — Songzanlin Monastery and flight or train back to Kunming/Lijiang.

Respectful travel rules
Ask before photographing elders, ceremonies, and temple altars. Some Bai and Naxi funerals and Tibetan ceremonies do not welcome cameras.

Walk clockwise around prayer wheels, stupas, and monastery halls in Shangri-La.

Don't touch monks' robes or step on thresholds in Tibetan and Naxi homes.

Buy directly from the villages — Nuodeng ham, Shaxi woodcarving, Baisha embroidery — rather than from airport duty-free versions.

Carry trash out. Many small villages have no public bins.

Use Amap (高德地图) rather than Google Maps, which is unreliable offline in the mountains.

Booking homestays as a foreigner
Three workable paths in 2026:
Trip.com (English) — Lists most Shaxi, Xizhou, and Baisha courtyards. Smaller Yuhu and Nuodeng family stays rarely show up. Foreign cards accepted.

Xiaohongshu (小红书) — Search for the village name in Chinese (e.g., 沙溪古镇) and message homestay owners directly. Owners reply on WeChat. Best selection, worst English support.

Direct WeChat / phone from the homestay's Baidu listing — Call ahead in Mandarin or via an AI translation app like Doubao. Owners in Nuodeng and Shigu often hold rooms without deposit for foreign travelers who commit by voice.

Expect to register your passport on arrival — the homestay will photograph it and upload to the local police portal. This is normal and required by law.
How do I get from Kunming to Shaxi Old Town?
The fastest route is the high-speed train Kunming South → Dali (¥145-220, 2 hours) on 12306 or Trip.com, then a 2-hour shared van from Dali bus station to Shaxi at ¥55-80. Most Shaxi homestays will book the van for you the day before. Direct private car transfers from Dali cost ¥300-450.
Is Nuodeng ham worth the detour from Dali?
Yes if you like food travel. Nuodeng ham (诺邓火腿) is air-cured for 3+ years in the local salt-brine climate and the village homestays serve it in a ¥60-90 family-style dinner with wild mushrooms and Yunnan vegetables. The village itself — a hillside of mud-brick Bai courtyards — is arguably the more interesting reason to make the 3.5-hour trip from Dali.
What is the difference between Baisha and Lijiang Old Town?
Lijiang Old Town (Dayan) is a UNESCO site that now sees 20+ million visitors a year and is packed with souvenir and bar streets. Baisha, 8 km north, was the Naxi capital before Lijiang was built and remains a quiet farming town with the original Ming-era murals in its main hall. Locals still live and farm there, and evening streets empty by 8 pm.
Can foreigners use Didi and Amap in rural North Yunnan?
Didi works in Dali, Lijiang, and Shangri-La city limits but rarely in small villages like Nuodeng, Shaxi, or Shigu — homestays will call drivers directly. Amap (高德地图) is the map app locals use; download the offline map for Yunnan before you leave cell coverage. Google Maps has inaccurate road data in most of rural Yunnan.
What altitude is Shangri-La and should I worry about altitude sickness?
Shangri-La (Zhongdian, home of Dukezong Old Town) sits at 3200 m. Mild altitude symptoms — headache, shortness of breath, poor sleep — are common in the first 24 hours. Drink 2 liters of water daily, skip alcohol on arrival day, and walk slowly on the stairs to the prayer wheel. Potatso National Park tops out at 3800 m and is where most visitors feel altitude most strongly.
