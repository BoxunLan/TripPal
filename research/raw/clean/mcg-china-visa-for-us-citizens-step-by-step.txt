Do US Citizens Need a Visa for China in 2026?
Short answer: yes, for almost every kind of trip.
This trips up a lot of Americans right now, because China has been aggressively expanding visa-free entry. Since 2024, citizens of 40+ countries (most of Europe, Australia, New Zealand, Japan, South Korea, several Southeast Asian and Latin American countries) can enter China visa-free for up to 30 days. Canada was added on February 17, 2026. US passport holders are not on that list. The US embassy in China says it plainly: travel to China from the United States requires a visa.
I know. Every travel influencer is shouting about "visa-free China" right now. They're almost always talking about European or Australian passports. If you hold a US passport, assume you need a visa unless one specific exception applies.
The one real exception for US citizens: 240-hour visa-free transit.
If you're flying from the US to a third country or region and want to stop in mainland China for up to 10 days along the way, you qualify for 240-hour visa-free transit at 65 ports. Hong Kong, Macau, Japan, South Korea, Thailand, anywhere that isn't the US all count as a third country. This is a legitimate way to visit China without a visa, but it has strict rules: onward ticket required, designated ports only, cross-province travel limited to certain areas. See our 240-hour transit guide for the full rules.
You need a full Chinese visa if:
You're flying round-trip from the US (no third-country onward ticket)

Your stay is longer than 10 days

You're going to work, study, or do journalism

You want a 10-year multiple-entry visa for long-term flexibility

You're a dual-national US/PRC

You have a criminal record or prior overstay

Pro tip : Pack a printout of whichever policy applies to you. If airline check-in staff question whether you need a visa, show them the printout.

The 2 China Visa Options for Americans
Before you start filling out forms, pick the right path. There are only 2 that matter for US citizens.
Option 1: The 10-Year L Tourist Visa ($140)
This is what "china visa for us citizens" usually means. One application, ten years of unlimited visits. Stay up to 60 days each time.
Cost : $140 (¥1,015) at consulate, or ~$217 (¥1,575) through CVASC

Validity : 10 years from issue date

Stay per visit : Usually 60 days (sometimes 30 or 90)

Entries : Unlimited during 10-year validity

Good for : Round-trip US–China travel, repeat visits, trips longer than 10 days

Most first-time US applicants for a china tourist visa get the 10-year by default. You don't check a "give me 10 years" box. The consulate decides based on your profile. More on improving your odds below.
Option 2: 240-Hour Visa-Free Transit (Free)
If you're flying from the US to a third country and want to stop in mainland China for up to 10 days, you qualify for visa-free transit at 65 ports. Key rule: you need an onward ticket to a third country or region (Hong Kong, Macau, Japan, etc.), not a round-trip back to the US. The rest of this guide focuses on Option 1: the actual china visa application, which is where most of the confusion lives.

The photo size is the #1 trip-up. US passport photos are 2x2 inches (51x51mm). China wants 48x33mm: rectangular, not square. About 20% of applications get bounced for this. Walgreens and CVS offer a "Chinese visa photo" option specifically. Ask for it by name. Costs $15-20.

Step 1: Fill Out the COVA Online Application
The COVA system (China Online Visa Application) launched September 30, 2025 at cova.mfa.gov.cn . It replaced the old paper forms. You must use it. No paper-only option anymore.
Budget 60-90 minutes the first time.
1. Register an account. Email + password. They send a verification code.
2. Start a new application. Pick "Ordinary Visa" → "L (Tourism)" → Multiple Entry, 10 years. Worst case they downgrade you. They'll never upgrade a single-entry request.
3. Personal info. Full name exactly as on passport, punctuation included. DOB, nationality, gender, marital status.
4. Passport details. Number, issue date, expiry, issuing city. Double-check every digit.
5. Travel details. Purpose = tourism. Cities planned. Arrival date. Length of stay. You don't need actual flight bookings. "Beijing, Shanghai, 14 days starting June 2026" is fine.
6. Employer and contact. Current employer name, phone, address. Self-employed? Write "Self-employed" and your business name. Retired? Write "Retired" and leave employer fields as N/A.
7. Contacts. One US-based emergency contact (not traveling with you). One contact in China: your hotel address works. No reservation required.
8. Prior travel history. Last 5 years of international trips. Be honest. They cross-check against passport stamps.
9. Upload documents. Passport bio page, photo (48x33mm JPG), proof of residence. Each under 1MB.
10. Submit. You'll get a confirmation code. Save it.
11. Wait for preliminary review. Status moves from "Submitted" to "Under Review" to "Passport to be Submitted." Takes 3-5 business days . Email notification when done.
12. Print the confirmation page. Log back in, download the Visa Application Statement PDF, print on plain paper, sign by hand. This is your ticket to in-person submission.
Heads up: COVA times out after 30 minutes of inactivity and loses unsaved progress. Save drafts every few sections.

Step 3: China Visa Application Fees and Payment
$140 flat. That's the consular fee for US citizens: same price for single entry, double entry, or the 10-year multiple entry. No upgrade fee.
The $140 is actually a reduced rate. Standard is $185. China has been extending the $140 rate since 2023, and it's currently valid through December 31, 2026 .

Common Rejection Reasons (And How to Avoid Each)
Approval rate for complete US applications runs around 96% . But that 4% rejection stings: you lose $140 and have to reapply. Ranked by frequency:
1. Inconsistent or incomplete forms (35%). Name punctuation must match your passport exactly. "JOHN R SMITH" on the passport means "JOHN R SMITH" on the form, not "John Robert Smith." Every field filled. "N/A" is better than blank.
2. Wrong photo dimensions (20%). Must be exactly 48mm x 33mm, white background, taken within 6 months. Walgreens, CVS, and FedEx offer "China visa photo" as a specific service. Ask by name.
3. Mentioning sensitive regions without permits (15%). Tibet, Xinjiang, and some border areas need special permits beyond a tourist visa. Don't list Lhasa as a destination unless you've arranged a permit through a licensed Tibet agency.
4. Criminal record or prior deportation (12%). Be honest. China cross-checks with Interpol. A minor old offense usually doesn't block you. An undisclosed one will.
5. Prior visa violations (10%). If you've overstayed before (even by a day), expect scrutiny. The fine is ¥500/day ($70/day) up to ¥10,000 ($1,400). Pay it before reapplying.
6. Sensitive profession (8%). Journalists, active military, and government officials face extra review. Don't lie about employment. An active CNN correspondent needs a J visa, not L.
7. Missing handwritten signatures. Every page needing a signature needs a pen signature. Digital rejected.
If you get rejected, the consulate usually tells you why. Fix the issue, file a fresh COVA, resubmit. You pay another $140. The first fee is gone.
The 10-Year Multi-Entry Visa: How Americans Get It
The question I get constantly: "How do I make sure I get the 10-year one?"
You don't explicitly request it. You check "multiple entries, 10 years" on COVA. The consulate decides based on your profile. Most US citizens with clean applications get the full 10-year multi-entry by default, even first-timers. The US-China reciprocity agreement defaults first-time tourist applicants from both countries to the 10-year version.
Improves your odds: clean passport (no prior violations), stable employment (2+ years), previous international travel history, US driver's license, clear tourism purpose.
May downgrade you: first international trip ever, recent criminal record, unexplained unemployment, vague destination answers, passport expiring in under 18 months (visa will match passport validity).
Important : When your passport expires, the 10-year visa is NOT automatically transferred. Carry both passports together to use remaining validity. Or reapply in the new passport, which most people do.
What to Check on Your Visa Sticker the Day You Get It Back
When you pick up your passport, don't shove it in your pocket and walk out. Open it, find the visa page, and check these details before leaving the consulate . Errors get fixed free at pickup. After you leave, you pay for a new application.
Check in this order:
Your name (exactly as on passport, punctuation included)

Passport number (digit for digit)

Visa type (should say "L" for tourist)

Number of entries ("M" for multiple)

Validity period (10 years from issue date, capped by passport expiry)

Duration of each stay (usually 60 days)

Issue date and place

Visa sticker number (record separately)

Find an error? Walk back to the counter immediately. They'll fix it, usually same day.
One more step before you leave: photograph the visa page with your phone right there at the consulate . If anything goes wrong with your passport on the trip, you have a record.
FAQ: China Visa Application for US Citizens
Do I need a visa for China from US in 2026?
Yes. US citizens are not on China's unilateral visa-free list. You need a full Chinese visa (almost always the 10-year L tourist visa, $140) for any round-trip visit from the US. The one exception is 240-hour visa-free transit: if you're stopping in mainland China for up to 10 days while flying to a third country or region, you can skip the visa. Everything else runs through a china visa application on COVA.
How much does a China visa cost for US citizens?
$140 (¥1,015) flat at the consulate for any visa type: single, double, or 10-year multi-entry. Through CVASC, total runs around $217-252 with service fees. Express processing adds $25. The $140 rate is a reduced fee valid through December 31, 2026.
How long does a chinese visa application take?
Realistic total: 1-3 weeks from starting COVA to picking up your visa. Breakdown: 3-5 days for online preliminary review, 4 business days standard consular processing (2-3 for express), plus appointment and travel time. Apply at least 4 weeks before you fly.
Can I apply for a China visa by mail?
Not directly at a consulate. You must submit in person. Some CVASC locations accept mail-in through authorized services, adding $15-25 and longer turnaround.
Do I need fingerprints for an L tourist visa?
No. China waived fingerprinting for L, M, Q2, X2, and G visas through December 31, 2026 . Long-term visas (Z, X1, Q1) still require them.
Can I work on a China tourist visa?
No. L visas are strictly for tourism and family visits. Working on a tourist visa is a deportable offense. If you need to work in China, you need a Z work visa (sponsored by a Chinese employer). Totally different process.
Which travelers get 30-day visa-free entry to China?
Citizens of 40+ countries currently on China's unilateral visa-free list (most of Europe, Australia, New Zealand, Japan, South Korea, several Southeast Asian and Latin American countries) can enter for up to 30 days without a visa. Canada was added on February 17, 2026. US passport holders are not on this list. Americans must use either the 10-year L visa or the 240-hour transit option instead.
Does a 10-year China visa mean I can live in China for 10 years?
No. The 10 years is your window to enter. Each individual stay is limited to around 60 days. Overstay costs ¥500/day ($70/day). For longer stays, you need a different visa class.
What if my passport expires before the 10 years are up?
Your China visa stays valid in the expired passport. Carry both passports together at the border: old one with the visa, new one for your current identity. Many people just reapply in the new passport for convenience.
