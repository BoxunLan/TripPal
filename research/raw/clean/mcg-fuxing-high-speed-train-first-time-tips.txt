The Fuxing (复兴号, pronounced fu xing hao) is the train that makes China's high-speed rail network famous. At a sustained 350 km/h top speed, a single Fuxing G train covers Beijing to Shanghai — a distance roughly equal to London to Milan — in 4 hours and 28 minutes. It runs on more than 40 major corridors in 2026, has replaced most of the older Hexie Hao (CRH2) fleet on the premier routes, and carries more high-speed passengers than any single train family on earth.
If you are taking your first Fuxing ride as a foreign visitor, the technical train itself is the easiest part. The boarding process, the seat classes, and the 12306 booking window are what typically trip travelers up. Here is exactly what to expect from the train, the ticket, and the station.

What is a Fuxing train, exactly?
Fuxing means "rejuvenation" in Chinese, and the name launched in 2017 as a brand replacing the older Hexie Hao ("harmony") fleet. Fuxing trains are the CR400AF (built by CRRC Qingdao Sifang) and CR400BF (built by CRRC Tangshan), both designed entirely in China to a shared specification rather than licensed from German, Japanese, or French partners. In practice, what matters for riders is this:
Top operating speed : 350 km/h, sustained, not just momentary

Seat pitch : slightly larger than older CRH2 and CRH3 fleets

Ride smoothness : noticeably better than the older trains at 300+ km/h

WiFi : installed on most new-build sets (CR400AF-Z and later generations)

Power outlets : universal at every seat in all classes including second class

Boarding door count : more doors per carriage than older trains

On the prestige corridors (Beijing-Shanghai, Beijing-Guangzhou, Shanghai-Kunming, Beijing-Chongqing) Fuxing is now the default. On secondary corridors (most east-west provincial lines) you might still get an older Hexie Hao CRH380, which is slower but functionally similar from a passenger experience standpoint.
The three seat classes and what they actually look like

Second class (二等座, er deng zuo)
The workhorse class. Every Fuxing has dozens of second class carriages and this is how most Chinese travelers actually ride. Seats are arranged 3+2 across the carriage, with armrests, a tray table, a footrest, universal 220V outlets at every seat, reading lights, and a reclining back. On a 4-hour Beijing-Shanghai trip second class is perfectly comfortable for an adult of average height. Taller travelers (over 6'2") will find the legroom tight but bearable. Second class is air-conditioned, clean, and has a dedicated luggage area at each carriage end for larger bags.
First class (一等座, yi deng zuo)
First class costs roughly 1.7 times second class. You get a 2+2 layout, significantly larger seat pitch, a deeper recline, and more aisle space. Carriages are quieter because there are fewer passengers per square meter, and the seats rotate manually so groups of 4 can face each other. First class does not include food, but the on-train service cart does make stops. First class is worth it on longer journeys (over 5 hours) or on busy holiday periods when second class carriages are packed.
Business class (商务座, shang wu zuo)
Business class on a Fuxing is a genuine lie-flat seat. The 2+1 layout gives each passenger significantly more space, the seat reclines to 180 degrees (true flat bed), has a personal screen on newer sets, and comes with a free meal, bottled water, a blanket, and sometimes slippers. At ¥1,748 for Beijing-Shanghai it is more expensive than a one-way economy flight on the same route, and three to four times the price of second class. Worth it on overnight-length routes, or on long daytime trips where you want to actually sleep.

Booking a Fuxing train as a foreigner
Since 2019, China Railway has allowed passport-based booking through the official 12306 app in English, and since 2022 the Trip.com and Ctrip international platforms have sold tickets reliably for most G trains. The booking window opens 15 days before departure for 12306 and 14 days for Trip.com.
Step by step for a first-time foreign booking:
Install the 12306 app or use Trip.com — both work on iOS and Android with English interfaces

Enter your passport details exactly as printed — hyphens, middle names, and punctuation all matter

Search by station names in English — "Beijing South" not "Beijing," "Shanghai Hongqiao" not "Shanghai Station"

Pick your train — G-prefixed trains are Fuxing or Hexie Hao high-speed; D-prefixed are slower regional EMU; K-prefixed are old-style expresses

Select seat class — second class fills first on popular routes

Pay with Alipay, WeChat Pay, or in some cases Visa/Mastercard — Trip.com accepts foreign credit cards reliably; 12306 is more restrictive

Receive a confirmation with a PNR code — bring your passport to the station, no paper ticket needed in 2026

You do not need to print a ticket. Since 2020, almost all Fuxing trains use passport-gate automated entry for foreigners, which means you scan your passport at the gate and your seat assignment is pulled from the reservation system. Some smaller stations still have manual passport checks at a dedicated foreign-passenger window, usually marked with an English sign.
Boarding timings and the rookie mistake
The single biggest mistake first-time riders make is showing up too late. Chinese high-speed rail boarding works nothing like a European or American train:
Gates open 15-30 minutes before departure

Gates close 5 minutes before departure, exactly

Once the gate closes, you do not board, period — no exceptions, no running onto the platform

Large stations like Beijing South, Shanghai Hongqiao, Guangzhou South, and Chengdu East are enormous. Security screening, ticket check, and the walk to your platform can take 15-25 minutes. Plan to arrive at the station entrance at least 45 minutes before departure, 60 minutes if you are not familiar with the station layout.
The actual arrival process looks like this:
Walk into the station and immediately go through security (X-ray plus metal detector)

Look up at the departure board for your train number and waiting zone (候车厅 hou che ting) or platform number

Walk to the correct waiting zone, which usually has a gate labeled with the train number

When the gate opens (marked by staff and an electronic sign), queue up with your passport

Scan your passport at the automated gate (foreigners) or hand it to a staff member at manual gates

Descend the escalator to the platform

Find your carriage number painted on the platform floor; stand at that spot

Board when the train stops and doors open, typically 3-5 minutes before departure

Do not leave the waiting zone before your gate opens. Do not go shopping in the main station hall if your train is less than 30 minutes away.
Onboard amenities and what to expect
Fuxing trains are noticeably nicer than the older Hexie Hao fleet, and significantly nicer than Western travelers typically expect.
Power outlets at every seat in every class (Type A and Type C sockets, 220V)

WiFi available on most new-build CR400AF-Z sets, free with a Chinese phone number or with an English workaround on some trains

Large windows with electric blinds that passengers can adjust

Dining cart selling instant noodles, hot meals, snacks, tea, and coffee at moderate prices

Hot water dispensers at the end of every carriage for making instant noodles or tea

Clean toilets including both squat and western-style; toilet paper is usually provided

Overhead luggage shelves plus dedicated large luggage areas at carriage ends

English announcements before every station stop

Dining cart service is a cultural experience worth trying. Instant noodles cost ¥15-25, hot meals ¥40-65, coffee ¥25-35. The quality is fine but most Chinese passengers bring their own food or buy from the station before boarding, which is cheaper.
Routes where Fuxing actually runs in 2026
Not every G train in China is a Fuxing. The premium corridors have fully transitioned to Fuxing rolling stock, but some secondary routes still run CR380 Hexie Hao fleets at lower 300 km/h speeds. The confirmed 2026 Fuxing corridors include:
Beijing-Shanghai (G1-G38 series)

Beijing-Shenzhen/Hong Kong (G79-G85 series)

Beijing-Harbin (G1201 onward)

Beijing-Xi'an and onward to Chengdu and Chongqing

Shanghai-Kunming (cross-country G-series launched 2021)

Shanghai-Wuhan-Chongqing

Beijing-Taiyuan-Xi'an corridor

Zhengzhou-Wuhan-Guangzhou main line

Fuxing sleeper service Beijing-Hong Kong G75/G76

If you want a guaranteed Fuxing experience, pick a Beijing-Shanghai G1, G7, G13, or G17 train. These are the flagship services and always use the newest CR400AF-Z sets.
Rookie mistakes to avoid on your first trip
The first-time rider traps that keep repeating in traveler forums:
Arriving 10 minutes before departure — you will miss the train, the gate closes 5 minutes early

Showing up without your passport — there is no workaround; passport is mandatory at every gate

Booking "Shanghai" instead of "Shanghai Hongqiao" — Shanghai has three major rail stations and they are not close to each other

Bringing liquids over 100 ml — security screens the same as airport security, liquids get confiscated

Packing lithium batteries loose in a checked bag — power banks over 100 Wh are banned; 100-160 Wh need airline-style approval

Assuming second class is crowded and bad — it is not; most seasoned travelers ride second class

Eating only at the dining cart — stock up on food at the station before boarding; selection is better and cheaper

Ignoring carriage number on the platform — the train is 400+ meters long and stops for 2-3 minutes; run to the wrong end and you are out of luck

FAQ
What is the difference between Fuxing and Hexie Hao?
Fuxing (复兴号, CR400 series) is the newer Chinese-designed generation of high-speed trains with a 350 km/h top speed, universal power outlets, and slightly better ride comfort. Hexie Hao (和谐号, CRH series) is the older generation launched from 2007 using licensed German, Japanese, and French technology, running at 300 km/h. Both are comfortable; Fuxing is faster and has newer amenities. On G-series services across flagship corridors, Fuxing is the default in 2026.
How far in advance can I book a Fuxing train as a foreigner?
The booking window opens 15 days before departure on 12306 and most travel platforms. Popular routes like Beijing-Shanghai and Beijing-Guangzhou fill up 3-7 days in advance during peak periods (summer, Golden Week, Spring Festival). For off-peak weekdays you can usually book same-day or day-of-travel without issue.
Is second class comfortable enough for a Beijing to Shanghai trip?
Yes. Most Chinese travelers ride second class, and at 4.5 hours the Beijing-Shanghai trip is short enough to be comfortable for adults of average height. Second class has power outlets, reading lights, a reclining seat, and a dedicated luggage area. First class is a modest upgrade mainly valuable on trips over 5 hours; business class is a lie-flat seat worth it only for long routes or special occasions.
Do Fuxing trains have WiFi and charging outlets?
All Fuxing trains have universal 220V power outlets at every seat in every class. WiFi is available on newer CR400AF-Z and CR400BF-Z sets, including most trains on the Beijing-Shanghai, Beijing-Guangzhou, and Shanghai-Kunming corridors. Free WiFi typically requires a Chinese phone number for registration; some trains now support passport-based login as a workaround.
How early should I arrive for a Fuxing train as a first-time rider?
Arrive at the station entrance at least 45 minutes before departure, or 60 minutes for large stations like Beijing South, Shanghai Hongqiao, and Guangzhou South. You need time for security screening, passport verification, walking to the correct waiting zone, and lining up at the gate. Gates close 5 minutes before departure, and there are no exceptions.
Before the platform, two things decide how the day goes. Booking on 12306 with a foreign passport avoids the OTA fee and gives you the seat you want if you book when tickets open fifteen days out — which costs nothing, because cancelling more than eight days ahead is free . And what you can bring aboard matters more on a Fuxing than people expect, because the racks fill early.
