If this is your first china trip, you need 12-14 days, roughly $3,200-4,800 per person all-in, and the Beijing to Xi'an to Shanghai Golden Triangle as your spine. Add Chengdu for pandas and Guilin for karst mountains, and you have the classic first-timer china trip 9 out of 10 of my clients book. China is 9.6 million square kilometers. You will not see it in a week, and anyone telling you otherwise is selling a bus tour nobody wants to remember.

Is Now Actually a Good Time for a China Trip?
Yes. 2026 is the easiest year to plan a china trip since before the pandemic. Four reasons:
Visa-free for 50 countries. As of February 15, 2026 , the visa-free list covers 50 nationalities for stays up to 30 days , including Germany, France, Japan, Australia, and (finally) the UK and Canada . It runs through December 31, 2026 per the China National Immigration Administration . Americans are not on this list but have two fallbacks: a standard L-visa (~$140, 4-5 business days) or the 240- hour visa -free transit across 65 ports in 24 provinces.
US travel advisory at Level 2. The US State Department downgraded China from Level 3 to Level 2 in early 2026, the same level as France or Italy.
Foreign cards work on Alipay and WeChat Pay. Since 2024, both apps let you link Visa, Mastercard, Discover, and JCB for payments up to ¥8,000 ($1,120) per transaction.
The numbers confirm it. International arrivals hit 150 million+ in 2025, up 17% year on year. Foreign tourism spending jumped to $131.1 billion (+39.2%) . American spending grew 50% . Traveling to china is a normal thing again.

How Long Do You Need for a China Trip?
The honest answer most clients never want to hear: China is bigger than you think. 9.6 million km² , slightly larger than the continental US. Shanghai to Beijing is New York to Atlanta. You cannot hop five cities in a week and call it a vacation in china, you will call it a travel day with snacks.
What works at each length:
7 days : Pick one base. Beijing-only, or Shanghai plus Suzhou and Hangzhou as a mini-loop. Better to see one city right than three cities badly.
10 days : The Golden Triangle. Beijing → Xi'an → Shanghai. The most-booked first-time china trip route, and for good reason: 3,000 years of history with energy left to fly home.
14 days : Grand Classic. Golden Triangle plus Chengdu and Guilin. My top recommendation for 9 out of 10 first-timers. History, nature, pandas, the Bund. Not rushed.
21 days : Deep dive. Grand Classic plus Zhangjiajie (the Avatar mountains), Hong Kong, or a Yunnan extension. Only if you have a high tolerance for travel fatigue, clients tend to burn out around day 16.
One rule nobody tells you: subtract one city from whatever you are tempted to book. Priya from Austin tried Beijing → Xi'an → Chengdu → Shanghai → Guilin → Hong Kong in 11 days. By day 8 she was crying in a Chengdu HSR waiting room telling me she did not come to China to see train stations. We cut Hong Kong. She finished the trip happy.
The 3 Classic Routes for a First China Trip
Most people asking where to travel to china land on one of three routes. I have booked all three many times.
Route 1: The Golden Triangle (10 Days)
Cities : Beijing (3) → Xi'an (2) → Shanghai (3) HSR : Beijing-Xi'an 4h30m, Xi'an-Shanghai 6h Best for : Limited vacation days, history-first travelers Skip if : You care about nature or pandas Mid-range budget : ~$2,500-3,500 per person all-in
Forbidden City, Great Wall at Mutianyu, Terracotta Warriors, Xi'an Muslim Quarter, the Bund, Yuyuan Garden, and a Huangpu river cruise. It is the trailer for China, tight and well-paced.
Route 2: The Grand Classic (14 Days)
Cities : Beijing (4) → Xi'an (2) → Chengdu (2) → Guilin/Yangshuo (3) → Shanghai (3) Transport : HSR for 3 legs, one 2-hour flight to Guilin Best for : Variety, couples, photo-driven travelers Skip if : You hate changing hotels every 3 days Mid-range budget : ~$3,200-4,800 per person all-in
My top pick. Capital, dynasty capital, panda capital, karst landscape that looks painted, modern skyline. Four very different Chinas in two weeks.
Route 3: The Western Adventure (14-21 Days)
Cities : Shanghai → Chengdu → Lijiang → Dali → Shangri-La → Xi'an Transport : 2-3 flights, some long bus transfers Best for : Repeat travelers, slow travelers, nature-first Skip if : This is your first china trip Mid-range budget : ~$3,800-5,500 per person all-in
This is where I push clients on their second trip. Yunnan minorities, Himalayan foothills, Silk Road echoes. Not where you start.

When to Go: Best Months, Worst Months, Festivals
Timing is where people ruin an otherwise good china travel plan. Quick guide:
Best months :
April-May : Spring, 15-25°C nationwide, cherry blossoms, manageable crowds. My personal favorite.

September to early October : Crisp blue skies, the famous Beijing autumn. Avoid October 1-7 (Golden Week, 800 million domestic travelers).

Late October to early November : The shoulder sweet spot. Fewer crowds, lower hotel prices.

Worst months :
Lunar New Year (Feb 17 to Mar 3 in 2026) : Roughly 3 billion domestic trips in a 40-day crush. Transport booked out, prices spiked, many small businesses closed.

July-August : Brutal heat and humidity (35°C+/95°F+), school holidays, summer thunderstorms. Peak hotel prices.

Golden Week (October 1-7) : Forbidden City hits 80,000+ visitors per day . Do not do this to yourself.

[ ] Alipay downloaded, foreign Visa/Mastercard linked, our credit card guide walks through every step

[ ] WeChat Pay set up as backup

[ ] VPN installed BEFORE you land, you cannot download one in China once you arrive

[ ] eSIM activated, our eSIM comparison covers which ones actually work

[ ] Offline maps cached, Apple Maps works, Google Maps does not. See our China map app guide

Bookings (3-4 weeks out) :
[ ] Hotels booked via Trip.com (flags foreigner-friendly) or Booking.com

[ ] HSR tickets booked on 12306.cn or Trip.com, see our high-speed rail guide

[ ] Forbidden City, Terracotta Warriors, and Panda Base tickets pre-booked, they sell out

Final week :
[ ] Screenshot every hotel name and address in Chinese , taxi drivers do not read Pinyin

[ ] ¥1,000-2,000 ($140-280) cash from an ICBC or Bank of China airport ATM

[ ] Medication plus prescription copies in original packaging

[ ] Power adapter (China uses Type A, C, and I plugs at 220V)

Tom from Denver skipped step 1. He assumed the 240-hour transit exemption covered a direct round trip to Beijing. It does not, you need a confirmed onward ticket to a third country. He was turned away at the gate in LAX. Three hours of tears and a rebooking fee he is still bitter about. Do not be Tom.
First-Timer Mistakes That Will Wreck Your China Trip
Same mistakes, on repeat, for years:
1. Trying to see all of China. The number one mistake. Pick 3-5 cities for your first china trip.
2. Not setting up payment apps before leaving. You land at PVG jet-lagged, want water, the vending machine only takes Alipay, and the Alipay signup needs an SMS code. I have watched this movie many times.
3. Forgetting a VPN. Google Maps, Gmail, Instagram, WhatsApp, YouTube, X, all blocked. Install one at home, you cannot download it once you land.
4. Booking the wrong train station. Shanghai has three. Suzhou has two. Beijing has six. Double-check your ticket against your hotel's location.
5. Booking flights into tiny airports to save $60. Saving $60 on a flight to Tianjin when you wanted Beijing costs you a three-hour transfer. Almost never worth it.
6. Ignoring Lunar New Year and Golden Week. Land during either and your trip is a different trip. Prices triple. Trains disappear.
7. Assuming English everywhere. Tier-1 hotels, fine. Xi'an taxi at 10pm, not a word. Screenshot hotel names in Chinese.
8. Packing wrong for the climate. Beijing in October is 8°C at night. Guilin is 24°C and humid. If your china travel plan crosses regions, pack layers.
9. Not downloading Trip.com. The single best app for China bookings, hotels, HSR, flights, attractions.
10. Skipping travel insurance. A hospital visit is cheap by US standards. An emergency medical evacuation is not.

FAQ
How much does a china trip cost for 2 weeks?
A mid-range 14-day china trip runs $3,200-4,800 per person all-in, including an international flight from the US. Backpackers can do it for $2,200-3,000. Upscale travelers should budget $6,500-9,000.
Do I need a visa for China in 2026?
Depends on your passport. 50 countries (including Germany, France, UK, Canada, Japan, Australia) are visa-free for 30 days through December 31, 2026. Americans need either a standard L-visa (~$140, 4-5 days) or the 240-hour visa-free transit with a confirmed onward ticket.
When is the cheapest time for a china trip?
Mid-November through mid-March , excluding Lunar New Year. Hotels drop 30-50%, flights are cheaper, attractions are nearly empty. Bring winter layers, Beijing hits -10°C (14°F) some January nights.
Is China safe for solo female travelers?
Yes, very. Violent crime against foreigners is extremely rare, and the US State Department now rates China at Level 2 . The real risks are scams, pickpocketing at tourist sites, and traffic.
Can I plan a china trip without a tour group?
Absolutely, and most of my clients do. Between Trip.com, HSR booking, tier-1 hotels with English staff, and translation apps, independent traveling to china is easier than ever.
Is traveling to china hard for first-timers?
Traveling to china takes more prep than Europe, you need payment apps, a VPN, an eSIM, and offline maps. Do the prep and the trip itself is smooth. Most first-timers come back saying China was easier than they expected and harder to leave.
Start Your China Trip Plan the Right Way
A good china travel plan comes down to three things: a route that fits your time, an honest budget, and the prep done before you fly. Nail those three, and the rest of your china trip is walking around eating.
One last thing. Every client who comes back from their first china trip says the same sentence: "I wish I had stayed longer." Build in two extra days if you can.
