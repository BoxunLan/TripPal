Three nights buys you two full days, one arrival evening, and a day trip. That is the whole budget, and the fastest way to waste a chunk of it is to change hotels halfway through.
So this is a sequence of decisions rather than a list of sights. Each one closes off options, which is the point — Hong Kong punishes people who keep renegotiating the plan at breakfast.
Decision one: which side of the harbour you sleep on
Pick a base and commute. The MTR makes the other side eight minutes away, so the choice is about what you want outside the door at 11pm.

For a first visit on three nights, Tsim Sha Tsui wins on value and on the view. Choosing a hotel in TST goes street by street. Trip.com tends to run 5–15% under Booking.com on Hong Kong rates.

Arrival night
From HKIA the Airport Express reaches Kowloon Station in 22 minutes for HK$115, or Hong Kong Station in 24 for HK$120. Landing after 11pm, the N11 bus to Central is HK$33 and runs hourly until 5am. A metered red taxi to TST is HK$300–360 including the HK$30 tunnel toll.
Get an Octopus card before you do anything else — see the payment section below, because there are two versions and the wrong one costs you a deposit.
Then walk the harbourfront. That is the entire plan for night one; you have been on a plane. Eat close: Mak's Noodle on Hankow Road does wonton noodles for HK$65, or take the MTR to Sham Shui Po for tofu pudding at Kung Wo Dou Bun Chong, HK$18.
Day 2: the Peak, and the queue you don't have to stand in
The Peak Tram queue at Garden Road runs 60 to 120 minutes on any clear weekend morning, and buying the ticket is the slow part, not the ride. In summer or on a public holiday it is worse.
Take bus 15 from the Exchange Square terminus in Central instead. HK$12.10 with Octopus, and it climbs the same hill on a road with better windows than the tram has. Ride the tram down later in the day if you want it, when the queue has gone.
If you want the Sky Terrace 428 platform, buy the combined ticket in advance — around HK$138 online against HK$148–175 at the door.
Down from the Peak, the afternoon writes itself: dim sum at Lin Heung Kui in Sheung Wan, the last of the push-cart halls where nobody speaks English and you point at what rolls past. Then Aberdeen Street up past PMQ, across to Man Mo Temple on Hollywood Road.
Finish on the Star Ferry from Central Pier 7 — HK$5 on weekdays, HK$6.50 at weekends, crossing since 1888. Board around 6:30pm and the lights come on while you are on the water.

Temple Street is the one to set expectations on. After the 2023 government revamp it reopened as an international-style night market — cleaner, brighter, and no longer the gritty bazaar the guidebooks still describe. The dai pai dong clustered around it are still worth the trip; the stalls are not.
If you are coming from mainland China, read this part
The Alipay and WeChat Pay you set up for the mainland are not the apps Hong Kong runs. AlipayHK and WeChat Pay HK are separate products with separate accounts. Your mainland wallet will fail at a Hong Kong till and it will not tell you why.
Octopus is still the thing that works everywhere, and there are two versions:
Tourist Octopus — from about HK$39, no refundable deposit, keep it as a souvenir.

Standard Octopus — HK$150 including HK$100 of stored value, with a HK$50 deposit you reclaim on the way out.

Visa and Mastercard work everywhere except small dai pai dong; carry HK$300–500 in cash for those and for taxi drivers who do not do electronic. The full Octopus guide covers Macau and cross-border use.
Hong Kong is also its own immigration jurisdiction. Most Western passports get 90 days visa-free here, and mainland visa-free transit rules do not carry over ( Hong Kong entry requirements ).

Adding mainland China on the end
If a beach day appeals less than crossing the border, West Kowloon to Futian in Shenzhen takes 14 minutes, with roughly 130 trains a day from 07:00. That is faster than getting to Stanley. Hong Kong to the mainland by high-speed rail covers the immigration arrangement, which happens inside West Kowloon station rather than at the border.
For a longer version of this trip, five days in Hong Kong adds the islands and a second day trip. For a much shorter one, an eight-hour layover is a different plan entirely.
FAQ
Is three nights enough for Hong Kong?
It gives you two full days plus an arrival evening. Enough for the harbour, the Peak, one neighbourhood on each side, one day trip and a night market. Not enough for the outlying islands, Ocean Park and Macau as well.
Do I still need cash in Hong Kong?
HK$300–500 covers dai pai dong, wet markets, and taxi drivers who decline cards. Everything else runs on Octopus, AlipayHK, WeChat Pay HK, Visa/Mastercard or Apple Pay.
Will my mainland Alipay work in Hong Kong?
No. AlipayHK and WeChat Pay HK are separate apps with separate accounts from the mainland versions. Use Octopus or a card instead.
When should I go?
October to early December, and March to April — dry, 20–26°C, low humidity. May to September is 28–33°C at 80–90% humidity with typhoon season on top.
