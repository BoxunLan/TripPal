Can tourists use Google in China?

Tourists can’t use Google in China the way they do elsewhere. With the Great Firewall of China , apps such as Instagram, X, WhatsApp, Google, and Western news portals like The New York Times are blocked .

However, there are ways to bypass restrictions, such as using the Holafly eSIM for China or using a Virtual Private Network (VPN) .

With Holafly, the eSIM routes your internet traffic through international networks outside China’s digital ecosystem , so blocked apps will work just fine.

If you decide to use a VPN, it’s better to choose a service that can bypass China’s restrictions, since not all apps work reliably.

Mobile internet in China: Best options for tourists

Getting mobile internet in China is necessary for many reasons: translating from Chinese, navigating big cities like Beijing, and staying in touch back home .

However, not all options are easily accessible: the China SIM card requires registration, local cafes are hard to find, and roaming is metered rather than unlimited.

For example, from Australia, Telstra’s International Day Pass covers China at $10 AUD a day for 2GB, while Vodafone’s $5 Roaming costs $5 AUD a day but uses the data already included in your plan. Check your own carrier’s rates before you fly.

There are other options available, though, so let’s explore how each one works and how it addresses local limitations to keep your phone working in China .

Pocket WiFi for internet in China

A pocket WiFi for China is a portable wireless router that creates a personal hotspot. It allows you to share your WiFi with a group or connect multiple devices simultaneously. These devices work in China because they usually come with a built-in VPN.

However, these can easily increase prices because travellers need to arrange pick-up and drop-off , pay a security deposit, and keep it as safe as possible, since these usually carry high penalties if something goes wrong .

Travellers can get anywhere about $59 to $197 AUD for a 1-week trip from companies like TravelersWiFi, 3G Solutions, and Cellular Abroad . However, this isn’t the final price, as delivery, insurance, and add-ons aren’t included .

Free WiFi

Free WiFi is widely available in China in restaurants, airports, hotels, trains, train stations, and cafes , especially in cities like Beijing and Shanghai . But these won’t work like a traditional WiFi network.

Here’s what to keep in mind regarding internet access using Chinese public networks:

Public WiFi in China often requires SMS verification : To get this type of internet access, you need a Chinese phone number or the ability to receive SMS on your primary number.

You still need a VPN : Like any other internet connectivity option in China, you’ll have to use a VPN to access apps blocked by the Great Firewall.

Free WiFi is unreliable : Be careful when using Chinese public WiFi in cities like Beijing, as it can leave your data vulnerable to hackers. It’s advisable to avoid sensitive activities like e-banking. You can use apps such as WiFimap to find available hotspots.
