Do You Actually Need a VPN with a China eSIM?
No. And this is the single biggest thing most eSIM articles get wrong, or deliberately fudge to sell you a more expensive plan.
Here's why: every international roaming eSIM routes your data through a server outside China. Usually Hong Kong or Singapore. Your phone connects to a Chinese cell tower, but the traffic exits through an international gateway before it ever touches the Great Firewall. Google works. WhatsApp works. Instagram, YouTube, Gmail -- all of it. According to esimdb's technical breakdown , all travel eSIMs currently available for China use roaming connections, so they're not affected by the firewall. Period.
So when a provider sells a "built-in VPN" as a premium feature for China, you don't need it for mobile data. Every provider in that table above bypasses the Great Firewall the same way, over roaming. You don't need the best eSIM for China with VPN. You just need any international eSIM.
Apps that work with a roaming eSIM:
Google Maps -- works for search and directions in major cities

WhatsApp -- messages and calls, no issues

Instagram -- normal speeds for scrolling and posting stories

Gmail -- sends and receives fine

YouTube -- streams, but eats through your data fast

Google Search -- works normally

The one catch: a local Chinese SIM card (the kind you buy at the airport counter or a China Mobile store) IS behind the Great Firewall. International roaming eSIMs bypass this entirely.
The same goes for Wi-Fi: the roaming route only covers your mobile data. The moment you connect to hotel, café, or airport Wi-Fi, you are back on a Chinese network and Google, Instagram and WhatsApp stop loading. Keep mobile data on when you need blocked apps, or install a backup VPN before you fly for laptop and Wi-Fi use.

How to Buy and Set Up Your eSIM (Do This Before You Fly)
This is where people mess up. You cannot count on setting up an eSIM once you're in China: Nomad won't sell you a new China plan there, and installing needs an internet connection, which in China usually means filtered Wi-Fi. Install everything before you board.
Confirm your phone supports eSIM. Most iPhones from XS (2018) onward do. Samsung Galaxy S20+ and newer. Check Settings > Cellular > Add eSIM. If the option shows up, you're good.

Buy your plan online. Go to getnomad.app (or your chosen provider), pick a China plan, and complete purchase. Takes 3 minutes.

Install the eSIM profile. Scan the QR code or follow the direct install link. Don't activate it yet. Wait until you land.

Turn on the eSIM when you land. Go to Settings > Cellular, enable your eSIM line for data. Keep your home SIM active for calls and SMS.

That dual-SIM setup is critical. Your phone runs both SIMs simultaneously: home number for receiving bank verification codes and calls from family, China eSIM for data. Set your eSIM as the data line and home SIM as the voice line in settings. Nobody explains this well, but it's the difference between a smooth trip and getting locked out of your bank account at a Chengdu ATM.
If you're planning when to visit, our best time to visit China guide can help you pick the right dates and the right eSIM plan length.
If you land without it installed, your fallbacks are a physical SIM from a carrier counter at the airport (bring your passport) or hotel Wi-Fi. If your route includes a stop in Hong Kong, Macau, or Taiwan, you can install the eSIM there before you reach the mainland.
If your iPhone was bought in mainland China, check the model before you buy a travel eSIM. Most mainland iPhones have two physical SIM slots and no eSIM. Apple lists a few newer mainland models that do support eSIM, including the iPhone Air (eSIM only, on sale in China since October 2025), the iPhone 17e and the iPhone 18 Pro, and says a mainland iPhone can't install an eSIM from a non-Chinese carrier while it is inside mainland China, so install the travel eSIM before you enter. iPhones bought anywhere else (US, EU, UK, Australia, Singapore, etc.) support eSIM normally. If you are unsure, dial *#06# and check for an EID number—if there is no EID, there is no eSIM.
In short: install whichever eSIM you choose before you arrive. For hotspot use, Nomad and Airalo allow tethering on their fixed-data plans, while Holafly's unlimited plan limits sharing to 1GB a day.

FAQ
Does eSIM work in China?
Yes. International eSIMs work through roaming agreements with China's three state-owned carriers: China Mobile (about 1 billion mobile customers), China Unicom, and China Telecom. Chinese carriers only began offering phone eSIMs in October 2025; activation happens in their stores, and Apple says iPhones bought outside mainland China can't install them. That's why you need an international provider like Nomad or Airalo.
Can I buy an eSIM in China?
Not reliably. Nomad blocks new China plans once you're inside mainland China, and installing any eSIM needs a stable internet connection, which in China usually means filtered Wi-Fi. If you arrive without an eSIM, your options are a physical SIM from the airport or a China Mobile store downtown; bring your passport. How to buy eSIM for China the right way: order online before your flight.
Does Google work with a China eSIM?
Yes. Google Maps, Gmail, YouTube, and Google Search all work with any international roaming eSIM. Your data routes through servers outside mainland China, so the Great Firewall doesn't apply. The only time Google is blocked is on a local Chinese SIM card or Chinese Wi-Fi.
What if my eSIM stops working mid-trip?
Toggle airplane mode on, wait 10 seconds, toggle off. If that doesn't work, go to Settings > Cellular and confirm your eSIM line is set as the data source. Still nothing? Restart your phone.
Does China have eSIM support for local plans?
Yes, since October 2025, but not in a way that helps most visitors. On October 13, 2025, China Mobile, China Telecom and China Unicom said they had received approval from the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (MIIT) to start commercial trials of eSIM service for smartphones. A local eSIM has to be activated in a carrier store with an ID check (a passport is accepted), and Apple says iPhones bought outside mainland China can't install an eSIM from a Chinese carrier. For most visitors, a travel eSIM remains the practical option.
Can I use eSIM in China with my iPhone?
Yes. iPhone XS, XS Max, XR and later models support eSIM, as long as the phone is unlocked -- check Apple's eSIM support page for the full list. The exception: most iPhones sold in mainland China have dual physical SIM slots but no eSIM. The mainland iPhone Air and a few newer mainland models do support eSIM, but Apple says they can't install an eSIM from a non-Chinese carrier while inside mainland China, so set it up before you enter. If you bought your phone in the US, Europe, or most other countries, you can use eSIM in China with no issues.
How much does a China eSIM cost?
Most plans cost between $4 (1GB for a few days) and $73.90 (unlimited data for 30 days), at prices checked September 27, 2026.
