Yes, foreigners can book and fly domestic flights in China with just a passport. You don't need a Chinese ID card, and foreign bank cards are accepted by ticketing platforms and by the airlines that also fly internationally. The easiest place to book in English is Trip.com (the international version of Ctrip); the airlines' own English websites and apps also work. On 27 September 2026, Trip.com's average one-way fare for flights in the following 30 days was US$117 from Beijing to Shanghai and US$161 from Beijing to Chengdu. This guide covers what to expect from booking to boarding.

Where to Book Your Flight
Trip.com (Ctrip's international site)
Trip.com is the foreigner-friendly face of Ctrip (携程). The site is in English, shows prices in US dollars or your own currency, takes foreign bank cards, and sells tickets on all the major Chinese airlines (Air China, China Eastern , China Southern, Hainan Airlines, Spring Airlines, Juneyao).
Key Trip.com advantages:
English customer service 24/7

Cancellation policy shown clearly in English before payment

Fliggy (Alibaba)
Fliggy (飞猪) is Alibaba's travel platform and sells the same domestic tickets, but its app is in Chinese.
Use Fliggy only if you read Chinese. Otherwise stick with Trip.com or the airline's own website.
Direct airline websites
Air China, China Eastern, and China Southern all sell tickets on English websites and apps. China's 2025 guide for foreign business travelers says airlines that fly internationally accept foreign bank cards on their ticketing channels. Changes and refunds follow the fare rules you see when you book, and you can request them through the website or app you booked on, or at the airport.
Domestic-only apps you should avoid
Qunar (去哪儿), Tongcheng (同程) and Meituan (美团) sell the same domestic tickets, but their apps are in Chinese. Unless you read Chinese, book on Trip.com or the airline's own website instead.
You don't need a Chinese bank card. Ticketing platforms such as Trip.com, and airlines that also fly internationally, accept foreign bank cards, and you can also pay with Alipay or WeChat Pay if you have set one up. When you book, give a phone number that will work in China: airlines send delay and cancellation notices by text message.

Peak travel dates
The busiest times to fly are Chinese New Year (late January to mid-February; the date moves each year), the May Day holiday at the start of May, the Mid-Autumn Festival (September or early October), and National Day Golden Week in the first week of October. Book as early as you can for those dates.
Budget carriers
Spring Airlines and 9 Air are low-cost carriers. Their cheapest fares include one cabin bag of up to 7 kg that fits within 20 × 30 × 40 cm and no checked bag; you add checked baggage when you book or pay at the airport counter. On 27 September 2026 the lowest Shanghai–Guangzhou fare on Trip.com was a 9 Air flight at US$55. Good for solo travelers with small luggage.
Check-In: What Actually Happens
Online check-in
Airlines open online check-in on their own websites and apps before departure. China Eastern, for example, opens it 48 hours ahead and closes it 45 minutes before a domestic flight; if you booked through Trip.com, it offers check-in for some airlines, China Eastern included. Airport security accepts a paper or an electronic boarding pass. If the airline's app or website doesn't give you a boarding pass with a QR code, or you have bags to check, go to the check-in counter.
At the airport: counter vs kiosk
Self-service check-in machines are common at Chinese airports. The China Civil Airports Association's 2024 service standard says they should read passports as well as Chinese ID cards, but whether a machine accepts your passport still depends on the airline and the airport. If it doesn't, go to the staffed counter; the same standard recommends placing an assisted counter near the machines for passengers who can't use them. At security and at the gate, some airports use face-scan gates; if one won't take your passport, use the staffed lane beside it, where an officer checks your passport and boarding pass.
Required documents:
Passport (must match booking exactly)

Booking confirmation (Trip.com PDF or SMS code)

The counter agent scans your passport, prints a boarding pass, and tags checked bags.
Arrive how early
Domestic flight: plan to arrive about 2 hours before departure

Checking bags after online check-in: be at the counter at least 60 minutes before departure (China Eastern's rule)

Public holidays: allow extra time for queues

Check-in counters for domestic flights close about 30 to 45 minutes before departure at most airports, depending on the airport and the airline; China Eastern publishes the exact time for every airport it flies from. At Shanghai Pudong , for example, domestic check-in closes 40 minutes before departure.
Security Rules You Must Know
Liquids: only small toiletries in your carry-on
On domestic flights, China's rule is stricter than the usual 100 ml rule: liquids are not allowed in your carry-on at all, except toiletries for the trip. Cosmetics must be in containers of no more than 100 ml, one of each kind, and may be opened for inspection; toothpaste and shaving cream are limited to one tube each, up to 100 g (ml). The limit is the size of the container, not how much is left in it. Liquids you buy after security can come on board, and baby milk and necessary liquid medicine are allowed after a check.
Items that won't get through security:
Full toothpaste tubes (150ml)

Sunscreen bottles over 100ml

Two of the same cosmetic, even if both are small

Any bottled water bought outside the airport

Tip: buy water after security.
Power banks: watt-hour limits and the 3C mark
Power banks go in your carry-on, never in checked bags, and the label must show the rated energy in Wh or enough information to work it out. Limits:
Up to 100 Wh: allowed in carry-on, must be in hand luggage (not checked)

Over 100 Wh and up to 160 Wh: only with airline approval, two per person at most

Over 160 Wh, or no readable rating: not allowed

Since 28 June 2025, a power bank also needs a clear 3C (CCC) certification mark to go on a domestic flight. Power banks with no 3C mark, an unreadable one, or from a recalled model or batch are refused at security, and you have to hand them over or use the airport's storage point. The 3C mark is China's own certification, so check any power bank bought abroad before you fly; if it has none, buy a 3C-marked one in China. On board, power banks must stay switched off: you can't use one to charge a device or charge the power bank itself.
Electronics scan
At security you first show your passport and boarding pass, then your bags go through the X-ray. Staff may ask you to take laptops and tablets out of your bag and put them in a separate tray.
Lighters and matches
China bans lighters and matches on domestic flights entirely: not on you, not in your carry-on, not in checked baggage. The same ban covers other fire sources such as fire strikers and self-heating meal packs. Carrying a lighter or matches through security can bring a fine or detention from the police, not just confiscation. If you smoke, buy a lighter after you land.

Air China, China Eastern, China Southern, Hainan, Xiamen and Juneyao limit the cabin bag to 55 × 40 × 20 cm, and most also allow a small personal item such as a handbag or laptop bag; Spring Airlines and 9 Air limit the free cabin bag to 20 × 30 × 40 cm. Some discounted fares include less checked baggage or none, so check the allowance shown when you book. Excess baggage on domestic flights costs 1.5% of that day's full one-way economy fare for the route per extra kilogram at Air China, China Eastern, Xiamen Airlines and Juneyao; Spring Airlines sells extra baggage online in steps from 5 kg to 50 kg. Weigh your bag before leaving the hotel.
Odd rules foreigners should know
Durian and other strong-smelling food: CAAC bans items with a strong, pungent smell from the cabin and from checked bags; China Eastern takes strong-smelling fruit only as checked baggage sealed so no smell escapes

Knives, scissors and tools: not allowed in your carry-on; pack them in checked baggage

Spare lithium batteries: carry-on only, each protected against short circuits (original packaging, its own plastic bag, or tape over the terminals)

Compensation only applies when the airline itself caused the delay, for example through maintenance, crew or scheduling; in that case the airline also has to provide meals or a hotel. For weather, air traffic control and other causes outside the airline's control, the airline helps you arrange meals and a hotel, but you pay. China Eastern pays in cash or, if you agree, in vouchers or miles, and infants get 10% of the amount; China Southern counts the delay at arrival. Airlines must post delay and cancellation information within 30 minutes of learning about it, and if you are kept on the plane for 2 hours or more they must give you water and food.
How to actually claim compensation
Ask the airline for a delay certificate (延误证明). Airlines must issue one on request, and many offer it on their website or app

Photograph the gate display showing the delay and reason

Claim through the airline or the platform you booked with, following the airline's own rules

If the airline refuses, complain to CAAC's service quality platform on 12326 or at www.12326.cn

Boarding and Gate Procedures
Once through security, follow the signs to your gate. At the largest airports the walk can be long, and gate changes are announced on the screens and over the speakers, so check the display board again before boarding.
At the gate, you present:
Boarding pass

Passport (matched to name on boarding pass)

The gate agent scans both and waves you through. Gates close 15 or 20 minutes before departure at most large airports, and the closing time is printed on your boarding pass. If you reach the gate after it closes, you have missed the flight, and the airline's rules for voluntary changes or refunds apply.
In-flight service in 2026
Full-service carriers: China Eastern, for example, serves free drinks or food on board under its conditions of carriage

Budget carriers: 9 Air serves no free food or drinks; you can pre-order when you book or buy on board

Phones: on China Eastern you can use your phone for the whole flight in flight mode

Do I need a Chinese SIM to book a domestic flight?
No. You can buy domestic tickets with a passport on Trip.com or on an airline's own website or app, and pay with a foreign bank card. Give a phone number that will work in China, because airlines send delay and cancellation notices by text message.
Will the airline accept a scanned passport on my phone?
No. You need the physical passport for check-in, security, and boarding, and it must be the same passport you booked with. A photo or scan on your phone is not a valid travel document.
Can I change a Trip.com flight after booking?
Usually, yes. Change and refund fees depend on the airline and the fare type, and the terms are shown before you pay. You can ask for a change through the platform you booked with or at the airport. If the airline causes a delay or cancellation, it has to rebook you free of charge.
What if the airline kiosk is the only option and it rejects my passport?
Go to a staffed check-in counter for your airline. China's airport service standard recommends placing an assisted counter near the self-service machines for passengers who can't use them.
Is there a kids or senior discount on domestic flights?
For children, yes. A child aged 2 to 11 pays 50% of the adult full fare for the same cabin, and an infant under 2 without a seat pays 10%. Children pay half the fuel surcharge and no civil aviation development fund. Because the child fare is based on the full fare, it can cost more than a discounted adult ticket, so compare both. There is no national senior fare, though some airlines run their own senior offers.
Can I bring food on a domestic flight?
Yes, solid food is fine. Soups, sauces, drinks and other liquids can't go in your carry-on on a domestic flight; only toiletries in containers of up to 100 ml pass. Budget airlines such as 9 Air don't serve free food, so bring snacks or buy on board.
What to Do If Something Goes Wrong at the Airport
If the kiosk won't read your passport, go to the staffed counter; that's normal. If a face-scan gate won't let you through, use the staffed lane beside it. If security stops your power bank, it probably lacks a readable 3C mark or energy rating; hand it over or store it, then buy a 3C-marked one in China. If the name on your ticket doesn't match your passport, contact the airline or booking platform before you travel, because the ID you show has to match the one used to book. If your flight is delayed, ask whether the airline caused it: if so, it provides meals or a hotel and may owe compensation; for weather or air traffic control you get rebooking or a refund under the fare rules.
