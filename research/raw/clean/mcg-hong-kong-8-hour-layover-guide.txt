Eight hours is not eight hours. Count from wheels-down: ten to fifteen minutes to walk off and reach immigration, thirty to forty-five through immigration and baggage, twenty-four on the Airport Express. Then the same again in reverse, plus your airline's cut-off.
What survives is three to three and a half hours of actual city. Enough for one anchor — the Peak, or the harbour crossing — plus a sit-down meal. Not two anchors.
And there is a wall at the other end that does not move: check-in closes exactly 60 minutes before departure at HKG, with no grace period. Everything below is arranged around that.
This page plans an eight-hour connection only; for shorter or longer stops, an overnight near the airport, hotels and the weather, see the Hong Kong layover guide by length .

The move that changes the arithmetic
Check in for your onward flight in the city, not at the airport.
The Airport Express runs a free In-Town Check-In at Hong Kong Station and Kowloon Station: drop your hold bags, collect your boarding pass at a downtown airline desk, then walk around with nothing and go straight to the gate when you come back. It is open from 24 hours before departure until 90 minutes before, and it is included with an Airport Express ticket.
Two things follow from that. The 60-minute airport deadline becomes a 90-minute downtown one that you have already cleared before you start sightseeing. And you stop paying for left luggage.
The catch: only certain airlines offer it, only on flights they operate out of HKG themselves, not on codeshare segments. Check your specific carrier before you count on it.
If you are keeping your bags, the official Left Baggage counter is in Terminal 1, Arrival Hall A, Arrivals Level (L5), landside, open 05:30 to 01:30, run by S.F. Express. A standard piece is HK$14 an hour or HK$165 a day, whichever is lower. Guides still quoting HK$12 an hour or HK$140 a day are out of date.

The free one is genuinely the best value, and if your timing puts you on the waterfront after dark the Symphony of Lights runs at 20:00 from the Tsim Sha Tsui promenade for nothing.
Coming back the same day costs nothing extra. With an Octopus or contactless bank card the fare is HK$120 to Hong Kong Station, HK$105 to Kowloon or HK$73 to Tsing Yi (HK$130, HK$115 and HK$80 for a paper Same Day Return ticket), and the ride back is free as long as you tap in and out with the same card both ways; a bank card loses the offer if you use it for other rides in between. For everything else inside the city, an Octopus card covers the MTR, buses, the Star Ferry and most shops.
The free layover tour
Hong Kong International Airport launched a free city tour for transfer passengers with Trip.com in September 2025, and it was still running when this page was checked in September 2026.
You need a connection of more than seven hours and must meet Hong Kong's entry requirements, so an eight-hour layover qualifies for most passports. Guide, transport and entry tickets are all included. Three routes run daily, each about four hours:

Reserve up to three months ahead, or register on the day at the Free Layover Tour counter subject to space. Note the four-hour commitment: on an eight-hour layover that is most of your margin, so it works best when your inbound is on time and your onward flight is at the later end.
Do not try Shenzhen
The most common way people wreck this. The round trip to Futian via West Kowloon is four hours door-to-door at the very least, and crossing into the mainland needs a Chinese visa or a qualifying visa-free arrangement — Hong Kong's own visa-free entry does not extend across the border.
Hong Kong is a separate immigration jurisdiction; most Western passports get 90 days on arrival here ( Hong Kong entry requirements ). If the mainland is genuinely the point of the trip, the high-speed rail from West Kowloon is worth doing on a real stopover, not on this one.
If you stay inside instead
Middle-of-the-night arrival, heavy jet lag, or a typhoon warning — all good reasons not to leave. HKG is one of the better airports to be stuck in.

Getting back without drama
Work backwards from departure:
Board the Airport Express three hours before departure.

At HKG two hours fifteen before.

At the check-in counter two hours before — the desk closes at sixty.

Through security ninety minutes before.

Security is the variable. Terminal 1 has two checkpoints, for Gates 1–40 and 41–80, and the wait swings from a five-minute walk-through at 06:00 to something much worse in the evening peak.
One weather note: typhoons between June and September can suspend the Airport Express and the ferries within a couple of hours. Check the Hong Kong Observatory app before you commit to leaving the terminal in that season. What to do when a layover turns into an overnight covers that case.
With more than a day to spend, three nights in Hong Kong is the version of this trip where you actually unpack.
FAQ
Is eight hours enough to leave Hong Kong airport?
Yes, with about three to three and a half hours of real city time once immigration, baggage and the Airport Express are paid for in both directions. Enough for one anchor activity and a meal. Under six hours, stay inside.
What is the single best thing to do first?
Use the free In-Town Check-In at Hong Kong or Kowloon Station. It hands you a boarding pass, takes your bags, and replaces the 60-minute airport deadline with a 90-minute downtown one you have already met.
Is there still a free Hong Kong layover tour?
Yes. Hong Kong International Airport launched it with Trip.com in September 2025 for passengers with connections of more than seven hours who meet Hong Kong's entry requirements, and it was still running in September 2026. Three four-hour routes daily, with guide, transport and entry included.
Can I visit Shenzhen on an eight-hour layover?
No. The round trip is four hours minimum door-to-door and mainland China needs its own visa or a qualifying visa-free arrangement. Hong Kong's visa-free entry stops at the border.
