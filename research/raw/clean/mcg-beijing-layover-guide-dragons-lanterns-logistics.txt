A 6-10 hour layover in Beijing is just enough to escape the terminal if you plan the timing properly. The 240-hour visa-free transit policy, Beijing's Airport Express at ¥25, and a clear 2.5-hour return buffer leave room for one major landmark - Temple of Heaven, Tiananmen Square, or a Hutong walking loop - plus a meal. Anything more is fantasy unless your layover hits 10+ hours. This 2026 guide breaks down exactly what fits.
This page is about what to see with the hours you have; the PEK layover timing guide covers whether you can leave the airport, the visa-free entry procedure and how long the trip into the city takes.

240-Hour Visa-Free Transit: The First Question
You can only leave the airport if your passport is covered by China's 240-hour visa-free transit and you fly on to a third country or region. As of August 2026 that means 57 countries, including the US, UK, Canada, Australia, every EU country, Japan and South Korea, and both PEK and PKX accept it.
PEK vs PKX: Never Switch Airports
Beijing's two international airports, Capital (PEK) and Daxing (PKX), have no airside link, so a layover is only worth leaving the terminal for if you land and take off at the same one. The itineraries below start from PEK.
The Time Budget: Why 8 Hours Is the Minimum
Immigration, the Airport Express each way, the check-in deadline and security take roughly five and a half to seven hours before you see anything, so eight hours leaves about 90 minutes at one sight and ten hours about three. The PEK layover timing guide breaks those hours down step by step and covers the entry procedure and the 24-hour option for other passports.
6-Hour Layover: Stay Airside
With less than 6 hours, the risk of missing your connection is too high. Stay inside the terminal.
Clear arrival security if you are transferring airside.

Use PEK Terminal 3 cultural corridor - rotating exhibits on Chinese calligraphy and pottery.

Shower or nap at Plaza Premium Lounge or Air China Lounge (PriorityPass accepted for some).

Eat noodles at the T3 food court - Beijing zha jiang mian or hand-pulled noodles from ¥40-80.

Walk the full length of T3 - at 2.9 kilometers it is one of the longest terminals in the world and good for jet lag.

8-Hour Layover: Olympic Green Only
Eight hours buys you exactly one close-in stop. The Olympic Green, site of the 2008 Summer Games and 2022 Winter Games, is the closest major landmark to PEK and the easiest to fit.
Land, clear immigration with a 240-hour stamp (60-90 min).

Store luggage at the PEK left-baggage counter; one bag is free if you show the temporary entry permit label from your 240-hour entry.

Airport Express to Sanyuanqiao, transfer to Metro Line 10, to Line 8 at Beitucheng, get off at Olympic Sports Center Station. The Airport Express is ¥25 and the metro adds a few yuan.

Walk the plaza, photograph the Bird's Nest and Water Cube exteriors (entry tickets require advance reservation and are often sold out).

Grab a quick lunch at a nearby mall.

Return to PEK at least 3 hours before departure.

10-Hour Layover: Temple of Heaven or Tiananmen
A 10-hour layover opens up the real downtown sights. You still only pick one - do not try to chain landmarks in a layover.
Option A: Temple of Heaven
Take the Airport Express to its last stop, Beixinqiao, and change to Line 5 south to Tiantandongmen; the Airport Express is ¥25 and the metro adds a few yuan. Temple of Heaven entry is ¥35 for the park and ¥14 extra for the Hall of Prayer. Budget 2 hours inside. Exit via the east gate, grab lunch in a nearby hutong.
Option B: Tiananmen Square and Wangfujing
Airport Express to Dongzhimen, Metro Line 2 to Qianmen. Walk north into Tiananmen Square (free but requires advance online reservation on a WeChat mini-program using your passport - reserve 1-7 days ahead). You cannot enter the Forbidden City without a ticket booked 7 days in advance and tickets sell out within minutes. Skip it on a layover. Walk up Wangfujing pedestrian street for lunch, snacks and shopping.
Option C: Hutong Walking Loop
Airport Express to Dongzhimen, taxi or Metro to Nanluoguxiang or Gulou. Walk the hutongs, visit the Drum Tower (¥20), eat at a local noodle house. This is the most atmospheric 3-hour option and does not require any advance booking.
Option D: Mutianyu Great Wall (12+ hours only)
The Mutianyu section is the closest Great Wall site to PEK at 70 km and 90 minutes by private car. Do NOT attempt this on less than 12 hours of layover. Private driver services run ¥600-900 round trip and must be booked via Trip.com or Klook before arrival. Public transport takes 3 hours each way and is not viable.
Spring Festival Dragon Dances and Lantern Festival
If your 2026 layover falls during Spring Festival (February 17, 2026) or the two weeks following, you can catch something most travelers never see.
Dragon dance temple fairs
Ditan Park (Temple of Earth) hosts the largest Spring Festival temple fair in Beijing. Dragon troupes perform 2-3 times daily, typically 10:00, 13:00 and 15:00. Entry ¥10-20.

Longtan Park hosts a similar fair with smaller crowds and more traditional folk performances.

The China Ethnic Culture Park (Minzu Yuan) near the Olympic Green includes daily ethnic minority performances year-round where dragon dances appear in the folk schedule.

Beijing Acrobatics Theater offers a guaranteed comfortable seat with English subtitles and often includes traditional elements in the finale.

Lantern Festival (March 3, 2026)
Beijing's Lantern Festival celebrations light up from around 17:00 on the holiday and for two weeks around it.

Important: Beijing parks now use a strict reservation system. Book tickets in advance via WeChat Mini-Programs (search "Changyou Park" or the individual park name) using your passport details. Walk-up tickets are often unavailable during holiday weeks.
Digital Setup: Non-Negotiable
Beijing runs on QR codes and the Chinese internet. Set this up before you land.
Install Alipay and WeChat Pay. Link an international Visa or Mastercard. Verify identity by uploading a passport photo inside the app. This unlocks full transaction limits.

Install Amap (Gaode Maps) or Baidu Maps. Google Maps is outdated in mainland China. Apple Maps works reasonably well in Beijing but Amap is more accurate.

Install Didi - either the English standalone app or the Didi mini-program inside Alipay. This is the only safe way to get a taxi outside the airport queue.

Activate international roaming before departure or purchase a travel eSIM with China support (Airalo, Nomad, Holafly). Data routed through overseas servers continues to access Google, WhatsApp and Gmail without a VPN.

Carry ¥500 in emergency cash from an airport ATM. Digital wallets are primary but a few small vendors and older taxis still prefer cash.

Scam Warnings
Beijing airports and major tourist sites attract a small but persistent scam economy. Stay alert.
Unlicensed taxis: ignore anyone inside the terminal offering "cheap rides" to the city. Use only the official taxi queue outside arrivals or book a Didi via Alipay. Official taxis run on the meter, and you pay the road tolls on top.

Tea ceremony trap: polite young people invite you to a "traditional tea ceremony" near Wangfujing or Qianmen. The bill arrives at the end for ¥2000-5000 per person. Decline all unsolicited tea invitations.

Art gallery scam: similar pattern - a student invites you to see their calligraphy work and pressures you into buying low-quality reproductions for hundreds of yuan.

Fake ticket sellers: do not buy Forbidden City or Temple of Heaven tickets from street touts. Use official park WeChat mini-programs or Trip.com.

Airport switching: never attempt to switch between PEK and PKX on a layover. You will miss your flight.

Can I leave Beijing airport during a layover?
Yes, if your passport is covered by 240-hour visa-free transit and you fly on to a third country or region, but it only pays off with at least eight hours between flights. The PEK layover timing guide covers the entry procedure, the 24-hour option for other passports and how the hours add up.
How much does the Beijing Airport Express cost in 2026?
¥25 one way on the Capital Airport Express, which runs about every 10 minutes between Terminals 3 and 2, Sanyuanqiao, Dongzhimen and Beixinqiao; the last train back to the airport leaves Dongzhimen at 22:30. Daxing Airport (PKX) has its own express line from Caoqiao.
What can I see in a 6-hour Beijing layover?
Nothing downtown. Six hours minus immigration, baggage, return security and buffer leaves under 2 hours on the ground - not enough time to reach any major site. Stay airside. Use the PEK T3 cultural corridor, lounges or food court. Plan for an 8-hour minimum before attempting to leave the terminal.
Is the Forbidden City possible on a layover?
Rarely. Forbidden City tickets must be reserved on a WeChat mini-program 7 days in advance using your passport, and popular days sell out within minutes of release. Additionally, the walking circuit through the Forbidden City requires 2-3 hours, pushing the total round trip from PEK above 7 hours. Skip it on a layover and see it on a proper trip.
Are there dragon dances in Beijing year-round?
Authentic large-scale dragon dances happen during Spring Festival (Chinese New Year falls on February 17, 2026) at temple fairs like Ditan Park and Longtan Park. Outside the holiday, the China Ethnic Culture Park near the Olympic Green schedules smaller daily performances that sometimes include dragon elements. For a guaranteed show with English subtitles, book Beijing Acrobatics Theater on Trip.com before arrival.
