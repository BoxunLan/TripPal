A private driver in Guilin costs ¥400-800 per day for a standard sedan in 2026, and that is the most reliable way to see the Li River karst country, Yangshuo, and the Longji Rice Terraces without spending half your holiday on tourist buses. For English-speaking drivers, expect to add ¥200-300 per day. The booking channels that work best for international travelers are Trip.com, Klook, and Viator, with DiDi Premier as a solid backup for short point-to-point trips.

Airport transfers
Guilin Liangjiang International Airport sits about 30 km from downtown Guilin and 65 km from Yangshuo. 2026 rates:
Airport to Guilin city hotel: ¥150-250 standard sedan, ¥250-350 MPV

Airport to Yangshuo: ¥400-600 standard sedan, ¥600-900 MPV

Airport to Longji (rare, most people break it up): ¥800-1,100

If you land after 22:00, expect a ¥50-100 late-hour surcharge on pre-booked transfers. DiDi rates can be lower on the airport-to-city leg if you are willing to use the app in Chinese.
Multi-day discounts
Booking a driver for 3 or more consecutive days usually unlocks a 10-20% discount off the day rate. A typical 3-day Guilin-Yangshuo-Longji package for a standard sedan sits around ¥1,600-2,200 in 2026, which is significantly better value than stringing together three separate single-day bookings.
What is actually included in a driver quote
Quotes vary a lot in what they bundle in. The sentence that matters is "all-inclusive" versus "driver and fuel only."
Usually included
Fuel

Driver's service fee and daily meal allowance (or the driver handles their own lunch separately)

Hotel pickup and drop-off within Guilin city or Yangshuo town center

A standard insurance policy on the vehicle and passengers

Usually excluded
Entrance tickets for you (Longji Ping'an gate is ¥95, Xiangshan Park is ¥70, Reed Flute Cave is ¥110)

Entrance tickets for the driver if they accompany you into a park (rare, most drivers wait at the parking lot)

Parking fees at busy sites (¥10-50 each stop)

Tolls on expressways (¥30-80 depending on the route)

Your own lunch, drinks, and snacks

Tips (not required in China but appreciated; ¥50-100 at the end of a full day is generous)

Overtime
Most charter contracts cover 8 to 10 hours from pickup to drop-off. Beyond that, drivers usually charge ¥50-100 per extra hour. If you know you will linger at the terraces for sunset or want a long dinner in Yangshuo before driving back, agree on the overtime rate before departure.
Where to book
There are five real options, ranked roughly by ease-of-use for international travelers:
1. Trip.com (formerly Ctrip international)
Search "Guilin private car charter" or filter the "transportation" category on the Guilin page. Trip.com runs in English, accepts Visa and Mastercard, and handles the local coordination for you. Prices are typically 10-20% above the bare local rate, which is a fair markup for the English support and payment security. Most listings let you pick car type, duration, and pickup location, and they send a WhatsApp or SMS confirmation 24 hours before pickup.
2. Klook
Klook has fewer Guilin listings than Trip.com but the ones it does have are curated and reviewed in English. Best for Longji Rice Terraces full-day tours and airport transfers. Prices are similar to Trip.com.
3. Viator
Viator's Guilin charters are often resold from local operators. Cancellation policies are usually better (free cancellation up to 24 hours before). Good if you want to keep flexibility for weather.
4. DiDi Chuxing for point-to-point
DiDi is the Chinese equivalent of Uber. The international version of the app has an English interface and accepts international Visa and Mastercard. For single trips (airport to hotel, Guilin to Yangshuo one way), DiDi Express or DiDi Premier is usually 30-40% cheaper than a charter quote. Limitations: drivers almost never speak English, they will not wait for you during sightseeing, and they will not do the typical "6 stops across the day" itinerary that a charter includes.
5. Hotel concierge
Any 4-star or 5-star hotel in Guilin or Yangshuo can arrange a driver from their preferred agency. Expect prices 20-30% above Trip.com rates. The upside: direct accountability through the hotel and a trusted, often English-speaking driver. Worth it if you cannot get the booking apps to work or want a fully concierge-level experience.
Why not WeChat contacts from forums
Travel forums sometimes offer "I know a great driver, DM me his WeChat." Avoid this route if you are traveling internationally. The contacts go stale, they often are not licensed charter drivers, and they have no accountability if anything goes wrong. The ¥50-100 you might save is not worth the risk of a mid-trip breakdown with no recourse.

Two-day Guilin-Yangshuo-Longji loop
Day 1: Guilin morning sights, drive to Yangshuo via the Li River, overnight in Yangshuo

Two-day rate for a standard sedan, roughly ¥1,200-1,600 total. The driver usually stays in a nearby village with their own accommodation arranged; you do not need to pay for their hotel unless explicitly stated in the quote.
Payment and communication setup
Apps to install before you arrive
Alipay with an international credit card linked. The Tour Pass feature on Alipay now lets international users pay Chinese drivers and merchants without a Chinese bank account.

WeChat Pay , also with an international card. Some drivers prefer WeChat Pay over Alipay; it is easier to have both ready.

DiDi Chuxing (the international version) for point-to-point backup rides.

Amap (the Chinese equivalent of Google Maps, also called Gaode Map) for navigation. Google Maps does not work reliably in China without a VPN.

WeChat for driver coordination. Almost every driver communicates via WeChat location pins and voice messages.

Communication without shared language
If your driver does not speak English, use WeChat's translation feature (press and hold on a received message to translate) or Google Translate with the offline Chinese language pack downloaded before you leave. The Amap app also has a driver-friendly "share destination" feature that works across languages.
Cash
China is largely cashless but carry ¥500-1,000 in paper yuan for small tips, rural village snacks, and emergency backups. Most drivers accept cash as a fallback if your WeChat or Alipay has a connectivity hiccup.
Scams and issues to watch for
Guilin is a major tourist region. Most drivers are honest, but there are recurring patterns worth knowing.
The commission shopping trap
A driver or agency quotes you a rate that is noticeably below market (¥300 for a full day when everyone else is at ¥500-700). The catch is usually that the driver will route the day through a tea shop, jade factory, silk workshop, or pearl shop where they earn a commission on your purchases. These stops are time-wasters and the prices inside are marked up 3-5x over downtown shops.
How to prevent it: write in the booking confirmation, "No shopping stops please. I do not want to visit tea, jade, silk, or pearl shops." If the driver brings it up on the day, decline politely but firmly. If they persist, contact the booking platform's customer service and ask to file a complaint or request a replacement driver.
Unlicensed "black car" touts at the airport
At the Guilin Liangjiang airport arrivals hall, men will approach you asking "taxi? taxi?" These are unlicensed black-car drivers. Decline and walk to the official taxi queue or meet your pre-booked driver at the designated pickup zone.
Price creep during the trip
A driver agrees to ¥600 for the day, then at the midpoint mentions that tolls, lunch, or parking are extra. These should already be clarified before you leave the hotel. A legitimate quote spells out exactly what is covered. If this happens, pay only what was in the written quote and settle the rest through the booking platform after the fact.
Vehicle condition
Ask to confirm car age and condition when booking. In 2026, most legitimate charters run vehicles less than 5 years old with working air conditioning. Older cars in the Guilin humidity are genuinely miserable.

Is it cheaper to take a Guilin-Yangshuo bus than to hire a driver?
Yes, significantly. The express bus from Guilin train station to Yangshuo costs ¥30-45 and takes about 90 minutes. For budget travelers doing point-to-point transfers only, the bus wins on price. A private driver wins when you want to stop for photos, visit multiple villages in one day, or carry luggage without public transport transfers.
Can I rent a self-drive car in Guilin as a foreigner?
Technically yes, but not practically. You need a Chinese driver's license (international permits are not recognized), and most rental agencies in Guilin will not rent to foreigners without one. Even if you have a Chinese license, the mountain roads to Longji are narrow, winding, and not where you want to be driving for the first time. Hiring a driver is the standard solution and is genuinely the better choice.
What is a realistic tip for a full-day driver in 2026?
Tipping is not mandatory in China and most drivers do not expect it. For a good day with a driver who was patient, punctual, and helpful, ¥50-100 is a generous and appreciated tip. For an English-speaking driver who served as an informal guide, ¥100-200 is reasonable. Do not tip if the service was actively poor.
How far in advance should I book a Guilin driver?
Peak season (April-May, September-October, Golden Week) book 2-3 weeks ahead to lock in the car type and driver you want. Off-season (November-March excluding Chinese New Year, June-August), 5-7 days is usually enough. Airport transfers can be booked day-of through DiDi in most cases.
Can I pay the driver directly in cash instead of through a booking platform?
Trip.com and similar platforms usually collect payment upfront, so the driver you meet has already been paid. Do not pay again on the day unless the driver shows the booking and explicitly asks for balance payment (which should have been disclosed in the quote). If you book directly through a local agency, cash or WeChat Pay on the day is normal. Always get a written receipt, either a photo of the quote or a WeChat transfer record.
Whether a driver is the right call depends on what you are actually doing. For the Li River and the villages around Yangshuo, yes — the sites are spread out and the buses are slow. For the climbing, no: Yangshuo's crags are reachable by bike and a driver spends the day waiting. And if Guilin is one leg of a longer route, the fifteen-day five-city itinerary shows where the Guilin stations sit. There are three, and direct trains to Shanghai leave from more than one of them; our train guide to Guilin and Yangshuo explains which station each route uses.
Booking the driver through a platform also means the refund rules are the platform's, not the railway's — which is worth knowing if the trains either side of your Guilin days might move. China Railway's own refund ladder is unusually generous by comparison: free more than eight days out.
