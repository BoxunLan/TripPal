4 business days. That's how long it takes to get a Chinese tourist visa — the official processing time for standard service. Express cuts it to 2-3 days for an extra ¥180 ($25), but that option isn't available if it's your first Chinese visa. The real China visa turnaround time from start to finish? About 7-10 days if you're organized. Here's the full breakdown.
If you're still figuring out whether you even need a visa, check out our complete guide to China visa requirements for Americans first, you might qualify for 240-hour visa-free entry and skip this whole process entirely.
Honestly, 8 out of 10 clients ask me how long does it take to get a China visa. And most of them make the same mistake — they hear "4 days" and assume they can apply on Monday and fly out on Saturday. Not quite. Let me walk you through what actually happens.
Chinese Visa Processing Time: The Official Numbers
Here's what the Chinese Embassy officially states for China visa processing times:

Who Can Use It

4 working days

1-2 working days

A few things to know about these numbers.
"Working days" means weekdays only. Weekends don't count. Chinese holidays don't count either. So if you submit your application on a Wednesday, standard processing means your passport is ready the following Tuesday, not Sunday.
Here's the thing nobody mentions: express and rush processing are not available for first-time applicants. If you've never had a Chinese visa before, you're looking at the full 4 working days. No shortcuts. I've had clients show up at the visa center expecting to pay extra for speed, only to find out they can't. Budget the full time.
I'm not 100% sure rush service is even available at every CVASC location. The centers in New York and San Francisco offer it, but some smaller offices might not. Call ahead if you're counting on it.

How Long Does a Chinese Visa Actually Take? (The Full Timeline)
So the China visa application processing time is officially 4 working days. But that's just the time your passport sits with the consular officer. The actual china visa application timeline — from deciding to apply until you're holding your stamped passport — looks more like this:
Step

Time Needed

Gather documents (passport, photo, proof of residence)

1-3 days

Complete COVA online form

30-60 minutes

Walk in to visa center + submit documents

1-3 hours

Processing (standard)

4 working days

Return to pick up passport

1-2 hours

Total (realistic)

7-10 days

I tell all my clients the same thing: start 3-4 weeks before your flight. Not because it takes that long to get a China visa, but because it gives you a buffer. Things go wrong sometimes, a photo gets rejected, you forgot a document, you hit a peak season backlog. Three weeks means room to breathe.
And whatever you do, don't apply too early. Had a client last month who submitted her application 4 months before her trip. Got a single-entry visa, valid for entry within 3 months of issuance. Her visa expired before she even packed her suitcase. ¥1,000 ($140), gone. She had to reapply from scratch.
The sweet spot? 3-4 weeks out. Not 3-4 months.
What You Need to Apply (Simpler Than You Think)
The documents needed for a Chinese visa application got much easier in 2024. Here's the full list for an L visa (tourist):
Valid passport , at least 6 months remaining, 2 blank pages

COVA application form , completed online at consular.mfa.gov.cn , printed and signed

One photo , 33mm x 48mm , white background, recent (this is different from the US passport photo size of 51mm x 51mm — see Chinese visa photo requirements for exact specs and common mistakes)

Proof of US legal residence , driver's license or utility bill

That's it.
What you DON'T need anymore:
~~Flight itineraries~~

~~Hotel reservations~~

~~Invitation letters~~

~~Travel insurance proof~~

All of those were required before 2024. If you're reading an older guide that tells you to book refundable flights just for the visa application, ignore it. Those requirements are gone. This alone shaves days off your prep time.
The COVA System: How It Changed Everything
In September 2025, China launched the COVA online application system . It replaced the old paper-heavy process and made the china visa application time significantly shorter. Here's how it works now:
Go to consular.mfa.gov.cn/VISA

Create an account and fill out your application online

Upload your photo (quick heads up, the photo upload sometimes rejects perfectly fine photos. Known bug. Don't panic, consular officers review manually anyway)

Wait for status to show "Passport to Be Submitted"

Walk in to your nearest CVASC or consulate with your passport and printed form

No appointment needed. Walk-ins have been accepted since October 2023. This alone shaves weeks off the old timeline — before that, how long to get a Chinese visa depended heavily on appointment availability, with wait times stretching 30+ business days at busy locations.
The whole in-person visit usually takes 1-3 hours. Get there before 10am. Lines are shorter early.
What Can Slow Down Your China Visa Processing Time?
Most applications process in exactly 4 working days. But some things add time:
Incomplete documents. This is the #1 delay. Missing signature on the form, wrong photo size, expired passport. Double-check everything before you go. Then check again. I've seen people drive 2 hours to the visa center only to get turned away because their photo was 2mm too small. Painful.
Peak seasons. Chinese New Year (January/February), National Day (first week of October), and summer travel season can create backlogs. I've seen processing stretch to 6-7 working days during Golden Week. If you're traveling during these periods, add an extra week to your timeline.
First-time fingerprinting. If you're between 14 and 70 and haven't given fingerprints for a Chinese visa in the past 5 years, you'll need to do it in person. Quick, takes about 5 minutes at the visa center, but it's one more reason you can't skip the in-person visit.
Born outside the US? If you're a US citizen born in China or certain other countries, you may need extra documentation. Processing can take a few extra days.

The ¥1,000 ($140) is actually a reduced rate; it was ¥1,315 ($185) before. This discount runs through December 31, 2026 , per the Chinese Embassy . And yes, ¥1,000 ($140) gets you a 10-year multiple-entry visa . Same price as single-entry. Get the 10-year. No reason not to.
For a deeper cost breakdown, check out our guide to China visa costs for US citizens .
And skip the visa agencies. Seriously. They charge ¥2,130-4,260 ($300-600) on top of the embassy fee for something you can do yourself in an afternoon. The COVA system made the process simple enough that you don't need a middleman taking ¥2,000-4,000 ($280-560) from your travel budget.
Do You Even Need a Chinese Tourist Visa?
Real talk, before you start this whole process, ask yourself: do you actually need a visa?
If you're American (or from any of 57 eligible countries ), you might qualify for China's 240-hour visa-free transit policy. That's 10 full days, enough for most trips. It covers 60+ entry ports across China.
Visa-free makes sense when:
Your trip is under 10 days

You're transiting through China (flying in from one country, out to another)

You'd rather not deal with the application at all

You need the full visa when:
Staying longer than 10 days

Flying a direct round trip (no third country involved)

Planning multiple trips this year (10-year visa is better value)

Working, studying, or doing business in China

FAQ
How long to get a China visa — and when should I apply?
3-4 weeks before your trip. That gives you plenty of buffer for the 7-10 day total process. Don't apply more than 3 months out, single-entry visas expire 3 months from issuance date. Apply too early and you've wasted ¥1,000 ($140).
Can I get a China visa in one day?
Rush service exists (1-2 working days), but it's emergency-only, requires consular approval, costs an extra ¥215-285 ($30-40), and is not available for first-time applicants . If you've never had a Chinese visa, how long to get a Chinese visa comes down to one number: 4 working days minimum. Plan around that.
Does a 10-year visa take longer to process than single-entry?
Nope. Same processing time. Same fee (¥1,000 / $140 for US citizens). Same paperwork. There's zero reason to get a single-entry visa if you qualify for the 10-year — same price, and saves you from reapplying next time. See our breakdown of China visa types and which to apply for if you're unsure which category fits your trip.
What documents do I need for a Chinese visa in 2026?
Passport (valid 6+ months, 2 blank pages), completed COVA form (printed and signed), one 33mm x 48mm photo, and proof of US legal residence. That's the full list. Flight tickets, hotel bookings, and invitation letters are no longer required as of 2026.
How long is China visa processing time during peak season?
Official processing is still listed as 4 working days. But during Chinese New Year (January-February) and National Day (early October), the real wait can stretch to 5-7 working days. Start your application earlier if you're traveling during these windows.
What's the overstay penalty?
¥500 per day ($70/day) , up to a maximum of ¥10,000 ($1,400). Overstays can also mean detention and future visa refusals. If your plans change while in China, contact the local Public Security Bureau (PSB) to request an extension before your visa expires. Don't risk it.

One more thing: once your Chinese tourist visa is sorted, fill out the China Arrival Card before your flight. It's been required since November 2025. You can complete it through the NIA 12367 app or via WeChat/Alipay mini-program. Takes about 5 minutes. The US State Department China page has the latest entry requirements too.
