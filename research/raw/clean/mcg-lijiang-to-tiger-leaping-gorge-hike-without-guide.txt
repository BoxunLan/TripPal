Lijiang to Tiger Leaping Gorge Hike Without a Guide

Tiger Leaping Gorge is one of the best treks in China, and the good news is you don't need a guide for it. The classic High Trail is well-marked, thousands of independent hikers do it every year, and getting there from Lijiang is simple. Here's the honest, current rundown — transport, the two-day route, fees, guesthouses, and when to go.
Getting from Lijiang to the trailhead
The trek starts at Qiaotou (桥头), the town officially called Hutiaoxia. Buses leave from Lijiang's main bus station, about a 15-minute walk south of the Old Town (a taxi from the train station is around ¥15). Morning departures run roughly at 8:00, 8:30, 9:00 and 9:30 — in winter sometimes only the later one — and the ride is about 2 to 2.5 hours on twisty mountain road, for somewhere around ¥35 to ¥45 one way.
If the dedicated gorge minibus is full, the reliable fallback is any Lijiang–Shangri-La bus: they pass through and will drop you at Qiaotou, and they run at least hourly. (There's also a back route via Daju at the far end, served by minivans in about 3 hours, but that's for entering the gorge in reverse and depends on the sometimes-closed ferry — check locally before relying on it.)
Standard practice with luggage: forward your big bag from the Lijiang bus station to the far end of the trek — tell the driver when you board — and hike with just a daypack. Pickup is near Tina's at the bottom.

The High Trail, day by day
The route runs from Qiaotou at the west end to Tina's at the east, and splits naturally over two days:
Day 1: Qiaotou to Halfway Guesthouse. About 10 to 14km, 5 to 7 hours including lunch. You pass the Naxi Family Guesthouse early, then hit the famous 28 Bends (二十八道拐) — a steep switchback climb of 400 to 500 vertical metres that takes most people 1.5 to 2 hours and tops out around 2,670m. This is the hardest part of the whole trek; everything after is easier. Beyond it comes the Tea-Horse Guesthouse and then Halfway Guesthouse, the classic first-night stop, famous for its balcony view across to Jade Dragon Snow Mountain.

Day 2: Halfway down to Tina's. About 8 to 11km, 2 to 4 hours, mostly downhill and flat. From Tina's you can carry on a little further to Walnut Garden if you want a quieter night.

The trail is roughly a third stone steps, two-thirds dirt path, often narrow and single-file. It's a moderate hike — the 28 Bends are the only genuinely strenuous stretch, and the altitude (trail elevations around 1,800 to 2,670m) is low enough that altitude sickness usually isn't a worry.
Fees and tickets
The scenic-area entrance fee is around ¥45 to ¥65 — several 2026 reports cite ¥65, up from the long-standing ¥45, so carry a bit extra. Bring cash : the fee point and many guesthouses don't reliably take Alipay or WeChat Pay. The fee is usually collected at a checkpoint near Qiaotou, often boarded onto the bus. If you take the side path down into the Middle Gorge to the river (the ladders and the "Sky Ladder"), expect an extra ¥15 to ¥30 charged by the landowners on the way down.
Note the difference between the High Trail and the lower paved road: the trail is the two-day mountainside hike covered by the scenic ticket, while the paved riverside road leads to the Tiger Leaping Stone viewing platforms for day visitors arriving by car. They're separate experiences.
Where to sleep on the trail
The well-known guesthouses are all real and operating:
Naxi Family Guesthouse — near the start, a common lunch or first stop.

Tea-Horse Guesthouse — mid-section, just after the 28 Bends.

Halfway Guesthouse (Bendiwan) — the classic Day 1 overnight, with the famous balcony view. It books out fast in peak season, so reserve ahead.

Tina's — at the bottom where the trail meets the road; the transit hub where onward buses leave.

Sean's Spring Guesthouse — in Walnut Garden past Tina's, the gorge's original guesthouse, open year-round.

Off-peak you can usually just walk in, but in spring and autumn book Halfway ahead. Booking is inconsistent — some take Trip.com or Booking.com, others are phone or WeChat only.

When to go, and staying safe
The best windows are roughly late March or April through June, and September through November — clear skies and mild temperatures. Avoid July and August. The monsoon brings heavy rain, sharply higher landslide and rockfall risk, poor visibility, and the gorge is sometimes closed on rainy days; this is a place where monsoon-season accidents have happened. Rockfall is a year-round hazard that gets much worse in summer, so check conditions locally before you set out, whatever the month.
Do you really not need a guide?
For the High Trail, no — it's well-marked with painted arrow waymarks (red or orange) and travelled by thousands of independent hikers, with emergency markers along the way. A guide is only worth it for the steep Middle Gorge descent and Sky Ladder, or for the less-travelled side routes. The main two-day trek you can absolutely do on your own.
Do I need a guide to hike Tiger Leaping Gorge? No. The High Trail is well-marked and done independently by thousands of hikers every year. A guide is only useful for the optional Middle Gorge descent or side trails.
How do I get from Lijiang to the trailhead? Buses to Qiaotou leave from Lijiang's main bus station in the morning (around 8:00 to 9:30), about 2 to 2.5 hours and ¥35 to ¥45. Shangri-La-bound buses also drop at Qiaotou as a backup.
How hard is the trek? Moderate. The two-day High Trail is manageable for reasonably fit hikers; the only tough stretch is the 28 Bends, a steep climb topping out around 2,670m. Altitude sickness usually isn't a concern.
What does it cost to enter? Around ¥45 to ¥65 for the scenic area (some 2026 reports cite ¥65), plus ¥15 to ¥30 if you descend into the Middle Gorge. Bring cash.
When should I avoid going? July and August — the monsoon brings landslide and rockfall risk and occasional closures. Aim for spring (April to June) or autumn (September to November).
