April Xinjiang looks nothing like the photographs that made you want to go. The high pastures are brown with snow in the hollows, and the green that Nalati is famous for does not arrive until late May.
Come anyway, but come for what April actually has: Sayram Lake's winter ice breaking up and shoving onto the shore, apricot blossom in the valleys, bazaars at their calmest, and a road network you have largely to yourself. Three gates decide whether the trip works — one closed by snow, one by a daily vehicle quota, and one by which return route you pick.
Gate one: Duku is shut
The Duku Highway (独库公路), Dushanzi to Kucha over passes above 3,000 m, is closed every April and usually does not reopen until early to mid-June. Snow and avalanche risk on the high sections. The transport authority announces the date year by year, so there is no calendar you can plan against — but April is never it.
Any itinerary that returns south through Duku will end at a snow gate with a long reverse behind it. Plan the northern loop and treat Duku as a different trip.
Qiongkushitai (琼库什台) goes the same way. The approach from Yining in April is a rough gravel track through brown pasture, most village accommodation is still shut for the off-season, and it costs four hours each way for a landscape that only pays out in June.

Sayram Lake, which is the reason to come in April
At 2,073 m, Xinjiang's highest and largest alpine lake spends the winter frozen. From late March through April the ice breaks and the wind drives slabs of it up onto the shore in ice-shove ridges — 冰推 — that are gone by the time the summer wildflowers arrive. This is a window, not a consolation prize.
The shoreline loop is about 90 km. Drive it counterclockwise: in by the East Gate, out by the South. Speed limit 30–50 km/h with pull-offs every two or three kilometres.

Peak season runs 1 April to 31 October: ¥70 entry plus ¥75 for a self-drive vehicle, so about ¥145, valid 24 hours.

Be off the western shore before 16:00. Local drivers are firm about this — afternoon squalls of wind and snow, 白毛风, blow up regularly and the west side is the exposed one.

Gate two: Nalati's 300 cars a day
Nalati caps self-drive entry at 300 vehicles per day. Since May 2025 a slot has to be reserved through the 智游那拉提 WeChat mini-programme, bookable up to seven days ahead, with photos of the driving licence and the vehicle registration uploaded. One real-name WeChat account books one vehicle per day, and slots are released dynamically against demand, road and weather.
Here is the part nobody connects: if you are travelling with a hired driver, which as a foreigner you almost certainly are, that reservation is made against his licence and his vehicle registration, from his WeChat account. Raise it when you book the driver, a week out. Turning up and discovering he did not know is a bad morning.
Most people skip the self-drive question entirely and take the shuttles on the park's three internal routes. The maths supports it: entry ¥95 and shuttle ¥40, against ¥300 to take your own vehicle in, which locals consider poor value. You scan the electronic voucher from the mini-programme at the gate either way.
You cannot drive yourself
This catches people who have already booked a rental car. A foreign driving licence is not valid in China, and neither is an International Driving Permit — China is not party to the road-traffic conventions, so no IDP applies and no Chinese body issues one.
The legal routes are a full Chinese licence, or a temporary driving permit (临时驾驶许可) from the traffic police, valid for the length of your visa up to 90 days and used alongside your home licence. The permit itself is not the obstacle — some airports now issue one at an arrivals-hall counter in under an hour. Xinjiang is the obstacle: the rental has to be in your name, the loop is long, checkpoints are frequent and average-speed enforcement is unforgiving, which is why nearly every foreign visitor here hires a car with a driver instead.
Budget ¥500–900 a day for a driver, and settle in writing whether the vehicle and its fuel are inside that number or on top of it. Quotes vary on exactly that point. Fill up in Urumqi, Dushanzi, Yining and Nalati — stations are sparse between towns and absent on mountain sections, and rural pumps sometimes want extra registration from a foreign passport, which your driver handles.
Gate three: which way you come back
The northern return on the G30 through Dushanzi is the route in the table above. It crosses no designated sensitive zone and needs nothing beyond your tourist visa. Checkpoint stops happen and are routine.
The southern G0711 via Korla and Hejing looks shorter on a map. In practice it passes through counties where foreigners have repeatedly run into extra permit requirements enforced at police checkpoints, the rules shift month to month, and travellers report being turned around and sent back the way they came. Without permits arranged in advance through your operator and confirmed with the local PSB, do not take it.
Southern Xinjiang proper — Kashgar, the Pamirs, Tashkurgan — is a separate case with its own border permit, and that one is not optional. The Tashkurgan guide covers what it is and where to get it in half an hour rather than a week.
At the checkpoints
Every passenger gets out with their passport, walks through a scanner that reads the photo page, and answers where you came from and where you are going. Ten to fifteen minutes normally; up to forty-five at busy times.
Your phone may be looked at. Do not carry photographs or map screenshots of military installations or border zones, and never photograph a checkpoint. Keep the passport in a front pocket for the whole trip — you will produce it dozens of times.
One more enforcement quirk that surprises drivers: Xinjiang measures average speed between points, not just at cameras. A fine can arrive weeks later because your elapsed time between two checkpoints implies a speed you never actually held. Build buffer rather than trying to make up a delay.
Beds that can register you
Not every hotel is licensed to take foreign guests, and in county towns that can mean one option in the whole place. Urumqi, Yining and the Nalati tourist area are fine — Hanting, Jinjiang Inn, Vienna and the four- and five-star internationals handle foreign check-in routinely.
Filter for 接待外宾 on Trip.com or Ctrip, then message the property on WeChat before booking and confirm they will register your passport with the local police. The registration itself happens at the desk on arrival.
What April weather actually does
It swings, and it swings by altitude rather than by date.

So: layers, with a windproof shell over a fleece or light down. The shore of Sayram Lake is exposed and the wind there is the deciding factor, not the air temperature.
April into early summer is also Xinjiang's windiest and dustiest stretch. Blowing dust cuts visibility some days. Check the forecast the night before each leg and keep the buffer in the schedule.
You will also be offline more than you expect. VPNs are unreliable to the point of useless inside Xinjiang. Download the offline Amap regions before leaving Urumqi, use WeChat for everything local, buy a China Mobile or China Unicom prepaid SIM on arrival — buying one as a foreigner is straightforward and coverage along the northern route is good — and tell people at home you may be unreachable in stretches. Options for getting online in China compares the approaches, though assume the Xinjiang leg is the weakest case for all of them.

Keep conversation off politics, religion and ethnic policy, with strangers and with your driver. The food and the hospitality are what you came for.
Rules here shift with little notice. Confirm the current position with your operator the week before, and again in Urumqi before you start driving.
If the driving is the part you would rather skip, the Silk Road works by rail as well — the K497 from Lanzhou to Dunhuang covers the eastern approach to the same region without a steering wheel.
FAQ
Is April a good time for a Xinjiang road trip?
It is good for apricot blossom, empty roads and the ice break-up at Sayram Lake. It is wrong for green grassland, which arrives in late May, and for the Duku Highway, which stays shut until early or mid-June.
Can I drive myself in Xinjiang on a foreign licence?
No, and an International Driving Permit does not help — China does not recognise them. You would need a Chinese licence or a temporary driving permit from the traffic police. Almost every foreign visitor hires a car with a driver at ¥500–900 a day.
Do I need a permit for this route?
Not for the northern loop on the G30 — a standard tourist visa covers it. The southern return via Korla and Hejing is where foreigners hit extra permit requirements, and southern Xinjiang around Kashgar and the Pamirs needs a separate border permit.
How do I get into Nalati?
Self-drive entry is capped at 300 vehicles a day and has to be reserved through the 智游那拉提 WeChat mini-programme up to seven days ahead, against the driver's licence and vehicle registration. Most people take the shuttles instead: ¥95 entry plus ¥40, against ¥300 to bring a car in.
