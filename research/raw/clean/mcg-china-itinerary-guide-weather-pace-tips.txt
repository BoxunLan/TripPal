One city per week of trip length, then subtract one. Seven days is one region. Fourteen days is three cities, not six. Twenty-one days is three regions.
That rule exists because of a number most itineraries ignore: the door-to-door time, not the one printed on the ticket. Beijing to Shanghai is 4h28m on the train and six and a half hours of your day. Every city change costs half a day to packing, moving and re-settling, before you have seen anything.

The rule that falls out of this: rail wins under about four and a half hours of track time, because stations sit in city centres and you arrive 30 minutes early rather than 90. Past that, fly.
A flight advertised as 1h50m is five hours door to door once you add the airport runs, security and the delay that Chinese domestic aviation reliably produces.

How long each city needs
No major Chinese city deserves less than two full nights, and Beijing, Shanghai, Xi'an and Chengdu each want three days minimum — which means four nights, because your arrival day is not a day.
Trip length

Realistic shape

7 days

One region. Shanghai + Suzhou + Hangzhou, or Beijing + the Great Wall + one town. Not Beijing and Shanghai both.

10 days

Two cities with day trips. Shanghai 4 nights, Beijing 5, fly home.

14 days

Three cities plus one deep dive. Shanghai 3 → Hangzhou 2 → Xi'an 3 → Beijing 4.

21 days

Three regions. North (Beijing, Xi'an), centre (Chengdu, Jiuzhaigou), south (Guilin, Shanghai, Hong Kong).

Adding a fifth city to a fourteen-day trip does not add a city. It removes a day from each of the other four and adds an airport. A worked two-week route shows where the days actually go.
Pick the season, then the cities
China is too large for a single best time. The season decides the region, not the other way round.

Golden Week, October 1–7.

Below the Yangtze: Yunnan at 10–18°C, Guangxi, Hong Kong, Chengdu. Above it: Harbin Ice Festival, Jilin rime frost.

Beijing and Shanghai in grey −5°C wind. Chinese New Year.

Two regional caveats English guides tend to skip. In the north, spring is shorter and dustier than the calendar suggests. And autumn in Beijing is genuinely the best two months in the country for walking, which is why everyone else is also there. Month by month across China goes deeper.
The two weeks not to come
Window

2026 dates

What happens

Chinese New Year

Transport sells out, small businesses close, prices climb

Labour Day

1–5 May

Domestic surge; Shanghai and Hangzhou full

National Day, Golden Week

1–7 October

Around 800 million domestic trips in one week. The Great Wall becomes a queue.

If your dates overlap one of these, either shift the arrival or go deliberately contrarian — Xinjiang, rural Yunnan, the southern coast — where domestic crowds are thinner.
Before you fix the dates
Confirm how you are entering, because it caps everything. Several nationalities now have 30-day visa-free access, while the 240-hour transit gives ten days and requires an onward ticket to a third country. A fourteen-day itinerary does not fit inside a ten-day policy, and that is the most common planning error after over-packing the route.
Then book the rail. Beijing–Shanghai, Shanghai–Hangzhou and Beijing–Xi'an sell out two to three days ahead at weekends and in holiday periods, so book five to seven days out. Trip.com charges about ¥20 and handles passport verification; registering on 12306 yourself is free but takes about a week to verify, so do it before you leave rather than the night before a train.

FAQ
How many cities should I visit in 14 days?
Three, or four at the outside. Shanghai 3 nights, Hangzhou 2, Xi'an 3, Beijing 4, plus a travel day home. A fifth city does not add a city — it subtracts a day from the other four.
Is high-speed rail better than flying in China?
Under about four and a half hours of track time, yes. Stations are central and you arrive 30 minutes before rather than 90. Past that the flight wins even after airport transfers.
When is the worst time to visit China?
Chinese New Year, 17–23 February in 2026 with disruption from the 13th to the 28th, and Golden Week, 1–7 October, when roughly 800 million domestic trips happen in a single week.
How far ahead should I book high-speed rail?
Five to seven days for the major routes, which sell out two to three days ahead at weekends and during holidays. Tickets release 15 days before departure.
