Hong Kong is one of the best airport layovers in Asia. The Airport Express puts you in Kowloon in 21 minutes for HK$115, immigration at HKG usually moves quickly, and the city itself is small enough to cover one attraction plus a meal on a 6-hour stop. The question is whether you leave the airport at all — and that depends on your layover length, your airline's baggage policy, and the weather on arrival.
This guide sorts a Hong Kong layover by length, from a few hours airside to an overnight or a one-to-two-day stop, and covers hotels and the weather; if you have about eight hours, the eight-hour layover plan counts that clock stage by stage.

Can I leave the airport during my layover?
Usually yes, with two caveats.
First, your bags. If your boarding pass shows both flights and the ground crew tells you your luggage is checked through to the final destination, you are fine to walk out with just a daypack. If you are re-checking bags, you will need to collect, exit, and re-enter airside — this eats at least 45 minutes.
Second, your visa. Hong Kong grants visa-free entry to most Western passports (US, UK, EU, Australia, Canada, Japan) for 90 days, or 180 days for British citizens. Mainland China visa rules do not apply here — Hong Kong has its own immigration. If your passport needs a Hong Kong visa, you will not be able to leave the airport.
Immigration at HKG is usually 15 to 30 minutes on arrival, though peak evening flights can push it past 45 minutes. Budget 60 minutes to clear inbound and another 45 minutes to clear security and immigration on departure.
Airport to city: the three options that matter
Airport Express — fastest and most predictable
The Airport Express costs HK$120 one way to Hong Kong Station and HK$105 to Kowloon with an Octopus or contactless bank card (HK$130 and HK$115 on a paper ticket), and the return is free if you come back the same day on the same card. A round-trip ticket valid for 30 days is HK$215 to Hong Kong Station. It runs every 10 minutes from around 5:54 AM to 00:48. Stops are Airport, AsiaWorld-Expo, Tsing Yi, Kowloon, and Hong Kong Station.
Airport to Kowloon: 21 minutes

Airport to Hong Kong Station (Central): 24 minutes

A short list of hotels runs its own shuttle buses from Hong Kong, Kowloon and Tsing Yi stations; the MTR website names them

If two or more people travel together, buy a group ticket at the Airport Express customer service centre: to Hong Kong Station it is HK$170 for two adults, HK$230 for three and HK$280 for four. That drops the cost to HK$85 per person or less.
Bus — cheapest option
The A-series CityBus routes run from the airport to every major district. Useful ones:
A21 — Airport to Tsim Sha Tsui and Hung Hom, HK$34.6, about 84 minutes end to end

A11 — Airport to Causeway Bay and North Point, HK$41.9, about 94 minutes end to end

A12 — Airport to Siu Sai Wan on Hong Kong Island, HK$47.1

N21 — the overnight route to Tsim Sha Tsui (Star Ferry), HK$23.8, with departures from 00:20 to 04:40

Pay with Octopus or exact cash. Buses have luggage racks. Tsim Sha Tsui is near the end of the A21 route, and peak-hour traffic can make the ride longer.
Taxi — fastest door-to-door, highest cost
Urban (red) taxis from HKG cost about HK$290 to Tsim Sha Tsui, HK$375 to Central and HK$370 to Causeway Bay, tolls included, plus HK$6 for each larger piece of luggage. Taxi ranks are outside arrivals — ignore any tout offering a "fixed fare."

Where to stay, based on layover length
Under 10 hours — stay at the airport
If your layover is under 10 hours, the math rarely works for a city hotel. By the time you exit, ride the Airport Express, check in, sleep for 4 hours, and return, you have maybe 2 hours of actual usable rest.
The Regal Airport Hotel and Hong Kong SkyCity Marriott are both walkable from Terminal 1. Rooms run HK$1,200 to HK$2,200. If you just need a nap, the airport has paid lounges and rest areas near Gate 60 and Gate 40 for HK$200 to HK$600 depending on the facility.
10 to 24 hours — Tsim Sha Tsui makes sense
This is the sweet spot for a 1-night city stay. Tsim Sha Tsui is a short taxi ride from the Airport Express at Kowloon Station, five minutes from the waterfront, and next to dozens of cha chaan teng and dim sum spots. Budget guesthouses along Nathan Road run HK$500 to HK$800, mid-range 3-4 star hotels HK$900 to HK$1,500.
24+ hours — treat it as a mini-trip
With a full day plus a night, you can hit Victoria Peak, do a Lantau Island cable car run, or take a ferry to Cheung Chau. Stay in Tsim Sha Tsui or Central. See our 5-day Hong Kong itinerary for a fuller plan.

What to do by layover length
Under 6 hours — stay airside
HKG itself has enough to kill 4-5 hours. Free shower facilities exist near Gate 60 (arrive early, they fill up). The 4D Aviation Experience center is paid but fun. Food-wise, you can get legitimate Hong Kong-style milk tea and pineapple buns in the airside concourse for HK$40 to HK$70 — cheaper than hotel bars, still tasty. Do not risk a city run with under 6 hours; one immigration delay and you miss your flight.
6 to 12 hours — one-attraction city dash
Six to twelve hours is enough for one sight and a meal in the city, such as Victoria Peak or a Star Ferry crossing, as long as you are back at HKG two hours before departure. The eight-hour layover plan works through that clock stage by stage, with the free in-town check-in and the free layover tour.
12 to 24 hours — pick two attractions plus a meal
Now you can do Peak + Star Ferry + dinner, or Lantau Big Buddha (Tung Chung MTR) if you start early. Sleep in Tsim Sha Tsui. Return to airport with 2 hours of buffer.
June weather and what to pack
June in Hong Kong is hot, humid, and wet. Daytime highs run 28 to 32°C (82 to 90°F), with humidity above 80% and occasional heat advisories. The summer monsoon brings short, intense afternoon thunderstorms — expect at least one downpour per day, rarely lasting more than an hour.
Typhoon season
June officially kicks off typhoon season. The Hong Kong Observatory issues warning signals from T1 (standby) to T10 (hurricane). If T8 or above is raised, almost everything closes: the MTR may partially suspend, ferries stop, airlines cancel or delay flights. Check the observatory website (hko.gov.hk) during your layover, and always buy layover travel insurance in typhoon season.

Practical tips
Octopus card
Even for a 12-hour layover, get an Octopus card on arrival. The Airport Express customer service centre in arrivals sells the Tourist Octopus for HK$170, of which HK$128 is usable value and HK$42 a non-refundable card cost; the standard Octopus is sold at other MTR customer service centres but not at Airport Station. Pay by tap on the MTR, buses, trams, Star Ferry, and 7-Eleven. Alternatively, Apple Pay Octopus works on the same system without the physical card.
Connectivity
HKG has free Wi-Fi. If you need a SIM, 7-Eleven at arrivals sells 5-8 day unlimited 4G/5G SIMs for HK$80 to HK$150. Portable Wi-Fi rentals at the airport counters run HK$40 to HK$80 per day.
Power
Type G (UK 3-pin) plugs. US/EU/JP travelers will need an adapter.
Currency
Have HK$200 to HK$500 cash for small vendors and wet market food. Most places take Octopus, Visa, and Mastercard. Alipay HK and WeChat Pay HK work but are not necessary.
What is the minimum safe layover for leaving Hong Kong airport?
Six hours is the practical minimum if your bags are checked through. Budget 60 minutes for inbound immigration, 30 to 40 minutes each way on the Airport Express (including buffer), and 90 to 120 minutes for outbound security and immigration. That leaves roughly 90 minutes of actual city time for a 6-hour layover.
Can I get a free Hong Kong city tour during my layover?
Yes, if your connection is more than seven hours and you meet Hong Kong's entry requirements: Hong Kong International Airport runs free guided city tours for transit passengers. The eight-hour layover plan lists the three daily routes and how to book.
Is the Airport Express worth it over the cheaper A21 bus?
If you have a tight layover, yes, because the Airport Express runs on a fixed 10-minute headway and is not affected by road traffic. If you have 10+ hours and a flexible schedule, the A21 at HK$34.6 saves you about HK$70 against the HK$105 Octopus fare to Kowloon.
Do I need a separate Hong Kong visa if I have a China visa?
Usually not. Hong Kong runs its own immigration, so a mainland China visa does not cover it, but US, Canadian, Australian, Japanese and EU citizens get 90 days visa-free and British citizens 180 days. A Hong Kong stamp does not cover the mainland either.
What happens if a typhoon hits during my layover?
If the Hong Kong Observatory raises a T8 or above, the MTR and public transit partially or fully suspend, and most flights are canceled or delayed. Stay at your hotel or the airport, monitor the observatory website, and contact your onward airline for rebooking. Travel insurance that covers weather disruption is worth it in June through October.
