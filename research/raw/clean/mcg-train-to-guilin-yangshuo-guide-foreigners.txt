Guangzhou is the easiest place to start a train trip to Guilin: dozens of direct trains a day, most taking 2 to 3 hours. From Shenzhen North and Hong Kong West Kowloon the ride takes about 2.5 to 3.5 hours, from Chengdu 5 to 7.5 hours, and from Beijing and Shanghai, which have only one or two daytime high-speed trains each, 7 to 9.5 hours.
Some trains from Guangzhou, Shenzhen and Chengdu also stop at Yangshuo Station. It only saves time if you are staying in Yangshuo or Xingping: the station is 33 km by road from Yangshuo town, and the shuttle bus into town takes about an hour.
Times and fares in this guide come from 12306, China Railway's booking system, for travel on 28 September 2026, searched on 27 September 2026.

A few things the table doesn't show:
Guangzhou: most trains leave from Guangzhou South, and a few from Guangzhou, Guangzhou East or Guangzhou Baiyun. Departures ran from about 6 a.m. to 9 p.m. A few slower trains took 4.5 to 5.5 hours, so check the duration before you pay.

Hong Kong: on the date checked, the trains left West Kowloon between 10:02 and 11:52, and the return trains left Guilin West between 12:41 and 15:43. None of them stopped at Yangshuo.

Beijing and Shanghai: the overnight sleeper from Beijing West (D919 on the date checked) left at 20:27 and reached Guilin North at 08:00. Sleeper trains from Shanghai South left around midday and arrived around 4 a.m. Conventional trains from either city take about 19 hours or more.

Chengdu: the overnight sleeper trains left Chengdu East around 7 p.m. and reached Guilin West around 6 a.m.

Which Guilin station: Guilin, Guilin North or Guilin West
Guilin has three passenger stations, and your ticket names one of them. Show a driver the Chinese name; 12306's English site lists the stations in pinyin.

Some trains stop at both Guilin North and Guilin, so pick whichever is nearer your hotel. Guilin North and Guilin West are both on the northern edge of the city. From either one, a taxi or DiDi is the simplest way into the center; city buses also run to Guilin Station, for example route 100 from Guilin North and route 22 from Guilin West.
Getting from Guilin to Yangshuo
If your train stops in Guilin and you are staying in Yangshuo, you have two options:
Take another train. Trains to Yangshuo Station run throughout the day and take 20 to 25 minutes from Guilin North or Guilin West and 35 to 50 minutes from Guilin Station, for ¥26–40 in second class. You then need the shuttle bus described below.

Go by road. Yangshuo town is 60 to 100 km from the three Guilin stations, about 1.5 hours by car. Yangshuo Bus Station runs dedicated buses to Guilin North and to the airport. For a car with a driver, see our guide to hiring a private driver in Guilin .

From Yangshuo Station to Yangshuo town
Yangshuo Station opened in January 2016 on the Guiyang–Guangzhou high-speed line. It sits east of Xingping, the Li River town whose stretch of river appears on the back of the 1999-series ¥20 note, not in Yangshuo town. By road it is about 34 km to West Street in Yangshuo and 8 km to Xingping. The shuttle bus stop is next to the station exit.

From the Fengming stop, the last 3 km to West Street is a short taxi or DiDi ride. Local bus 801 (¥2) also passes West Street, but Amap lists only a few departures a day.
Going back, the shuttle leaves from Fengming Visitor Center and calls at Xingping on the way; Amap lists departures from 07:00 to 18:30. The ride takes about an hour, and you still need to clear security at the station, so leave West Street at least 1.5 hours before your train, or take a taxi after the last bus.
Late arrivals need a plan. On the date checked, the last Guangzhou train reached Yangshuo at 22:18, after the last shuttle Amap lists. If you arrive after about 9 p.m., book a pickup or plan on a taxi.

When to get off at Yangshuo Station instead of Guilin
Direct trains to Yangshuo Station run from only some cities. For 28 September 2026, 12306 showed:
Guangzhou: dozens of trains a day, 1 h 50 min to 3 h 06 min, ¥137–191 in second class

Shenzhen North: a few trains a day, 2 h 38 min to 3 h 05 min, ¥245.5–265.5

Chengdu East: a few trains a day, 5 h 51 min to 7 h 35 min, ¥443–502

Hong Kong, Beijing and Shanghai: none. Change trains in Guilin; from Hong Kong you can also change at Shenzhen North or Guangzhou South.

Yangshuo Station makes sense when
you are going straight to Yangshuo town or Xingping and have a direct train from Guangzhou, Shenzhen or Chengdu;

you want to start with Xingping and the Li River around it, 8 km from the station;

your train arrives while the shuttle buses are running.

From Guangzhou South, the train to Yangshuo Station takes about 2 hours and the shuttle about 1 hour. Going through Guilin means 2 to 3 hours on the train and then about 1.5 hours by road.
Get off in Guilin when
you are coming from Hong Kong, Beijing or Shanghai, where no direct trains stop at Yangshuo;

you want time in Guilin itself, or plan to take the Li River cruise from the Guilin end. Our Guilin and Yangshuo travel guide compares the cruise options;

you arrive late in the evening;

you want more trains to choose from: far more trains run from Guangzhou and Shenzhen North to Guilin than to Yangshuo Station.

Buying tickets with a foreign passport
Every train in this guide is sold on 12306, China Railway's official website and app, which has an English version and accepts foreign passports. You verify your identity before the first purchase: 12306 checks the details you enter automatically, or you upload a photo of your passport's information page for a manual check, or you show your passport at a station ticket window. You can pay with a Chinese or foreign bank card or a mobile payment app.
Tickets go on sale 15 days ahead, counting the day of sale; on 27 September 2026, 12306 was selling up to 11 October. Search by station name in pinyin (Guilin, Guilinbei, Guilinxi or Yangshuo) and check which station the train uses before you pay.
Agencies such as Trip.com sell the same seats with an English interface and may add a service fee. Our guide to buying China high-speed rail tickets compares the options, and the 12306 guide for foreigners walks through account setup and passport verification.
At a station ticket window, you can buy with your passport and pay by bank card, cash or mobile payment. China's official guidance for foreign visitors lists the self-service ticket machines only for permanent residence ID cards, so use a window if you are buying with a passport.
From Hong Kong, MTR lists 12306, designated ticket agents and the West Kowloon ticket counters as the places to buy, and its destination list includes Guilin West (Guilinxi).
At the station: security, gates and boarding
Bring the original passport you booked with. The e-ticket is linked to it, and a screenshot or printed itinerary is not a ticket.

Expect a security check at the entrance. Ticket gates are automatic or staffed; if an automatic gate doesn't accept your passport, use the staffed lane.

Ticket checking closes shortly before departure, at a time each station sets, so allow for queues at security.

Leaving from Hong Kong West Kowloon
MTR says you can start the real-name check, security, immigration and boarding procedures up to 90 minutes before departure. The boarding gates open 15 minutes before departure and close 5 minutes before it. You clear Hong Kong departure and mainland entry inside West Kowloon, so you need a mainland visa or visa-free eligibility before you board. Our Hong Kong high-speed rail guide explains the process step by step.
