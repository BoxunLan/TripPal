Your flight from Frankfurt lands at Shanghai Pudong International Airport at 22:40. By the time you clear biometric immigration, grab your bag from the carousel and walk out the customs exit, it is 23:30 and the China Unicom kiosk has a metal shutter pulled down to the floor. The cleaning staff tells you, in Mandarin, to come back at 08:00. You have a hotel transfer waiting and no way to share your location.
This is one of the most common first-day problems for travelers to China, and it has better answers in 2026 than it did two years ago. The PVG telecom kiosks still mostly close around 21:30 to 22:00, but three new workarounds have emerged: 24-hour convenience store SIMs, eSIM download over airport WiFi, and the Maglev-to-downtown sprint for next-morning setup.

The Real PVG Telecom Kiosk Hours in 2026
Pudong International has two terminals. International arrivals mostly come through Terminal 2 (T2), with a growing share through the new Satellite Terminal S2 that connects to T2 by an automated people mover. Forget whatever blog post you read from 2019 about 24-hour SIM counters. Those never reopened after 2020.

These hours are the posted sign hours. In practice, staff often close 15 to 30 minutes early on low-traffic nights. If your flight lands after 21:00 and you are in a back row, assume the kiosk will be shut by the time you walk out.
Terminal 1
T1 has one China Mobile desk, and it closes earliest — often 20:30 in winter. Very few international flights arrive at T1.
What the closed kiosks still have
A sticker with a WeChat QR code and a Chinese-only phone number. Neither helps you at 23:30 with no connectivity.
Option 1: 24-Hour Convenience Store Tourist SIMs
This is the most reliable late-night fallback in 2026. FamilyMart (全家) and 7-Eleven both stock prepaid tourist SIMs on the counter behind the cashier, priced ¥120-180 for 7 to 15 days with 10-30 GB of data.
FamilyMart at PVG
T2 Arrivals Level 1 , near Exit 3, opposite the taxi queue — open 24 hours

T2 Departures Level 3 , inside security (not useful on arrival) — 24 hours

S2 Satellite Terminal — 24 hours at the central plaza

Ask for "旅游 SIM 卡" (lǚyóu SIM kǎ, tourist SIM card). Hand over your passport. The cashier scans the SIM QR, you pay ¥130-180 with cash, Alipay Tour Pass, Apple Pay or a foreign Visa/Mastercard (some stores still reject foreign cards — carry ¥200 cash as backup). Activation is manual: pop the SIM into your phone, insert the included pin to trigger a text, and follow the English SMS instructions.
7-Eleven at PVG
T2 Arrivals has one 7-Eleven near Exit 6, open 24 hours. Stock varies; sometimes only China Unicom SIMs, sometimes dual Unicom/Mobile. Slightly cheaper than FamilyMart, roughly ¥120-150 for the same plan.
What to expect from the tourist SIM
Voice and SMS work immediately

Data typically activates within 10-30 minutes

Coverage uses the host carrier's network (China Unicom is best for tourists in Shanghai, China Mobile best elsewhere)

No VPN included — if you need Google, Instagram, Gmail or WhatsApp, bring your own VPN or rely on an eSIM that routes via overseas servers

Option 2: eSIM Activation Over PVG Free WiFi
If your phone supports eSIM (iPhone XS and newer, Pixel 4 and newer, most 2021+ Samsung Galaxy models), you can skip the kiosk entirely. Do this before you leave the airport, and ideally before you clear immigration.
The 2-hour PVG free WiFi trick
PVG offers free airport WiFi for 2 hours per device. To activate, you normally need to scan your passport at one of the self-service WiFi kiosks near the immigration queue. In 2026, the kiosks are plentiful and the process takes under 90 seconds.
Walk to a "Free WiFi" self-service kiosk (yellow and blue signage)

Place your passport on the scanner, photo page down

Read the printed slip — it contains a WiFi username and 2-hour password

Connect to "AIRPORT-FREE-WIFI-PVG" SSID

You now have 120 minutes of connectivity on one device

Your hotel, your Airbnb host, Google, and your favorite eSIM provider are all reachable.

Option 3: Maglev to Downtown Shanghai, Next Morning
If it is already past midnight and the convenience store shelves are empty, the cleanest path is to accept one night without mobile data and head to downtown Shanghai.
Maglev (磁浮列车)
Operates PVG Station to Longyang Road (龙阳路) 06:45 - 21:40. At 23:30 it is closed — use Metro Line 2 instead.
Metro Line 2
Line 2 runs from Pudong International Airport to East Nanjing Road (南京东路) until roughly 22:00. After that, take a DiDi (book via Trip.com or ask airport staff to hail one from the official taxi queue). Budget ¥170-220 to People's Square.
Downtown flagship stores
China Mobile Nanjing Road flagship (南京东路) — opens 08:30, extensive English support, sells full 30-day plans with 50 GB data for ¥199-299

China Unicom People's Square — opens 09:00, smallest queue in the morning

China Telecom Huaihai Road — opens 09:00, best fiber-speed plans if you also want home internet for a long stay

Bring your passport and expect the whole process to take 20-35 minutes, most of it form filling.
A Realistic Late-Night Decision Tree
Flight lands 19:00 - 21:00 — walk to any kiosk in T2 Arrivals, you will make it before they close

Flight lands 21:00 - 22:00 — go directly to the China Unicom kiosk near Exit 5, skip baggage photos. You have 20 minutes

Flight lands 22:00 - midnight — head straight to FamilyMart near Exit 3 for a tourist SIM, or the 7-Eleven near Exit 6

Flight lands after midnight — use PVG free WiFi to download an Airalo eSIM, then transfer to your hotel. Buy a long-term SIM downtown tomorrow

Your phone does not support eSIM — skip to FamilyMart first, then fallback to next-day downtown China Mobile

What About Roaming With Your Home Carrier?
Your T-Mobile, Vodafone, Orange or Telstra plan technically works in Shanghai, but there are two big problems. Speed is throttled to 128 kbps or 256 kbps on most "free international" tiers, which is barely enough for WeChat text, and the routing goes through your home country — which means Google and Instagram work without a VPN. For a one-hour airport taxi ride to your hotel, this is fine as a bridge. For a multi-day trip, buy a local SIM or eSIM.
Registration and Legal Requirements
Every SIM sold in China is real-name registered. The kiosk or convenience store scans your passport, takes a brief photo of your face, and links the number to your identity. This is legally mandatory and takes 90 seconds. You cannot buy a SIM with a friend's passport or without ID. eSIMs bought abroad skip this step because the regulation applies to Chinese carriers, not to overseas reseller SIMs roaming into China.
What to Expect Over the First 24 Hours
Your first day in Shanghai is a dance between getting a working SIM, getting to your hotel, and getting enough sleep to function. A realistic timeline for a traveler landing at 23:00:
23:30 — clear customs, walk through the exit doors into Arrivals Level 1

23:35 — head straight to the nearest FamilyMart or 7-Eleven, buy a tourist SIM, pay with cash

23:45 — activate the SIM by inserting and following the English SMS prompt

00:00 — book a DiDi or walk to the official taxi queue near Exit 7

00:45 — arrive at your hotel, check in, collapse

If the convenience stores are sold out, fall back to the eSIM flow over airport WiFi. If eSIMs are not supported on your phone, grab a free 2-hour WiFi session, message your hotel through WeChat or WhatsApp that you are en route, and head downtown. A single night without connectivity is not a disaster — you will sort the SIM first thing in the morning at Nanjing Road or at the hotel's business center.
The Taxi and DiDi Reality at PVG
Even with a working SIM in hand, the taxi queue at PVG can take 30-60 minutes at peak arrival hours. The queue is supervised, meters are enforced, and the fare to downtown Puxi runs ¥180-240 plus a ¥25 tunnel toll. Negotiated flat-fare offers from drivers standing in the arrivals hall are usually scams — always join the official queue outside Exit 1, 5 or 7.
DiDi is the better default once your eSIM or tourist SIM is working. The DiDi Chinese app has a full English mode in 2026. Open the app, set destination, pick Express (快车) or Premier (专车). Fares are typically ¥170-210 to central Shanghai, and pickup happens at a marked DiDi lane at Arrivals Exit 6. Payment goes through the card you linked, or through Alipay Tour Pass.

Can I buy a Chinese SIM card before I fly?
Yes, from Amazon, Trip.com and Klook. Orders include pre-activated China Unicom tourist SIMs that arrive in 3-5 days. Good if you want zero stress on arrival. Expect to pay $20-35 for a 7-15 day plan.
Does Airalo work everywhere in China?
Airalo's Changcheng Mobile plan works across all major Chinese cities. Rural western regions (parts of Xinjiang, Tibet, remote Sichuan) can be patchy. Upload speeds are lower than a physical local SIM.
Is PVG WiFi really free without a Chinese phone number?
Yes, the passport-scan kiosks are specifically for international travelers and do not require a Chinese mobile number. It is one of the more traveler-friendly features at PVG.
Which carrier has the best coverage in Shanghai?
China Unicom gives the best speeds on the metro and in downtown Pudong. China Mobile has the widest suburban and intercity coverage. For a Shanghai-only trip, Unicom wins.
Can I get a monthly contract as a tourist?
No. Tourist SIMs are prepaid only, 7 to 30 days. For longer stays, apply at a flagship store with your passport and a Chinese friend as guarantor, or use a business visa to access contract plans.
