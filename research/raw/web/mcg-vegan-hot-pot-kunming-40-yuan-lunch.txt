The ¥40 Vegan Hot Pot Lunch I Eat Every Day in Kunming

Sarah Liu · Cultural Guide, Food Explorer · Updated September 14, 2026

You've probably seen "¥40 vegan hot pot in Kunming" floating around and wondered if it's real. Here's the honest answer: yes, you can eat vegan, and eat well, in Kunming on a ¥40 lunch budget — but the famous Yunnan wild-mushroom hot pot isn't quite that cheap, and it isn't vegan by default. Once you understand both of those things, getting a great plant-based hot pot meal here is easy. This is how to actually do it.
The mushroom hot pot reality check
Yunnan's signature dish is wild-mushroom hot pot (野生菌火锅), and it's worth your time. But two things trip up vegetarians:
The default broth is not vegetarian. Traditional Yunnan mushroom hot pot is simmered on a chicken (often black-bone silkie chicken) or pork-bone stock. The mushrooms are the star, but the soup underneath usually isn't meat-free.

A proper one isn't a ¥40 meal. Wild mushrooms are seasonal and pricey, and a sit-down mushroom hot pot at a real restaurant typically runs ¥80 or more per person. ¥40 won't cover the full experience — at most a small solo mushroom-soup portion.

So if your heart is set on the famous wild-mushroom pot, budget more. If your goal is genuinely good vegan hot pot for around ¥40, you have two reliable routes.

Route 1: build your own veggie pot
Hot pot is self-cooked and ordered by ingredient, which actually works in a vegetarian's favour — you choose a broth, then add raw items yourself. To make it vegan:
Ask for a vegetarian broth — 素汤底 (sù tāng dǐ) — or a divided pot, 鸳鸯锅 (yuānyāng guō) , so your veggie side stays separate from any meat your group orders.

Pick a tomato (番茄) or mushroom (菌汤) base , then load up on mushrooms, tofu, and vegetables.

One honest caveat: at a regular (non-vegetarian) restaurant, even a "vegetable" broth may have been started from a meat master stock, and dishes can be blanched in it. If you're strictly vegan rather than flexible, Route 2 is the safer bet.
Route 2: Buddhist vegetarian restaurants (where ¥40 shines)
This is where your budget goes furthest. Kunming has an established cluster of Buddhist-style vegetarian restaurants, strongest around Yuantong Temple (圆通寺) on Yuantong Street , with a second pocket near Green Lake (翠湖) . Look for signs reading 素食 (sùshí) or 素斋 (sùzhāi) . Because the entire kitchen is meat-free, you don't have to interrogate every dish — and the mock-meat cooking (tofu and gluten "chicken" or "pork") is genuinely good.
Many of these places run an all-you-can-eat buffet for roughly ¥20 to ¥25, which makes ¥40 comfortable rather than tight. So the real shape of the "¥40 vegan lunch" is this: it's a generous budget for a Buddhist vegetarian buffet or a couple of veggie dishes with rice, and a stretch for hot pot specifically. Set your expectations there and you won't be disappointed.
Ordering without Mandarin
A few phrases carry you a long way:
我吃素 (wǒ chī sù) — I'm vegetarian

素 (sù) — the key character to spot on menus and signs

不要肉 (bù yào ròu) — no meat

素汤底 (sù tāng dǐ) — vegetarian broth

菌菇 (jūn gū) / 豆腐 (dòufu) — mushrooms / tofu

不要鸡精 / 不要蚝油 (bù yào jī jīng / bù yào háo yóu) — no chicken bouillon / no oyster sauce

我是佛教徒 (wǒ shì fójiàotú) — I'm a Buddhist (a well-understood way to signal strict vegetarian)

The real trap in China isn't visible meat — it's the invisible kind. Lard as cooking oil, chicken bouillon powder, oyster sauce in a dipping sauce, a master stock under a "veggie" broth. Saying you're Buddhist tends to get the message across more reliably than "no meat." Our guides to reading a Chinese restaurant menu and ordering food without Chinese go deeper on the mechanics.
The wild-mushroom safety rule you must not skip
If you do go for mushrooms, this matters more than anything else in this guide. Yunnan's wild mushroom season runs roughly May to October, peaking June to September with the summer rains — and it brings a wave of poisoning cases every year. The province has over 200 toxic mushroom species, and a handful are deadly.
The rules:
Cook them thoroughly. Reputable mushroom hot pot restaurants enforce this — staff often set a timer and make the pot boil for around 20 minutes after wild mushrooms go in before anyone eats. Never eat them rare or early. One local mushroom, 见手青 (jiàn shǒu qīng), is famous for causing vivid "little people" hallucinations when undercooked, and it's a viral meme for a reason.

But cooking is not a magic shield. Thorough boiling neutralises some toxins, not all — the deadliest species (the Amanita family) stay lethal even after cooking. The real protection is letting a reputable, licensed restaurant source and prepare known-safe mushrooms. Don't forage, and don't buy wild mushrooms to cook yourself.

Finding the right spot
Two tools cover it. HappyCow is the go-to English app for vegan and vegetarian listings, though coverage in a mid-tier city like Kunming is thinner than in Beijing or Shanghai, and some listings go stale — check before you go. Dianping (大众点评) , China's main review app, has far more complete local coverage; search 素食 and pair it with a translation app. Between the two you'll find the temple-area spots and any dedicated vegan hot pot places currently operating. Frequently asked questions
Can I really eat vegan hot pot in Kunming for ¥40? You can eat a great vegan lunch for ¥40 — a Buddhist vegetarian buffet or veggie dishes with rice. For the famous wild-mushroom hot pot specifically, expect ¥80 or more per person; ¥40 only stretches to a small solo portion.
Is Yunnan mushroom hot pot vegetarian? Not by default — the broth is usually chicken or pork-based. Ask for a vegetarian broth (素汤底) or a divided pot, or eat at a fully vegetarian Buddhist restaurant to be sure.
Where do I find vegetarian restaurants in Kunming? Around Yuantong Temple and Green Lake in the city centre, where Buddhist vegetarian spots cluster — look for 素食 or 素斋 signs. Use HappyCow or Dianping (search 素食) to locate current ones.
Are wild mushrooms safe to eat? Only when sourced and cooked properly at a reputable restaurant. Yunnan has hundreds of toxic species; cook thoroughly, never eat them undercooked, and don't forage or self-prepare — some species are deadly even after cooking.
How do I tell a restaurant I'm vegan? Say 我吃素 (I'm vegetarian) or 我是佛教徒 (I'm a Buddhist), ask for 素汤底 (vegetarian broth), and specify 不要鸡精 and 不要蚝油 (no chicken bouillon, no oyster sauce) to avoid hidden animal products.
