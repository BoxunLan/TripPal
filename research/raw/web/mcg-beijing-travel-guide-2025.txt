Four days covers Beijing properly. Three is possible, but you will spend it moving.
The city is cheap for a capital. The Forbidden City is ¥60, the Great Wall at Mutianyu is ¥40, a subway ride across town is ¥5. What catches first-timers isn't the cost — it's the booking. Forbidden City tickets release seven days ahead, at 8pm Beijing time, through a WeChat mini-program, and summer dates go fast. The museum is also closed every Monday.
Get those two wrong and Day 1 is gone. The full ticket walkthrough is here . The rest of this page is the easy part.

Check which airport you're landing at
Beijing has two, about 70 km apart, and they don't share baggage. PEK (Capital) sits northeast of the city. Daxing (PKX) is south and newer. Crossing between them takes 70 to 90 minutes by car, or about 90 minutes on the shuttle bus at ¥35–55. People get this wrong more often than you'd expect, so read your ticket twice.
Minimum connection times at PEK — terminal layout, and how tight is too tight

Fee-free ATMs in Daxing arrivals — Bank of China and ICBC machines inside PKX. Global ATM Alliance cards skip the ¥20–30 surcharge.

What a Beijing layover is actually good for

Paying for things
Alipay with a foreign card linked through Tour Pass covers almost everything — metro gates, street food, museum bookings, Didi. You need WeChat too, not for payment but because the Forbidden City and the National Museum both take reservations through mini-programs inside it. Cash still works. Nobody will be thrilled about it.
Paying in Beijing: the wallet setup

Yikatong card vs Alipay on the subway — the card still works when your phone dies, and there's a monthly discount most visitors never hit

Apps you actually need
Alipay (支付宝) — universal payment. Link a Visa/Mastercard in Tour Pass before you arrive. Works for metro, food stalls, ticket booking, Didi.

WeChat (微信) — needed for the Forbidden City mini-program booking and countless other museum reservations.

Didi (滴滴) — ride-hail in English mode.

Trip.com — HSR tickets, hotels, attraction entries in English.

Amap (高德地图) — Google Maps is offset in China. Amap has an English interface and accurate routing.

Pleco — Chinese dictionary with OCR menu translation.

A prepaid China eSIM (Airalo, Nomad, Holafly; $8–25 for a week's 5GB plan) keeps all of the above working over a foreign network that bypasses the Great Firewall.
Getting around
Subway fares go by distance: ¥3 under 6 km, ¥4 to 12 km, ¥5 to 22 km, ¥6 to 32 km, then ¥1 more per extra 20 km. Signs and announcements are bilingual.
Line 1 — east-west through Tiananmen, Wangfujing, Guomao. The tourist spine.

Line 2 — the Ring Line, around the old city walls. Connects Beijing Railway Station, Qianmen, Xuanwumen, Fuxingmen.

Line 4 — north-south through Xidan, Summer Palace, Beijing University. Airport-convenient via transfer.

Line 5 — north-south through Dongdan, Tiantan (Temple of Heaven), Lama Temple.

Line 8 — connects Qianmen, Nanluoguxiang, Olympic Park, Houhai (Shichahai).

Line 10 — the inner ring, connects National Library, Haidian, Sanlitun, Guomao.

Leaving the city by rail:
Beijing to Xi'an , Beijing to Shanghai , Beijing to Chengdu — high-speed, all bookable on a passport

Beijing to Pingyao on the overnight K601 — the slow one, and the better one

Beijing to Lhasa on the Z21 — permits, oxygen, and a very long sit

The Great Wall: pick a section, not a tour
Mutianyu is the right answer for a first visit. Entry is ¥40, plus ¥120 for the round-trip cable car or ¥100 for the chairlift-up, toboggan-down combination. Badaling costs the same and takes the coach tours, which is exactly why it fills up.
Getting there without a tour: the Mubus leaves Dongzhimen Outer at 9am, takes 90 minutes, ¥80 return. A Didi runs ¥280–400 each way — ask the driver to wait and the round trip lands near ¥700, which is fine split four ways. Either way you climb: the cable car drops you at tower 14, and the good stretch of wall is a stair-heavy walk from there.

Day 1: Tiananmen, Forbidden City, and Qianmen
Start at Tiananmen Square. Entry is free but you need your passport and you go through a security screening before entering the square. Line 1 metro to Tiananmen East or Tiananmen West gets you there for ¥3. Arrive by 8:30am to avoid the 10am crowd.
Walk through the square, face the portrait of Chairman Mao, then enter the Forbidden City (Meridian Gate / Wu Men). This is the one museum in China where advance booking is not optional — the Palace Museum only sells 40,000 tickets per day and they go fast. Book 7 days ahead on the official WeChat mini-program "故宫博物院 Palace Museum." Tickets are ¥60 from April 1 to October 31, ¥40 the rest of the year. Closed Mondays year-round.
Give yourself 3 hours for the main axis (south to north, Meridian Gate through Imperial Garden). If you want the Clock and Watches exhibition (my favorite), add ¥10 and 30 minutes. The Treasures Gallery is also ¥10 and worth it.
Exit through the north gate into Jingshan Park (entry ¥5). Climb the hill in 10 minutes for the best bird's-eye view of the entire Forbidden City. This is the photo you actually want from Beijing.
Lunch: walk 15 minutes east to Nanluoguxiang hutong for something tourist-priced but quick, or Metro Line 8 down to Qianmen Street for a sit-down meal. A hand-pulled noodle bowl runs ¥28–45, jianbing (Chinese breakfast crepe) is ¥15 from a cart.
Afternoon: Qianmen Street (前门大街), the restored commercial drag south of Tiananmen. It's touristy but useful for context. Walk it south to Dashilar (大栅栏) — the real Qing dynasty shopping alley with less tourist markup. Buy nothing unless you want Beijing's famous fabric shoes at Neiliansheng (¥200–500).
Dinner: Peking duck. First night, first duck. Details are in the next section.
Day 2: Great Wall at Mutianyu (full day)
Transport options are in the Great Wall section above. Once you're there:
At the wall itself: entry is ¥40, the shuttle bus from the gate to the wall base is ¥15, and the cable car up/down is ¥120 round trip. If you want to walk up both ways (steep stone steps, 30 minutes), skip the cable car.
Which towers to walk. Mutianyu has watchtowers numbered 1 to 23. The cable car drops you at Tower 14. Walk east to Tower 20 for the best scenery and fewest crowds. The restored section ends at Tower 23. Don't try to walk past 23 — the unrestored wild wall is closed to tourists.
Back in Beijing by 5pm. Dinner near your hotel, then early bed. Tomorrow is another long walking day.
Day 3: Temple of Heaven, Houhai, and a hutong walk
Morning: Temple of Heaven (天坛). Metro Line 5 to Tiantan Dongmen Station, walk 2 minutes to the east gate. Entry is ¥35 in peak season, ¥30 in low season, and you can buy either park-only (¥15) or the combo that includes the main circular hall and the Echo Wall. Spring for the combo; the Hall of Prayer for Good Harvests is the postcard.
Morning bonus: arrive at 8am for the park-only entry and watch Beijing retirees do tai chi, play pingpong, practice calligraphy on the ground with giant brushes dipped in water, and sing Chinese opera in the garden. This is the real Beijing morning that no tour bus will show you.
Lunch: head up to Dongsi for a bowl of Beijing zhajiangmian (炸酱面) — thick wheat noodles with salty fermented bean paste and julienned vegetables. ¥25–35. Or duck back into a hutong restaurant for jiaozi (dumplings) at a family-run shop.
Afternoon: Houhai Lake (后海) and the real hutong walk. Metro Line 8 to Shichahai. Skip the main Houhai bar strip — that's the Vegas version. Walk north and west into the alleys: Yandai Xiejie (烟袋斜街), Mao'er Hutong (帽儿胡同), and Ju'er Hutong (菊儿胡同). These are where locals still live behind gray brick walls, with ancient trees, the occasional siheyuan courtyard house, and cats sleeping on the doorsteps.
Dinner: a boutique hutong restaurant like Wangfujing Quanjude if you want formal, or Siji Minfu for a ¥180/person Peking duck set. Both are described below.
Evening: walk back through Houhai for the lit-up lakeside. It's genuinely pretty at night.
Day 4: Summer Palace, 798 Art District, and departure
Morning: Summer Palace (颐和园). Metro Line 4 to Beigongmen for the north gate entrance. Entry is ¥30 peak, ¥20 low season. The ¥60 combo ticket includes the Tower of Buddhist Incense and is worth it. The must-sees: the Long Corridor (728m of painted beams), Marble Boat, Tower of Buddhist Incense, and the 17-Arch Bridge on the east side. Exit at the east gate.
Lunch: grab a jianbing from a cart on your walk out of the palace (¥15) or head to Wudaokou for student-priced options.
Afternoon: 798 Art District (Metro Line 14 to Wangjing South, then Didi ¥15 or Bus 401). Free entry. Bauhaus factory complex converted into galleries, cafes, and studios. Great for 2 hours of slow wandering.
Evening: back to hotel, pack, and catch the Airport Express or PKX Express.
Peking duck
You cannot leave Beijing without eating Peking duck. Here's my real ranking, from neighborhood favorite to special-occasion splurge:
Siji Minfu (四季民福) — Beijing locals' current favorite for quality-per-yuan. Multiple branches; the Nanchizi location next to the Forbidden City east gate is the best. Half duck ¥148, whole ¥268. Book via Dianping or walk in at 5pm.
Li Qun Roast Duck (利群烤鸭店) — hutong institution in Qianmen. Whole duck ¥298. Follow your map into a gray-brick alley. Traditional fruit-wood oven, arguably the best skin in Beijing. Reserve ahead.
Da Dong Roast Duck (大董烤鸭) — modern fine-dining version. Whole duck ¥358–428. Carved tableside, styled on the plate. Bill for two with wine: ¥800–1,200. Special-occasion pick.
Quanjude (全聚德) — the famous chain. Whole duck ¥268–398. Fine, not amazing. The Wangfujing branch has the traditional show-carving ceremony if you want the classic brand experience.
Bianyifang (便宜坊) — older than Quanjude (founded 1416), uses a closed-oven method. Whole duck ¥248–298. Less touristy and arguably better.
How to order. You'll be asked whole (整只) or half (半只). For two people, half is enough; for three or four, whole. You'll get crispy sliced duck skin, duck meat slices, paper-thin mandarin pancakes, cucumber, scallions, and sweet bean sauce. Assemble the pancakes yourself and eat with your hands. Per-person cost runs ¥180–400.

What goes wrong
Monday. The Forbidden City closes, and it's not the only one.

Booking windows. Seven days for the Forbidden City, one day for the National Museum. Both through WeChat.

Your passport. Tiananmen Square, every museum, every hotel check-in. Carry the actual book.

The tea ceremony. Friendly English-speaking art students in Wangfujing invite you for tea, then produce a ¥2,000 bill. Decline and keep walking.

Badaling. Not a scam, just full.

Paddle boats at Beihai — ¥80 for visitors against ¥40 for residents. Walk the shore instead.

FAQ
How many days do I need in Beijing?
Four full days is the floor: two for the Forbidden City and the centre, one for the Great Wall, one for the Summer Palace and 798. Five or six if you want to stop walking occasionally. Three is doable but tight, and you will feel it. Two only makes sense on a layover.
When is the best time to visit?
Late September to mid-November is the best stretch — dry, clear, warm enough to walk all day. Late March through May is the other one. Both draw crowds, autumn more so. Avoid the first week of October, when the entire country travels at once. Winter is cold, empty, and ¥20 cheaper at the Forbidden City.
Do I need to book the Forbidden City in advance?
Yes. Tickets release seven days ahead at 8pm Beijing time through the Palace Museum WeChat mini-program, tied to your passport number, and summer slots sell out the same evening. There is no ticket window to queue at.
Two easy escapes from the city that most itineraries skip. Tianjin is thirty minutes and ¥54.5 away, with the most complete concession architecture left in China. Datong is two hours, for the Yungang Grottoes and the Hanging Temple — both of which have booking rules that strand people who turn up without checking.
