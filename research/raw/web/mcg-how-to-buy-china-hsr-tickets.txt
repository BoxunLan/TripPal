There are four ways to buy China HSR tickets: the official 12306 app or website, Trip.com, Klook, or a ticket window at the station. All four sell the same China Railway seats. They differ in fees, English support and how early you can book.
Quick heads up: this guide is about China mainland HSR , the G, D, and C-trains from Beijing to Shanghai to Guangzhou to Kunming. Not Taiwan HSR (THSR), which is a separate system. If your route starts with Taipei–Kaohsiung, you want THSR, not this article.
Everything below covers China Railway's high-speed network, which passed 50,000 km in December 2025. If you need a primer on routes, train types, and seat classes first, start with our China high-speed rail guide . This article is strictly about the booking part.

Booking HSR Train Tickets on 12306: The Official Channel
12306 takes foreign bank cards in the app and on the English website. The English site has accepted Visa, Mastercard, JCB and Diners Club since it launched in late 2020. Alipay and WeChat Pay work at checkout too. 12306 doesn't publish a spending cap of its own for foreign cards. The limit that applies is your card's online payment limit, and your bank may add a foreign transaction fee.
The honest downside : the English UI is still a 2 out of 5. Menus are translated unevenly. Error messages sometimes come back in Chinese. Some foreign cards are still declined. Have a backup card ready.
Registering and booking take a few steps: enter your passport details, pass the identity check (automatic, or by uploading a photo of your passport page), then search by station, add yourself as a passenger and pay. Our 12306 guide for foreigners walks through each step, plus changes and refunds.
Pro tip : if a card is rejected, try Alipay. Alipay lets foreign visitors link a Visa, Mastercard or other international card, and 12306 accepts Alipay at checkout.
Booking HSR Tickets on Trip.com: The Peace-of-Mind Option
Look, I'll be honest: if you've never been to China before and you just want the ticket to exist when you land, use Trip.com. It's not a scam. It's the same ticket.
Trip.com buys your ticket from 12306 and gives you an English app with live chat. Changes and refunds go through Trip.com, under China Railway's refund fee rules . For a first-time visitor, that support is the reason to use it.
What it costs : the checkout page shows the fare plus any Trip.com service fee before you pay. Compare that total with the 12306 fare for the same train. If the gap is small, the English support is worth it.
Step-by-step: Book on Trip.com
Open Trip.com (web or app). Tap "Trains" on the homepage.

Search . Enter origin, destination, date. You'll see the same HSR timetable you'd see on 12306, with nicer fonts.

Pick the train . Tap the G-train you want. Select seat class.

Enter passenger info . Passport type, passport number, full name, date of birth, nationality. No 12306 account required.

Pay . Credit or debit card, PayPal, Apple Pay or Google Pay.

Wait for confirmation . Inside the 15-day sale window, Trip.com emails you once the ticket is issued. Book earlier and your order becomes an advance reservation: Trip.com tries to buy the ticket as soon as sales open for your date.

One thing to watch : if tickets sell out before Trip.com can grab them (common on Chinese New Year), they refund you. You don't pay for tickets you don't get.
Klook and Other Aggregators
Most travelers don't need them.
Klook also sells China HSR tickets in English. Its China rail page takes bookings up to 90 days ahead and confirms them about 15 days before departure, once 12306 sales open. The page doesn't list a fee, so compare the checkout total with Trip.com and 12306.
Ctrip is the Chinese-language sister site of Trip.com, with less English. Skip it.
Hotel concierge desks can book for you, and some charge for it. Useful if your Wi-Fi is dead. Otherwise, book it yourself.
Buying HSR Tickets at the Station Counter (Last Resort)
Sometimes your phone dies. Sometimes the app rejects every card you own. Sometimes you land at Pudong at 11 pm and want to get to Suzhou.
Counter booking still works. Every major station has a ticket hall ("售票厅"), and some large stations have a window for foreign passengers. The clerk speaks limited English. Usually enough. The self-service ticket machines won't help most visitors: official guidance lists the Foreign Permanent Resident ID Card, not the passport, for machine purchases.
What to do:
Find the ticket hall. Follow the blue signs reading "售票" or "Tickets."

Queue at the foreigner window if there is one. Any window works.

Hand over your passport and a piece of paper with your destination in Chinese characters, the date, and the time.

Pay. Cash always works. Chinese bank cards always work. Foreign cards work at some counters but not all. Have ¥500–1,000 in cash as backup.

You may get a printed slip with your train and seat. It is not the ticket; you board with your passport.

A phone note with the destination in Chinese characters and a rough departure time is usually enough. The clerk can show you the available trains on the screen, and you pick one. The fare is the same as on 12306.
Do not try this during Chinese New Year, National Day week (Oct 1–7), or Labor Day week (May 1–5). Queues are brutal and the good trains are gone.
How Far in Advance Should You Book HSR Tickets?
Tickets go on sale 15 days ahead, counting the day of sale . On the 1st of a month, for example, you can buy tickets up to the 15th, online or at a station ticket window. Each station opens sales at its own time of day, so check the 起售时间 (sale start time) lookup on 12306 rather than assuming midnight.
Off-peak (March, April, Sep, Nov outside holiday weeks) : Book 3–7 days ahead. Second class is usually available same-day.

Regular peak (summer weekends, Fridays) : Book at the 15-day release or within 48 hours after.

Chinese New Year : Book the moment sales open for your date; popular dates sell out fast. A Trip.com advance reservation set up weeks ahead gets your order in as soon as sales open, but no agency can guarantee a seat.

National Day (Oct 1–7) and Labor Day (May 1–5) : Same story. Book the instant they release.

Same-day last minute : Usually possible outside holidays. Counter works.

For context, trains between Beijing and Shanghai run throughout the day. Off-peak, there's usually a seat. Spring Festival, you're competing with most of the country for the same car.
The Passport Rule Nobody Explains Clearly
This is the one thing that actually catches people: your ticket is your passport . There's no paper ticket to pick up. The booking is a record in China Railway's system tied to the passport number you entered.
At the station:
Go to the gate marked with your train number.

Scan your passport at the automatic gate. Some gates read foreign passports and some don't.

If the reader rejects it, walk to the manual staff lane on the side. Show your passport. The staff checks it and waves you through.

No printed ticket. No collection.
The gotcha : if the name on your booking doesn't match your passport exactly (one missing middle name, a hyphen in the wrong place, an initial instead of a full name), you can be turned away. Triple-check the name field when you book.
Common Booking Mistakes
Booking on an old passport . The ticket is tied to the passport number you entered. If you renew your passport before the trip, book with the new one.

Typos in the name field . "Christopher" vs "Chris" gets you rejected at the gate. Copy-paste from your passport.

Assuming all G-trains are equal . Between Beijing and Shanghai, some G-trains make only a few stops and others make many, which adds a lot of time. Check the duration.

Booking the wrong station . Shanghai has Hongqiao and Shanghai Station. Beijing has Beijing, Beijing South, Beijing West and Beijing Chaoyang, among others. Wrong station can mean a long cross-city taxi ride.

Forgetting your card's limit . 12306 has no published cap of its own, but your card's online payment limit applies. For a large family booking, check the limit with your bank or split the order.

Paper ticket assumption . There is no paper collection. The ticket is your passport.

FAQ
Can I really use my Visa on 12306 in 2026?
Yes. 12306 accepts foreign bank cards in the app and on the English website; the English site has taken Visa since it launched in late 2020. Alipay and WeChat Pay work too. 12306 publishes no cap of its own for foreign cards, so your card's limit applies. If your card fails, try Alipay or Trip.com.
How early should I arrive at a China HSR station?
Trip.com's station guide suggests 45 to 60 minutes before departure. Allow more at a big station, or if the gate may not read your passport and you need the staffed lane. Security is airport-style but faster. Ticket checks close before departure, at a time the station announces, and the train does not wait.
What's the difference between G, D, and C-trains?
G-trains top out at 350 km/h. D-trains are 200–250 km/h and usually cheaper. C-trains are short-haul commuters. For most intercity travel, book a G. See our full high-speed rail guide for the breakdown.
Are Taiwan HSR tickets booked the same way?
No. Taiwan HSR (THSR) is a separate system. Book on thsrc.com.tw or via Klook. This article only covers China mainland HSR.
Do children need their own tickets?
China Railway has used age, not height, since January 1, 2023. One child under 6 per paying adult rides free without a reserved seat; any other child under 6 needs a child ticket. Children aged 6 to 13 need a child ticket, priced at half the published fare. From 14, buy a full-price ticket. Bring each child's passport.
What happens if I miss my train?
You can change your ticket (改签) once. A change to another train on the same day is free after your train has left, if there are seats. Moving to a later date after departure costs 40% of the lower fare, and a ticket changed after departure can't be refunded. Ask at the station ticket counter.
