West Sichuan Is Where Foreigners Can Actually Drive

Nearly everywhere in China, a foreigner who wants to drive gives up and hires a driver. West Sichuan is the exception, and the reason is a counter in the arrivals hall at Chengdu Shuangliu.
Your International Driving Permit is worthless here — China is not party to the conventions that create them. But the temporary Chinese permit (临时驾驶许可) is issued on the spot at the vehicle-management desk in Shuangliu's T1 arrivals hall, and once you have it the paperwork stops being the problem. Altitude becomes the problem, which is a better one to have.
Getting the permit
Bring the passport, your home driving licence and the entry stamp.

The vehicle-management counter in T1 arrivals, open 09:00 to 17:30.

¥10 for the health check — blood pressure and an eye test.

A paper permit valid for your visa length or 90 days, whichever is shorter.

Thirty to sixty minutes if the queue is short. It covers class C1 passenger cars only, inside mainland China, and it does not include insurance — that comes from the rental company. eHi in particular will walk you through the counter if you have booked a car for same-day pickup. If you are landing at Tianfu instead, confirm with your rental company first rather than assuming the same desk exists there.

The loop that works first time
Chengdu out and back through Danba and Kangding. Paved end to end on the G318 and G350, fuel every 60 to 80 km, and — the part that matters — it raises you gradually rather than throwing you at 4,000 m on day one.

Day 3 into Xinduqiao is the one people photograph — morning light on painted Tibetan houses along a stretch that has been called a photographer's paradise often enough that it has stopped being a compliment and started being a fact.
With nine or ten days you can carry on south to Daocheng Yading on the G227 and G216: another 800 km round trip and two passes above 4,500 m. Only with acclimatisation behind you. Above 4,000 m both steering response and braking distance change, and neither changes in your favour.

Take the AWD mid-size as a floor. You are not buying comfort, you are buying torque on Zheduo Pass and clearance on the gravel detours around roadworks. The ¥100–150 a day over a 2WD is the cheapest insurance on the trip.
eHi (一嗨), Shenzhou Zuche (神州租车) and Avis China are the three that matter in Chengdu. Add the collision damage waiver at ¥60–90 a day, which drops the deductible from ¥3,000 to nothing. Counters price in yuan only and take Chinese cards, Alipay, WeChat Pay, or Visa and Mastercard with a 3–5% surcharge. The deposit, ¥3,000–8,000 depending on the vehicle, is frozen rather than charged — bring two cards in case one is declined.
Against a licensed Chengdu operator running the same loop at ¥4,500–6,800 per person for five days, self-driving works out at roughly ¥3,200–4,000 each for a party of two once the car, fuel and lodging are split. The saving is real; the flexibility is the actual reason.
Altitude is the constraint now
You climb from 500 m to 4,298 m in four days. Most people are fine. The ones who are not usually made an avoidable mistake on day one.
If a headache does not respond to ibuprofen, that is not a normal headache.

Breathlessness while sitting still is a signal, not a phase.

Confusion, stumbling or loss of coordination means go down now.

Pink or frothy sputum is pulmonary oedema. That is an emergency, not a rest day.

The rule that covers most of it: if symptoms are worse after twelve hours at the same altitude, drop 500 to 1,000 m. Kangding People's Hospital (康定市人民医院) has an altitude ward and basic English.
Driving adds its own layer. Above 3,500 m a second licensed driver is a genuine safety asset and you should swap every 90 to 120 minutes, because altitude fatigue arrives without announcing itself. Solo, stop for fifteen minutes every hour and keep drinking.
Where the road stops
Past Batang on the G318, the boundary into the Tibet Autonomous Region is a permit checkpoint, and self-driving across it is not permitted for foreigners at all — you need a Tibet Travel Permit and a registered guide vehicle. Only a licensed agency can file for that permit , and it comes bundled with a booked tour.
Garze and Aba prefectures, which is everything in this guide, are culturally Tibetan and permit-free. That is the whole appeal: the landscape and the monasteries without the paperwork.

Practicalities
Amap is the only navigation that works — real-time traffic, speed-camera warnings and fuel stations, with rural road data that Google has not had for years. Download the Sichuan offline pack before you leave Chengdu, because coverage disappears between Danba and Tagong. Getting online in China covers the SIM side, and Didi is your fallback if the car dies somewhere paved.
On fines: cameras photograph the plate and the bill reaches the rental company, which charges your card. ¥50 at 10% over, ¥200 at 20%, and ¥2,000 plus suspension at 50%. Limits drop to 40 km/h through Tibetan villages and are enforced. An empty road is not an unwatched road.
Emergency numbers worth having offline: traffic police 122, Sichuan highway rescue 12122, tourist complaints 12301. eHi's hotline is 4006-006-666 and Shenzhou's 4006-166-166; say "English please" first and you will get an English operator. Roadside help is 60 to 180 minutes on a main road and four to eight hours near Danba or Tagong, so the water, snacks, power bank and warm layers in the boot are not decoration.
Before and after the drive, the Chengdu guide covers the city, and Jiuzhaigou is the other high-altitude Sichuan trip — though that one you reach by train and shuttle, not by car.
FAQ
Can foreigners self-drive in West Sichuan?
Yes, with a temporary Chinese driving permit. An International Driving Permit is not valid in mainland China, but the temporary permit is issued at the vehicle-management counter in Chengdu Shuangliu's T1 arrivals hall in 30 to 60 minutes, for a ¥10 health check.
Do I need an AWD car for the Kangding loop?
In dry summer a 2WD SUV can physically do the paved loop, but take AWD. Zheduo Pass gets sudden snow even in September, the Hailuogou access road has 15% grades, and gravel detours around roadworks appear without warning. The difference is ¥100–150 a day.
Can I drive from Sichuan into Tibet?
No. The boundary past Batang on the G318 is a permit checkpoint and foreigners cannot self-drive across it — a Tibet Travel Permit and a registered guide vehicle are required. Garze and Aba prefectures on the Sichuan side need no permit at all.
How bad is the altitude on this route?
You reach 4,298 m at Zheduo Pass on day four, having climbed gradually, which is why this loop suits first-timers. Most people get no worse than a headache. Drop 500 to 1,000 m if symptoms worsen after twelve hours at the same height; Kangding People's Hospital has an altitude ward.
