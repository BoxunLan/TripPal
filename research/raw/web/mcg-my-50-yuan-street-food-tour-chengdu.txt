The ¥50 Chengdu Street Food Walk I Take Every Visiting Friend On

My cousin flew in from Frankfurt last spring with two rules: no tour groups, and no more hotpot chains with laminated English menus. So I walked him the route my family walks when we want lunch out. Five dishes, five stalls, ninety minutes, ¥50.
The food tours sold online for this run ¥300 to ¥600. They are not buying better food. They are buying you a guide through Jinli, where the same dish costs three times more.

The trick that makes five dishes fit
Order by weight, not by bowl. Noodles and wontons here are sold in 两 (liǎng), about 50 grams of dry noodles. Say 一两 (yī liǎng) and you get a small bowl — a tasting portion, which is what street food is supposed to be. Default is usually 二两, and two of those will end your afternoon at stop three.
This is the entire mechanism. Not a cheaper neighbourhood, not a secret stall. Just a smaller number.
Stop 1 — Dan dan mian, ¥8
Out of Kuanzhai Alley's north exit, left onto Changshun Shangjie. About 200 m down there is a row of noodle shops; look for the handwritten yellow sign reading 担担面. Kuanzhai itself is a tourist alley and the food inside it is priced accordingly — you are walking past it, not into it.
Hand-pulled wheat noodles, ground pork, chopped sui mi ya cai, roasted peanuts, chili oil. The bowl is small on purpose. Hold up one finger: 一碗担担面 (yī wǎn dān dān miàn). She will probably ask 辣吗 (là ma) — spicy? Nod once.
Eat it standing, or on the plastic stool outside. This is the warm-up for your tongue.
Stop 2 — Chao shou, ¥12
Four hundred metres west onto Kangzhuang Jie. Any red-signed place with 抄手 in the name. Chao shou means crossed hands — the fold of the wrapper.
Ask for 红油抄手，小碗 (hóng yóu chāo shǒu, xiǎo wǎn). Eight to ten pork wontons in chili oil with vinegar, sugar, garlic and sesame. The sauce is sweeter than the noodles were, which is the point of the order.
Want it gentler? 清汤 (qīng tāng) puts the same wontons in clear pork broth for the same ¥12. Some people order one of each.
Worth knowing what you are choosing between: chao shou are the thin triangle-folded wontons; shui jiao are the thicker pleated dumplings — 钟水饺, the chili-oil dumpling brand going since 1893, is about ¥10 at a neighbourhood shop and ¥15 to ¥25 inside Jinli or Kuanzhai. Same dumpling, different postcode.
Stop 3 — Mapo tofu, ¥15
Back east into Zhitai Jie. This is a street where people live, not the Tang-dynasty stage set inside Kuanzhai. Any rice-plate shop will do it.
麻婆豆腐小锅 (má pó dòu fǔ xiǎo guō). A small clay pot of silky tofu in doubanjiang, ground beef and Sichuan peppercorn, with rice.
The 麻 in mapo is not heat. It is the peppercorn, and it makes your lips buzz like a low current. First-timers usually think something has gone wrong with their mouth for about a minute. Nothing has. 少辣少麻 (shǎo là shǎo má) dials it back, though the peppercorn is going in regardless — without it the dish is not mapo tofu.
Stop 4 — Cold noodles, ¥10
Five minutes back toward Kuanzhai's south exit, onto Tongren Lu, where the cold-dish stalls are. Chengdu 凉面 is not the Xi'an version: yellow alkaline noodles, cooked and cooled, tossed in soy, vinegar, chili oil and minced garlic.
If you only order one cold dish in Chengdu, make it 甜水面 (tián shuǐ miàn) instead — thick, chewy hand-pulled noodles in a sweet-spicy chili and sesame sauce. Same price bracket, and it is the one my cousin ordered twice.
Stop 5 — Something to drink, ¥5
Your mouth is vibrating. This is the rescue round.
Fresh fruit tea stalls are everywhere in this neighbourhood — ¥5 for passionfruit or mango. If you want something savoury instead, ¥5 at a soy milk stall gets you warm 米浆 (mǐ jiāng), rice milk, which is the native Chengdu breakfast drink and the only thing in the city that properly cuts peppercorn.
Ordering when you speak no Mandarin
Nobody on this route speaks English, and it does not matter. Point at the photo menu, or show the characters on your phone — copy 担担面, 红油抄手, 麻婆豆腐, 凉面 into your notes before you leave. Count with fingers. Then two phrases carry the rest of it:
不要辣 (bù yào là) — no chili at all

少辣 (shǎo là) — less spicy, the safe setting if you can take some

Locals walking this same block do not say much more than that. Ordering food in China without Chinese has the longer version if you want it.
Bring a phone set up to pay. Most stalls here take only Alipay or WeChat Pay QR codes, and plenty of them no longer keep change. Keep ¥20 in small notes for the one that does.
Why the ¥380 version costs ¥380
The gap is location, not cooking. Street food inside Jinli runs ¥25 to ¥50 a bite — roughly triple what the same thing costs four streets away. A booked tour spends its money on the guide, a pre-arranged hotpot lunch that is not street food, a commission-paying tea shop, and enough walking between them that you eat about one dish an hour.
Jinli is still worth an evening for the lanterns. Just eat first — the stalls on Wuhou Ci Dong Jie, immediately outside its east exit, sell the same dishes at neighbourhood prices. What street food actually costs across China puts Chengdu against the other cities.
Going at the right hour
11:30, not 1pm. Stalls are freshest as the lunch rush starts; by 2pm the good ones have sold out.

Shoes you can wash. Chili oil on the pavement is normal.

Tissues. Small shops rarely provide napkins; a convenience store sells them for ¥2.

Amap, not Google Maps — Google is offset by around 500 m here and Amap has English search.

If you want more of the same neighbourhood afterwards, the Chengdu citywalk route picks up a few streets over.
FAQ
Can vegetarians do this route?
Most of it. Mapo tofu can be made without meat — 不要肉 (bù yào ròu). Dan dan mian is harder, since the pork is structural; swap in 甜水面 or cold noodles instead.
How spicy is it, really?
Hotter than most visitors expect. 少辣少麻 gets you a mild version, 不要辣 gets you none. 特辣 (tè là) is what locals order and is not a dare worth taking on day one.
Can I do it with kids?
Yes, ordering the 不要辣 versions. Chengdu children do not eat full-strength mapo tofu until they are teenagers either.
What if ¥50 is my whole day's food budget?
Drop the drink. Dan dan mian ¥8, chao shou ¥12, mapo tofu ¥15, cold noodles ¥10, and ¥5 of water still leaves you five dishes.
