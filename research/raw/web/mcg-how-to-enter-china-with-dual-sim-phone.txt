If you are travelling to China in 2026 with a dual-SIM phone, you already have the best possible setup. You keep your home number for bank 2FA, WhatsApp, and Google. You add a Chinese SIM for local data, DiDi, and payment apps. No customs paperwork, no fees, no hidden rules. The only thing to get right is which SIM goes in which slot, how to switch data between them, and how to avoid the one common configuration mistake that leaves foreigners with a ¥2,000 roaming bill.

Customs at the border: the short answer
You do not declare your phone at Chinese customs. You walk through the green channel. The officers are not interested in what is in your pocket. Dual-SIM is not flagged, not restricted, not even noticed — it is what 90% of Chinese residents use.
The only thing that matters at the border is that your phone is not broadcasting GPS-denied or mesh-radio frequencies, which is a constraint for drone controllers and Starlink terminals, not normal smartphones.
Why dual-SIM is the ideal China setup
You keep your home number alive
Your home SIM (US, UK, EU, Australia) stays active in slot 1. It receives SMS verification codes from:
Your bank (critical for international transfers and card top-ups)

Google and Apple (for account recovery)

WhatsApp (if you use it to contact family)

Your corporate VPN or SSO

If your home SIM goes dark for two weeks, you can lock yourself out of half your accounts. Keeping the dual-SIM slot live — even with data off — keeps all that working.
A local Chinese SIM gives you real data
A local SIM lets you run DiDi, Amap, Alipay, Meituan, and WeChat at local speeds. It also assigns you a +86 number, which unlocks things like train ticket pickup via 12306, some hotel check-ins, and any online service that requires SMS verification to a Chinese number.

US-market iPhone 14 and later are eSIM-only — no physical SIM tray. That is fine for dual-eSIM, but it means you cannot insert a Chinese physical SIM from China Mobile. You need a carrier that sells eSIM activation to tourists, which in 2026 is limited.
The airport SIM buying routine
Step 1 — exit to arrivals
Beijing Capital, Shanghai Pudong, Guangzhou Baiyun, Chengdu Tianfu, Shenzhen, and most tier-1 airports have China Mobile (中国移动), China Unicom (中国联通), and China Telecom (中国电信) counters in the arrivals hall. Go to any of them.
Step 2 — ask for a tourist package
The phrase is "travel SIM card" or just "SIM" plus your length of stay. They will offer packages with prepaid data. 2026 pricing at airport counters:

Passport is required for registration. The clerk scans it, takes a photo of you, and the SIM is active in 5 minutes. Payment by cash, Alipay, WeChat Pay, or foreign card.
Step 3 — insert in slot 2
Open your phone's SIM tray. Your home SIM goes in slot 1 (already there). The Chinese SIM goes in slot 2. If you have an iPhone with eSIM + physical SIM, the home eSIM stays and the physical China SIM goes in the tray.
iPhone dual-SIM configuration in China
Set up Mobile Data on the China SIM
Settings → Cellular

Tap the China SIM (labelled "China Mobile" or similar)

Under Cellular Plan Label, rename it "China Data"

Set Default Voice Line to your home SIM (keeps incoming calls on your home number if roaming is on, or silent if roaming is off)

Set Cellular Data to the China SIM

Turn OFF "Allow Cellular Data Switching"

That last step is critical. Data Switching will allow your phone to fall back to the home SIM's roaming for data if the China SIM signal is weak — and that is how you get a ¥2,000 roaming bill.
Disable home SIM data roaming
Settings → Cellular → Home SIM → Data Roaming OFF

Keep Voice Roaming ON if you want to receive 2FA SMS

Data Roaming OFF, Voice Roaming ON is the safe combination

Android dual-SIM configuration in China
On most Android phones
Settings → Connections → SIM card manager

Mobile data: select the China SIM

Calls: select home SIM (for 2FA) or China SIM (for local calling)

SMS: select home SIM (for 2FA to your home bank)

Roaming: turn off on the home SIM

Google Pixel specific
Pixel phones sometimes struggle to auto-configure APN on Chinese carriers. If the China SIM connects but data does not flow:
Settings → Network & internet → SIMs → China Mobile

Access Point Names → add new

Name: CMNET

APN: cmnet

MCC: 460

MNC: 00

Save, select the new APN, toggle airplane mode on and off. Data should flow in 30 seconds.
eSIM alternatives if you cannot buy physical
Some travellers prefer not to deal with airport queues. eSIM providers like Airalo, Holafly, Nomad, and Ubigi sell China data plans. In 2026 these work by tunnelling your traffic via Hong Kong or Singapore, which has two consequences:
Pros
Activated before you fly, working on landing

No airport queue, no ID check

Traffic routed via international gateway, so Google and Western apps work without VPN

Cons
More expensive per GB (roughly ¥35/GB vs ¥7/GB on a local SIM)

No +86 number, which locks you out of some Chinese apps

Some providers have throttling above 5–10 GB

For a short trip where you only need data for maps, DiDi, and messaging, an Airalo eSIM is the cleanest solution. For a longer stay or heavy use, a physical China SIM is still better.
The combined strategy
This is what I recommend to every dual-SIM traveller:
Home SIM in slot 1: Data Roaming OFF, Voice Roaming ON. This keeps 2FA SMS and bank calls flowing, no data cost.

Chinese physical SIM in slot 2: bought at the airport, ¥100 for 7 days or ¥200 for 30 days. All mobile data routes through it.

Airalo or similar eSIM as standby: pre-loaded on iPhone/Samsung/Pixel for the 20-minute window between landing and getting to the airport counter, or for cities where the counter line is too long.

This gives you three layers of redundancy, no surprise charges, and full functionality on day one.
WhatsApp, Google, and the Great Firewall note
Your home SIM remains registered to WhatsApp and your Google account as normal. That registration does not depend on whether you have data. What changes when you connect to a Chinese data network is that the traffic cannot reach WhatsApp and Google servers without a VPN.
For foreigners, the practical workaround in 2026 is one of:
Use an Airalo-style international eSIM for apps that need the free internet

Install a VPN before you fly (Astrill, ExpressVPN, WireGuard custom — test first)

Switch to Chinese equivalents (WeChat for messaging, Baidu Maps or Amap for maps)

Frequently asked questions
Do I need to register my phone with Chinese authorities when I arrive?
No. You do not register the device itself. The Chinese SIM you buy is registered to your passport automatically at purchase. Your home SIM is not registered anywhere — it just works.
Will Chinese customs ask to inspect my phone?
Extremely rarely, and only if you are flagged for another reason. Normal tourist entry does not involve phone inspection. Dual-SIM is not a flag.
Can I buy a Chinese SIM without a passport?
No. All SIM sales in China require ID since 2016. Foreigners use passport, Chinese nationals use ID card. There is no way around this.
Does dual-SIM drain more battery?
Slightly, yes. Expect 5–10% more battery drain on dual-SIM active because both radios stay registered to their networks. Turn off the home SIM radio (via airplane mode per-line on iPhone, or toggle in SIM manager on Android) if you want maximum battery.
Will my US iPhone work in China?
Yes, for data on an Airalo-style eSIM. No, for physical China SIM since US iPhones 14+ have no tray. Your best bet with a US iPhone is to add a Chinese carrier eSIM remotely (China Mobile offers this in some markets) or rely on an Airalo-style eSIM.
What about 5G support?
All three Chinese carriers run 5G in major cities. Your international phone needs to support China's 5G bands (n41, n78, n79) to benefit. Most 2022-and-newer flagships do. Older models fall back to 4G LTE, which is still fast enough for everything.
Can I tether my home phone to my China SIM for a friend?
Yes. Hotspot works normally on both SIMs. Select the China SIM as the data source, enable hotspot, share with any device. Useful if your travel companion's phone is not dual-SIM or is eSIM-only US hardware.
Do Chinese SIM cards expire?
Tourist packages are time-boxed — a 7-day package dies in 7 days. The SIM itself can be topped up and kept alive for longer if you stay in China, but for short trips the package-and-discard model is normal. Do not panic when the data cuts off on day 8.
What phone number will people see when I call from China?
If you use the China SIM as the calling line, the +86 Chinese number shows. If you keep the home SIM as the calling line (with Voice Roaming on), your home number shows — but the call routes internationally and the cost is high. Most people send messages via WeChat or WhatsApp instead of using voice calls at all.
Do I need a VPN?
Only if you need to access sites and apps blocked by Chinese filtering — Google, Gmail, YouTube, Facebook, Instagram, Twitter/X, WhatsApp, most Western news. If your trip is three days of Forbidden City photography and dumpling eating, you can live without a VPN. If you depend on Gmail or Google Docs daily, install and test a VPN before you fly, because VPN download sites are themselves blocked from inside China.
Setup checklist before you board your flight to China
Print or screenshot this list.
Dual-SIM phone charged, home SIM in slot 1

Home carrier's data roaming turned off in settings

Home carrier's SMS roaming left on (for 2FA)

Alipay installed, passport verified, Tour Pass topped up

WeChat installed (optional but useful)

Amap installed, offline map of your first city downloaded

VPN of choice installed and tested (if needed)

DiDi international app installed (as backup to Alipay DiDi mini-program)

Copy of passport photo on your phone (for SIM registration if asked)

¥500 cash from home currency exchange or airport ATM for backup

Ten minutes of prep before you board saves you two hours of WiFi-hunting at arrivals.
That ten minutes of prep is mostly one decision: which eSIM. The eSIM comparison covers what each carrier actually routes through, and eSIM versus VPN explains why the roaming route has held up better than VPNs since the April 2026 crackdown. If you want a local number as well as data, buying a Chinese SIM as a foreigner covers the passport registration at the counter.
