Tiger Leaping Gorge Solo Hiking 2026: Bus, Gear & Trail Guide

The last minibus from Qiaotou back to Lijiang pulled away at 16:30 while I was still arguing with a noodle stall owner about whether ¥25 was a reasonable price for a bowl that smelled like diesel. It was June, I was soaking wet, my pack weighed more than 14 kg, and the only thing standing between me and a very long night was whether Jane's Guesthouse still had a bunk at 19:00.
They did. They always do if you arrive before dark and smile. That is the first rule of hiking Tiger Leaping Gorge solo: the trail has an economy, and the economy runs on walk-ins, cash, and showing up when you say you will.

Lijiang to Qiaotou: the only bus route that makes sense
The cheapest, most reliable way into the gorge starts at Lijiang Bus Station (Lijiang Keyun Zhan), not the old town. Tourist touts in Lijiang Old Town will try to sell you a "Tiger Leaping Gorge day tour" for ¥280-400 that drops you at Walnut Grove and calls it done. That is not the hike. That is a photo stop at the scenic area gift shop.
The minibus schedule in 2026
Minibuses leave Lijiang Bus Station for Qiaotou (sometimes listed as "Hutiao Xia Zhen" on the departure board) at 07:30, 08:30, and 09:30 most days. Tickets cost ¥60-80 one way depending on season and whether the driver is running a shared van or the scheduled coach. Journey time is 2-3 hours on a twisting mountain road. Buy tickets the night before through Trip.com (their English interface lists most long-distance Yunnan buses now) or walk up to the station counter 30 minutes before departure.
Tell the driver "Qiaotou" (pronounced "chow-toe"), not "Tiger Leaping Gorge." Point at your phone map if needed. Amap shows the stop as 桥头 — that is the village you want. If the driver tries to drop you at the scenic area gate 2 km down the road, stay firm and ask for "Qiaotou cun" (Qiaotou village).
Coming from Shangri-La instead
If you are starting north in Shangri-La (Diqing), buses to Qiaotou run from Shangri-La Bus Station at roughly 07:30, 09:00, 11:00, and 13:00 for ¥35-50. The ride is shorter — about 1.5 hours — and the road is gentler. This is the route most hikers use when chaining the gorge onto a Yunnan circuit that includes Songzanlin Monastery or Lugu Lake.

Skip this section only if you already know the difference between Qiaotou (the trailhead village) and Walnut Grove (the scenic area parking lot 25 km downstream). Most "Tiger Leaping Gorge" listings on Ctrip and Klook mean Walnut Grove. That is not where the trail starts.
The High Road vs the Low Road
There are two trails through the gorge. Only one is worth your time.
The Low Road is a paved road that runs along the Yangtze at the bottom of the gorge. Tour buses use it. It has rockfall warnings and has been closed to independent walkers several times since 2023 for landslide repairs. Check the status at the Qiaotou gate before you commit. As of April 2026, the Low Road is officially closed to solo hikers except for a short section near Walnut Grove.
The High Road (also called the Upper Trail or the "28 Bends") is the real hike. It climbs above the gorge on old Naxi footpaths, passes Naxi Family Guesthouse, switches back through the infamous 28 Bends climb, and finishes at Tina's Guesthouse near the Middle Rapids viewpoint. Total distance: roughly 22 km over two days. Highest point: around 2,670 m. Starting elevation at Qiaotou: 1,800 m.
Entry fee at the Qiaotou gate is ¥45 in 2026 (up from ¥34 in 2021). Cash or WeChat Pay accepted. Bring your passport — staff will note your name in the log book in case mountain rescue ever needs it.
Gear that survives the High Road
I hauled crampons up the High Road in October 2020. There was no snow. There was just wet moss, sore knees, and a lesson about trusting Reddit threads from 2014. Here is the gear that actually works in 2026.
Footwear
Trail runners with aggressive tread beat hiking boots on the 28 Bends. The granite steps get slick in afternoon rain, and boots punish your ankles on the long descent to Tina's. Salomon Sense Ride, La Sportiva Bushido, or any mid-range Decathlon Quechua trail shoe with a Vibram or Contagrip sole will work. Hiking sandals are a terrible idea. So are canvas Converse. I have seen both.
Rain shell
A packable 3-layer hardshell, not a "water-resistant" softshell. The gorge funnels weather hard — I have watched bluebird mornings turn into sideways rain by 13:00. A ¥180 Decathlon Quechua MH500 jacket will outperform most ¥1000+ brand-name shells for the two days you need it. Pack it at the top of your bag, not the bottom.
Water
Carry 2 litres out of Qiaotou. There are three reliable refill points: Naxi Family Guesthouse (km 6, ¥3-5 per litre), Tea Horse Guesthouse (km 11), and Halfway Guesthouse (km 14). A bladder with a drink tube beats bottles because you can sip on the uphill sections without stopping to dig into your pack.
Navigation
Download the offline Yunnan map in Amap (高德地图) or Maps.me before leaving Lijiang. Cell signal is spotty above Naxi Family and dies entirely around the 28 Bends. The trail itself is well marked with red paint arrows on rocks, but fog can erase the route in minutes. GPX tracks from AllTrails work offline if you paid for the Pro tier.
What to leave home
Crampons unless you are hiking January or February. A three-season tent (the guesthouses are cheap and you will want a hot shower). Jeans. Cotton anything. A "ultralight" tarp that is neither light nor ultra. Also, do not bring bear spray. There are no bears. There are friendly village dogs that want your biscuits.
The full pack list
Trail runners with sticky rubber soles

Packable 3-layer rain shell

2L water bladder or two 1L bottles

Headlamp with fresh batteries (dorm showers sometimes lose power)

Warm layer: synthetic puffy or fleece (it drops to 8-10 deg C at night even in summer)

Sunblock and a brimmed hat (high UV at 2,600 m)

Blister tape and ibuprofen

Power bank (10,000 mAh minimum)

¥400-600 cash — no ATMs past Qiaotou

Passport (required at the gate and at guesthouse check-in)

Guesthouses at the trailhead and along the route
This is where solo hikers panic. The real answer is calm: walk-ins are the norm, and the 5 main guesthouses almost always have beds if you arrive before dark.
Jane's Guesthouse (Qiaotou)
Jane's is in Qiaotou village itself, a 5-minute walk from where the bus drops you. Dorm beds ¥60-80, private rooms ¥180-280 in 2026. Jane (real name Shufen, but everyone calls her Jane) has been running the guesthouse since the early 2000s and still prints a hand-drawn trail map for every guest. She takes cash, WeChat Pay, and Alipay.
Naxi Family Guesthouse (km 6)
About 2.5 hours into the High Road. Good lunch stop. Dorm beds ¥50-70 if you want to break the hike into three shorter days. The courtyard has a persimmon tree that ripens in October.
Tea Horse Guesthouse (km 11, past the 28 Bends)
The classic "survived the Bends" celebration point. Beds ¥60-100. Their yak meat noodles are the best on the trail — worth the ¥35.
Halfway Guesthouse (km 14)
The most famous name on the trail. Their terrace bathroom has been called "the best view toilet in China" for 15 years and they lean into it. Beds ¥80-150, private rooms ¥200-350. Book the private room through Trip.com if you are hiking in October peak season — dorms stay open for walk-ins.
Tina's Guesthouse (km 22, trail end)
The endpoint of the standard 2-day hike. Dorm beds ¥60-120, private rooms ¥180-400. Tina's also runs the shuttle back to Qiaotou or onward to Shangri-La — ¥30-50 per seat, departs 11:00 and 15:00 daily.
A realistic 2-day itinerary
Day 1: Qiaotou to Halfway (14 km, 6-8 hours)
07:30 — Arrive Qiaotou on the Lijiang minibus

08:00 — Breakfast at Jane's (¥25 for noodles and tea)

08:30 — Register at the gate (¥45, passport required)

09:00 — Start climbing toward Naxi Family

11:30 — Lunch at Naxi Family (¥35-50)

13:00 — Begin the 28 Bends climb (2,670 m high point)

15:30 — Tea Horse Guesthouse, refill water, rest

17:30 — Arrive Halfway Guesthouse, shower, dinner ¥40-60

Day 2: Halfway to Tina's, shuttle out (8 km, 3-4 hours)
08:00 — Breakfast at Halfway (¥25, pancakes are good)

08:30 — Hike to Middle Rapids lookout

11:00 — Arrive Tina's, drop pack

11:30 — Optional side hike down to the Sky Ladder and the gorge floor (extra 1.5 hours round trip, steep)

14:30 — Back at Tina's for a shower and lunch

15:00 — Shuttle back to Qiaotou or onward to Shangri-La

Seasonal windows and altitude notes
Best months are late March through early June, and mid-September through early November. July and August are monsoon — the High Road is still walkable but the 28 Bends turn into a waterfall and the Low Road gets closed for landslides. December through February is cold, dry, and often beautiful, but nights at the guesthouses drop below freezing and some rooms do not have heaters.
At 2,670 m the 28 Bends are not high enough to cause serious altitude sickness, but if you fly into Shangri-La (3,200 m) and hike the gorge the same day, you will feel it. Spend a night in Lijiang (2,400 m) first. Drink water. Skip alcohol on your first night at Halfway.

Frequently asked questions
Is Tiger Leaping Gorge safe to hike solo in 2026?
Yes, on the High Road, if you are in reasonable shape and carry basic gear. The trail is well marked, other hikers are usually within shouting distance during daylight, and the guesthouses form a natural rescue chain. Avoid the Low Road (officially closed to solo walkers), do not hike after dark, and register at the Qiaotou gate so staff know you are on the mountain.
Do I need a guide?
No. The High Road is a marked trail with frequent guesthouses. A guide can be useful if you do not speak any Chinese and want cultural context, but for logistics and safety, solo hiking is the norm. Most hikers on the trail on any given day are going without a guide.
Can I hike in one day?
Technically yes — strong hikers can push Qiaotou to Tina's in 9-11 hours. But you will miss the best part, which is waking up at Halfway to clouds pouring over the Jade Dragon Snow Mountain across the valley. Do the two-day hike. The extra ¥120 is the best money you will spend in Yunnan.
How do I pay for everything without a Chinese bank account?
Cash for the bus and the trail entry, Alipay Tour Pass or WeChat Pay (linked to your foreign Visa/Mastercard via the app) for the guesthouses. Carry at least ¥400 cash because signal drops on the mountain and card readers fail regularly. ATMs at Qiaotou are unreliable — withdraw in Lijiang before you leave.
Is there cell signal on the trail?
Patchy. China Mobile has the best coverage around Qiaotou, Naxi Family, and Halfway. Between km 8 and km 12 (the 28 Bends) signal disappears entirely. Download offline maps before you hike. Tell someone in Lijiang your expected arrival time at Halfway so they can alert the guesthouse if you go missing.
