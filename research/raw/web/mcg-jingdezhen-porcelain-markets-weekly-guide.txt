What You Can Buy in Jingdezhen Depends on the Day You Arrive

M MyChinaGuide Editorial Team · Updated September 14, 2026

Jingdezhen has made porcelain for more than a thousand years, and what you can buy there depends entirely on which day you arrive. The three markets that matter run on different days at different hours, and none of them is open on a Tuesday.
Get the day wrong and the town is shops. Get it right and you are buying from the person who made the thing.

A Friday-to-Monday visit catches all three. Arriving Tuesday morning and leaving Thursday catches none of them, which is how people come away thinking Jingdezhen is a museum town.

Why Taoxichuan is different from a tourist market
Nearly five hundred stalls a session, and the reason the quality holds is the entry rule rather than anything about the setting. To get a stall you must hold a college diploma or above, the work must be your own original design, and factory goods are barred from applying at all.
That is an unusual filter and it shows. The people behind the tables are mostly young ceramicists who moved here to make things — several thousand of them live in the town for exactly this reason — and the stall is their own studio output rather than a wholesale reseller's. Prices are correspondingly real: you are paying a maker, not a middleman.
It is not only ceramics. Handmade leather, wood and root carving, ironwork and copper kettles, textiles and handmade food all qualify under the same rules.
Taoxichuan itself is the former Yuzhou state porcelain factory, kept as brick and kilns and converted rather than demolished. Going in daylight to see the site and returning at 16:30 for the market is the right order.
The Ghost Market, and what it actually is
Four in the morning on a Monday, at the antiques market on Shuguang Road, people walk between sellers with torches. It disperses at dawn. The name comes from exactly that: the dark, the torchlight, and the fact that the provenance of what is being sold cannot be established.
Treat that last part as the operating assumption rather than a rumour. Assume everything is a reproduction, buy only what you would be happy to own at the price if it were made last year, and do not believe a dynasty attribution offered at 04:30 by torchlight. Within those limits it is a genuinely enjoyable couple of hours and unlike anything else you will do in China.
Bring a torch. Everyone else has one and the sellers do not provide light.
Getting it home
The failure mode of a good day here is a bag you cannot fly with. Most stallholders will pack and ship, and packing porcelain is a skill they have — but agree it at the stall rather than discovering later that you are carrying a vase through three airports.
If you would rather handle it yourself, sending a package from China as a foreigner covers the couriers, the customs declaration and what the difference between EMS and SF Express actually costs. Keep the receipt: for anything that could be taken for an antique, being able to show it was bought new from a named maker is worth having at customs.
Getting there
Jingdezhen is in Jiangxi, on the high-speed line between Nanchang and Huangshan, and it is about three hours from Shanghai Hongqiao and ninety minutes from Huangshan. Booking on 12306 with a passport is straightforward and avoids the OTA fee.
Elsewhere in Jiangxi, Wangxian Valley is the cliffside village most people have seen online without knowing which province it is in — the answer is this one. And if buying craft is the theme of the trip, bargaining in Suzhou's silk market is the other end of the same skill, with very different rules: at Taoxichuan you do not haggle with a maker over their own work.
Two days, in the right order
Friday. Arrive, Taoxichuan in daylight for the factory site, then the market from 16:30.

Saturday. The Imperial Kiln Museum and the old kiln sites in the day, Sculpture Factory market from 15:30.

Sunday. Taoxichuan again if the first visit went well, because the stalls rotate.

Monday, if you are still there. Ghost Market at 04:00, sleep afterwards, leave in the afternoon.

FAQ
When are Jingdezhen's markets open?
Taoxichuan runs Friday to Sunday, 16:30 to 22:00. The Sculpture Factory market is Saturday afternoon, 15:30 to 21:30. The Ghost Market is Monday only, 04:00 to 07:00. Nothing runs on a Tuesday.
Is the porcelain at Taoxichuan authentic studio work?
Yes, by rule. Stallholders must hold a college diploma or above, the work must be their own original design, and factory goods are barred from applying. It is close to five hundred stalls a session, mostly young ceramicists selling their own studio output.
What is Jingdezhen's Ghost Market?
An antiques market on Shuguang Road that opens at 04:00 on Mondays and disperses at dawn, shopped by torchlight. Provenance cannot be established, so assume reproductions and buy only at prices you would pay for new work. Bring your own torch.
How do I get porcelain home?
Most stallholders pack and ship, and they are good at it - agree it at the stall. Otherwise use EMS or SF Express yourself, and keep the receipt, because being able to show a piece was bought new from a named maker is useful at customs.
