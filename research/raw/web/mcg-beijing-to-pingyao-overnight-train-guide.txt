Pingyao Has Two Stations and the Fast Train Goes to the Wrong One

Michael Chen · Travel Writer, China Specialist · Updated September 27, 2026

Pingyao has two railway stations twelve kilometres apart, and the fast train goes to the wrong one.
That single fact decides this journey. The G-train reaches Pingyao Gaoxinqu in three and a half hours and leaves you outside town, negotiating with drivers who know what tourists pay. The K601 sleeper takes eleven hours overnight and puts you at Pingyao Station, the old downtown one, 1.5 km from the Ancient City south gate — and you sleep through the whole thing instead of paying for a hotel night.

K601 and K609 are interchangeable; K601 is marginally faster and runs more consistently. Both arrive at the right station. Hard seat exists at ¥95 and I would not put a friend through eleven fluorescent-lit hours of it.
Take the G-train anyway if you have luggage you cannot carry down a sleeper corridor, if overnight trains make you ill, or if you are travelling with small children. Those are real reasons. Speed is not one, once you count the transfer at the far end.

The sleeper is cheaper before you even add the pass to the other column, and it hands you a full extra day in Shanxi. Soft sleeper adds ¥145 for a four-berth cabin with a door — worth it if you are tall, travelling as a couple, or cannot sleep among strangers.
Which berth
Hard sleeper is six berths to a bay in stacks of three, open to the corridor. The tiers are not priced identically in practice and they are not equivalent.
Lower (下铺) is the one to want: you can sit upright, your bag goes underneath, no climbing. It sells out first.

Upper (上铺) is cheapest and involves hauling yourself up at 23:00 after dinner.

Middle (中铺) is the worst of both — no headroom to sit, still a climb. Take it last.

There is no dining car on K601. Bring food, water, earplugs and an eye mask; the carriage lights stay on later than you want. There is hot water at the end of each carriage, which is why everyone around you is eating instant noodles. Toilets sit at both ends — one squat, one Western, cold water only — and the paper runs out by the second half of the night, so carry your own. Two alarms for 06:45. If you sleep through it, the next stop is Jiexiu, thirty minutes on, and a return ticket back to Pingyao costs ¥12–20 bought on the spot.
Booking it
Both routes work with a foreign passport. The official 12306 English site and app take a passport number directly with no Chinese ID and no booking fee — enter the name exactly as printed, because the passport is the boarding credential and a typo becomes a gate problem. If the site will not accept the passenger record, a staffed ticket window sorts it out; that is a fallback, not a step everyone has to do. The full 12306 walkthrough covers the flow.
Trip.com charges ¥20–40 a ticket and is the easier first booking. Either way you board by scanning the passport at the gate.
Book seven days ahead in a normal week and 20 to 30 days for Spring Festival, Golden Week or May Day. Lower berths go first. Booking early costs nothing to change your mind about: cancel more than eight days out and the fee is zero. The refund ladder has the rest of the tiers.
07:38, and the men shouting at you
You step off into grey light, and there is a wall of touts inside and outside the station quoting ¥100 to the Ancient City. It is 1.5 km away.
Walk past them to the metered rank on the station square, usually to the left as you exit. On the meter it is ¥15–25. A flat ¥30 offer is fair enough.

Or walk it — 15 to 20 minutes through quiet morning streets, which is a better start to the day than an argument.

Didi covers Pingyao and runs ¥18–25.

Bus 108 goes to the Ancient City for ¥2, slow and cramped with luggage.

Keep ¥100–200 in small notes. Station-rank drivers here often want cash and some will not take a foreign-linked mobile payment. Download offline Amap before you arrive — signal around the station is patchy.
Inside the walls
Walking the Ancient City is free. The ¥125 three-day pass is what gets you onto the wall and into roughly twenty sights, and it runs three consecutive days from first use. Buy it at the south gate ticket office.
Two days is the right length. Day one: the 6 km wall circuit on foot, two to three hours, then Rishengchang — the Qing-era draft bank where Chinese banking as a system was invented — and dinner in a courtyard inside the walls. Day two: out to the Qiao Family Courtyard (乔家大院), 30 km away and the set for Raise the Red Lantern, on the public bus at ¥15 each way. Or Shuanglin Temple, 6 km out, for the painted statues, if you would rather not spend the day on a bus.
Coming from or going back to the capital, the Beijing guide covers the other end.
FAQ
Why not take the high-speed train to Pingyao?
Because it goes to Pingyao Gaoxinqu, 12 km outside the Ancient City, which adds a ¥80–100 taxi and a negotiation. The K601 sleeper arrives at the old downtown station, 1.5 km from the south gate, and saves you a hotel night on top.
How much is the Beijing to Pingyao sleeper?
¥230 for a hard sleeper berth on the K601 and ¥375 for a soft sleeper cabin. Hard seat is ¥95 and is not worth eleven overnight hours.
Can foreigners book the K601 online?
Yes. The official 12306 English site takes a foreign passport with no booking fee; Trip.com charges ¥20–40 and is simpler for a first booking. You board by scanning the same passport at the gate.
How far is Pingyao Station from the Ancient City?
About 1.5 km, or 15 to 20 minutes on foot. A metered taxi is ¥15–25 and a Didi ¥18–25. The men quoting ¥100 inside the station are not the official rank — walk out to the square.
