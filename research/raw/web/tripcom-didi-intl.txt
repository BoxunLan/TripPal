By Abagail

Didi Taxi: what is it?

Photo source: yicaiglobal.com

DiDi, officially known as Didi Chuxing, is a leading Chinese mobility platform offering services similar to Uber or Grab. Its China platform covers ride-hailing and several other transport services.
DiDi was founded in 2012 and achieved market leadership in just a few years. In 2015, DiDi merged with rival Kuaidi Dache, and a year later, it acquired Uber's China operations. Today, DiDi is one of China's largest ride-hailing platforms and remains a major part of the country's urban transport network.
The official DiDi China website lists services including Express, Premier, Luxe, Taxi, Hitch, Bus, chauffeur services, Qingju bike sharing, freight, and car rental.

What Services Does the DiDi Taxi App Offer?

Photo source: miracuves.com
The DiDi app offers several ride and transport options, although availability varies by city.
DiDi Taxi: Connects passengers with licensed taxis through the DiDi platform.
DiDi Express: A budget-friendly private ride-hailing option for everyday journeys. Carpooling is available separately rather than being the main purpose of Express.

DiDi Chauffeur: Allows car owners to hire a driver.
DiDi Bus: Provides bus-related transport services.
Qingju: DiDi's bike and e-bike sharing service.
DiDi Car Rental: Car rental services are also available through the wider DiDi platform.

DiDi China for International travellers App: How to Use It
How to use DiDi in China as a foreigner? There are 2 ways to use the DiDi app on your phone.
Option 1: DiDi China App Download: Which DiDi App to Use in China?
For mainland China, international visitors should use the official DiDi China: Ride Hailing app, also referred to as DiDi-Greater China in official Chinese travel guidance. It supports international mobile numbers, an English interface, bilingual in-app messaging, and international bank cards.
China's 2026 guidance for foreign travellers specifically recommends this version and confirms that it supports registration with foreign mobile numbers and international credit cards.
Step 1. Download the DiDi app
Go to the Apple App Store or Google Play and search for DiDi China: Ride Hailing. If the name appears as DiDi-Greater China in official guidance or on your device, it refers to the mainland China version of DiDi.
Step 2. Set Up Your Account With a Phone Number
Register using your mobile number and verify it with the SMS code. Foreign phone numbers are supported, so travellers from the UAE can register using a +971 UAE mobile number.
If possible, complete registration before leaving the UAE and make sure your UAE number can still receive verification messages while abroad.
Step 3. Add a Payment Method
Add a supported international credit card to the app. The DiDi-Greater China app supports international credit cards for foreign travellers, so a UAE-issued card can be used if it is accepted by the platform and your bank authorizes the transaction.
You can also set up Alipay or WeChat Pay with an international bank card before your trip.
Step 4. How to Book a Taxi Using the DiDi App
Just like other apps, DiDi will automatically detect your pickup location, but you can also change it on the map. You can enter your destination address or select it directly on the map.
Step 5. Communicating With Your Driver
Once you've booked, you can contact your driver through the app. In-app messaging is particularly useful if you don't speak Chinese.
Save the Chinese version of your hotel or destination address so you can show it to the driver if needed. If your driver needs to contact your registered UAE number by phone, international roaming may be required; otherwise, use DiDi's in-app messaging where available.
Step 6. Ending Your Trip and Checking the Receipt
Once you arrive at your destination, simply hop out of the taxi. The fare will be charged automatically based on your chosen payment method. You can check your trip details and get a receipt via the app or request an invoice if you need to submit it for expense reimbursement.
Option 2: Use Ride-Hailing Through Alipay Without Downloading DiDi
If you already use Alipay, you can book an online ride without downloading another ride-hailing app. China's current guidance for international travellers recommends opening Alipay → Transport → Taxi, then entering your pickup location and destination.
This is often convenient for UAE visitors who have already set up Alipay with an international bank card.

DiDi China for International Travellers: Price and Ride Costs

Photo source: unsplash

DiDi is often affordable, but it is not always cheaper than a regular taxi. Your DiDi China for international travellers price depends on the city, distance, ride category, traffic, tolls, and demand at the time of booking.
Express is generally one of the more budget-friendly options, while Premier and Luxe cost more. DiDi can also use dynamic pricing, so fares may rise during rush hour, bad weather, major events, or periods of high demand.
Check the estimated fare in the app before confirming your ride. Prices are displayed in Chinese yuan (CNY), while the final AED amount on a UAE-issued card will depend on your bank's exchange rate and any overseas transaction fees.

What are the Alternatives to Didi Taxi?

If you can't book a DiDi ride, you can use an official city taxi. Official taxis are licensed and metered, although fares vary by city.
In Beijing, the current official starting fare is CNY 13 for the first 3 km, plus a CNY 1 fuel surcharge.
In Shanghai, standard taxis currently start at CNY 14 for the first 3 km, while some larger or electric taxi models start at CNY 16.
RMB cash remains useful for taxis, and mobile payments are widely used. Direct international card acceptance varies by vehicle and operator, so don't rely on every street taxi accepting a UAE-issued card.

Useful Tips for Taking Taxis in China
Save Addresses in Chinese
Show the driver the name in characters—this will save you from any misunderstandings. It's especially useful for hotels, landmarks, and ports.
When hopping into a regular city taxi, make sure the driver switches on the meter.
Taxi drivers might try to negotiate a fixed price, which is usually higher. If the meter isn't on, just ask them to turn it on or hop out of the car.
Check the Car's License Plate
Make sure to memorize or take a photo of the car's license plate before you hop in—it'll come in handy if you leave something behind or run into any issues.
Set Up Your Taxi App Before Leaving the UAE
Install DiDi-Greater China and register with your UAE phone number before your flight. Add your international payment method and save the Chinese addresses of your hotel and other important destinations.
DiDi requires mobile data for booking, location tracking, and communicating with drivers, so it is also worth setting up a China eSIM before you travel.
Additional tips:
Use DiDi-Greater China for direct DiDi bookings in mainland China.
If you prefer not to download DiDi, you can book ride-hailing through Alipay → Transport → Taxi or WeChat → Me → Services → Mobility/Ride Hailing.
During morning and evening rush hours, expect higher prices and longer waiting times.
Keep your hotel and destination names saved in Chinese.
At airports and railway stations, follow signs to the designated ride-hailing pickup area rather than requesting pickup from an unofficial or restricted location.

Often, but not always. DiDi Express can be competitive with regular taxis, but dynamic pricing may make it more expensive during peak periods. Compare the DiDi estimate with local metered taxi fares before booking.

How to avoid the 3% transaction fee in China?

The 3% fee mainly relates to some Alipay and WeChat Pay transactions made with linked international cards, rather than every DiDi ride. Current official payment guidance states that QR-code payments of CNY 200 or less with a linked international card do not incur the additional 3% service fee, while transactions above CNY 200 may. Payment rules can vary by product, so check the fee shown before confirming. Your UAE bank may also charge its own foreign transaction or currency-conversion fee.

What is the best Chinese taxi app for foreigners?

DiDi-Greater China is one of the most practical ride-hailing apps for foreigners because it supports foreign mobile numbers and international credit cards. Alipay and WeChat also offer ride-hailing options without requiring the standalone DiDi app.

What is the difference between DiDi Rider and DiDi China?

For travel in mainland China, use DiDi-Greater China, which is the version specifically recommended for foreign travellers in China's current transport guidance. DiDi-Rider is used in DiDi's international markets. DiDi states that the same DiDi account credentials can be used to sign in to DiDi-Rider, but visitors travelling within mainland China should use DiDi-Greater China or access ride-hailing through Alipay or WeChat.
