Xi'an Travel Guide 2026: Terracotta Warriors, Costs and Three Days

Two full days does Xi'an. Three if you want Hua Shan.
The Terracotta Warriors are the reason you came, and they are ¥120 from March to November, ¥60 the rest of the year, on timed entry you book ahead. Getting there is the part people overpay for: public bus 5 from Xi'an Railway Station costs ¥7 and drops you at the gate. Tour operators charge ¥300 and up for the same ride with a shopping stop attached.
The other thing to book early is the Shaanxi History Museum. Free, seven-day window, WeChat mini-program, and it sells out.

Terracotta Warriors: take the bus
Look for the green buses signed "Tourist Bus 5 (306)" outside Xi'an Railway Station. ¥7, about an hour, stops at the site entrance. Coming from Xi'an North, the high-speed station, ride metro Line 2 down to the railway station and walk the last ten minutes.
Arrive by 8:15 for the 8:30 opening. Do that and Pit 1 — the one on every postcard — stays close to empty for the first 45 minutes, before the coaches turn up. The other window is after 15:30, once the tour groups have gone. Budget three to four hours inside.
Tickets. Peak season (March 1 – November 30) is ¥120. Low season (December 1 – end of February) is ¥60. The ticket includes all three pits plus the separate Lishan bronze chariots hall. Children under 1.2m get in free. You buy through the official WeChat mini-program "秦始皇帝陵博物院" (search it inside WeChat) or through Trip.com if you want an English interface. Tickets are timed entry and they do sell out on Saturdays in May and October — book two days ahead.
What to skip. The wax figure halls, the "Pit of the Horses" VR experience, and the jade shops on the walk-in path. The only thing worth extra time is the Lishan bronze chariots exhibit, which is included in your ticket and criminally underrated.
Eating in the Muslim Quarter
Where to walk. Enter from the north side of the Drum Tower and keep going north. After about 200m the crowds thin and the prices drop. Turn right onto Xiyangshi Street or further north onto Dapiyuan for the local-priced stalls.
What to order. A short list of things every foreign friend I bring loves:
Roujiamo (肉夹馍) — a crispy baked bun stuffed with slow-cooked beef (most Xi'an stalls do beef, not pork, because they're Muslim-operated). ¥10–15. Look for the stall with a queue of Chinese college students.

Biang biang noodles (biangbiang面) — hand-pulled belt noodles topped with chili oil, garlic, and tomato-egg or pork sauce. ¥15–30. The character "biang" itself has 57 strokes and isn't in most dictionaries.

Yangrou paomo (羊肉泡馍) — mutton soup with chunks of flatbread you tear by hand at the table. A full bowl is ¥35–58. Lao Sun Jia on Dongda Street is the famous version; any local shop does it for ¥30.

Liangpi (凉皮) — cold rice noodles with sesame paste, cucumber, and chili oil. ¥10–15. The ideal summer Xi'an lunch.

Persimmon cake (柿子饼) — deep-fried sweet persimmon fritter with walnut and osmanthus filling. ¥5 each.

Lamb skewers (羊肉串) — ¥5–8 per skewer. Order from the stalls where the grill master is already working, not the ones waving menus at foreigners.

Pomegranate juice (石榴汁) — fresh-pressed. ¥15 is honest, ¥25 is tourist tax, ¥40 is a scam.

What to avoid. Anything called "Eight Treasure Porridge" that costs ¥80, "Xi'an Famous Mystery Snack Box" for ¥150, and photo-menu restaurants with English touts at the door. The guy yelling "friend, friend" in English is always more expensive than the silent stall next to him.
The main drag (Beiyuanmen, Xiyangshi, Beiguangji Street) is the most touristy and where prices and quality slip. Locals eat one block deeper: along Dapiyuan and Daxuexi Lane, and especially at Sajin Bridge (Sajinqiao) to the northwest, where stalls are mostly Xi'an residents and a full meal runs about 50 RMB per person. Trusted local picks include Pangzi sticky rice cake (mornings only), Lao Jin's egg roujiamo, Jia Wu's crispy beef pies, Zhiliang soup dumplings, and Li Weiyi spicy meatball pepper soup; on the main street the long-standing names worth the crowd are Ding Family braised pork (Dingjia xiao su rou), Lao Ma small-stir paomo, and Jia San soup dumplings.
If you are working out what any of this should cost before you order, what street food actually costs across Chinese cities is the reference — the Quarter sits at the upper end of normal, not at tourist-trap prices, provided you walk past the first fifty metres. And for how Shaanxi food fits into the wider picture, the regional cuisine guide explains why the Northwest is not one of the Eight Great Cuisines and why that says nothing about how good it is.

The no-backtracking version of this route lays out the walking distances, which matter more than you'd think — the City Wall is 13.74 km around, about 100 minutes of unhurried pedalling.
Day 1: Bell Tower, Muslim Quarter, City Wall bike
Morning: walk to the Bell Tower (Zhonglou), climb up for ¥40 (combo with Drum Tower is ¥50 and worth it). You get views straight down Xi'an's four main avenues and a photo of the wooden pagoda most people never actually climb.
Lunch: cross the underpass into the Muslim Quarter (Huimin Jie). Start at the main drag but walk two blocks north into Beiyuanmen Street — prices drop 30% and the food is better. Roujiamo (Chinese hamburger) is ¥10 at a stall and ¥15 at the shiny chain. Biang biang noodles run ¥15–30 depending on toppings. Get a lamb skewer (¥5–8) and a glass of pomegranate juice (¥15, honest; ¥25, tourist trap).
Afternoon: enter the City Wall at the South Gate (Yongningmen). Entry is ¥54. Bike rental is a separate ¥45 for 2 hours, ¥90 tandem. The wall is 13.74 km around and it takes about 100 minutes of easy pedaling to complete the loop. Do it. You'll thank me at sunset.
Evening: walk to Yongxingfang, the food street just inside the wall at the east gate area. The "knife-cut noodles tossed from a chef on stilts" show is touristy but the noodles are genuinely good (¥25).
Day 2: Terracotta Warriors day trip
Full day as described above. Get back to the city by 4pm, nap, then go to the Small Wild Goose Pagoda area (Xi'an Museum entry is free but you need your passport at the ticket window). This museum is underrated — it holds 130,000 artifacts and is usually empty.
Dinner: De Fa Chang on Bell Tower Square. This is the real dumpling banquet restaurant, founded in 1936. The 18-dumpling tasting banquet is ¥200/person and they're genuinely different fillings shaped like walnuts, ducks, and fish. Book through WeChat or just show up before 6pm.
Day 3: Shaanxi History Museum, Big Wild Goose Pagoda, HSR out
Morning: Shaanxi History Museum (陕西历史博物馆). The base ticket is free but requires advance booking on the official WeChat mini-program — release is 9am, 7 days ahead, and they go in minutes. If you miss the free ticket, the Grand Tang Treasures Hall (珍宝馆) is ¥30 and you can walk up. It's worth it.
Lunch: anywhere along Xiaozhai Road. Try a bowl of yangrou paomo (mutton soup with torn flatbread) at Lao Sun Jia. ¥58.
Afternoon: Big Wild Goose Pagoda (大雁塔). Entry to the temple courtyard is ¥40, climbing the pagoda itself is an extra ¥30. The surrounding Tang Paradise area is free to walk through and the fountain shows happen at 12:00, 14:00, 18:00, 20:00 and 21:00. Be there 20 minutes early for a spot.
Evening: head to Xi'an North Station (metro Line 2) for your next HSR leg.
The Shaanxi History Museum is free but the daily 12,000 tickets are the hardest reservation in the city: roughly 600,000 people compete for them each day. Tickets for the main hall release at exactly 17:00 five days in advance (so on the 26th you book for the 1st), and weekend slots can vanish within 15 seconds. Pre-save every traveler's name and ID under 'frequent visitors' in the museum's WeChat account a day ahead so you only have to tap 'book' at 17:00. If the main hall sells out, book the Qin and Han Dynasty branch (Qin-Han Hall) instead, which is easier to reserve and now holds many relics moved from the old building.
City Wall bike rental is 45 RMB for three hours on a single bike and 90 RMB for a tandem, plus a refundable 100 RMB deposit. The full 13.7 km loop takes 2 to 3 hours, so the base package usually covers it, but overtime is charged per 10 minutes (5 RMB single, 10 RMB tandem). Rent at the South Gate (Yongning Gate) service points on the east and west sides, open 8:00 to 22:00; after 20:00 you can only return bikes at those South Gate points. Children under 12 and adults over 65 are not allowed to ride.
Day trips
If you have an extra day and want something beyond the wall, here are three honest options:
Mount Huashan (华山) — 2 hours by HSR to Huashan North, then a free shuttle to the cable car. All-in day cost is roughly ¥380 including HSR, entry (¥160), and round-trip cable car (¥140). Go if you like mountains with chain-supported cliff walks.

Hanyangling Mausoleum (汉阳陵) — less famous than the Terracotta Warriors, half the crowds, and the glass-floor walkway over the pits is genuinely impressive. ¥80 entry. Take Bus 4 from Xi'an city center (~40 min).

Famen Temple (法门寺) — holds a finger bone of the Buddha. 2.5 hours by bus each way. Only worth it if you're into Tang Buddhist history.

Skip: the package tours that bundle "Warriors + Huaqing Palace + Banpo Village" into a 10-hour bus day. Huaqing Palace is ¥120 for a recreated Tang garden, Banpo is skippable unless you're a Neolithic archaeology nerd, and the "bus ride included" burns four hours at jade shops.
Ninety minutes further east, Luoyang has the Longmen Grottoes and is a timing puzzle rather than a ticket one: the night tour costs the same as the full daytime ticket and covers half the site, and booking opens at ten past midnight.

Apps you actually need in Xi'an
Alipay (支付宝) — pays for metro, buses, food stalls, tickets, everything. Link a Visa/Mastercard in the "Tour Pass" section.

WeChat (微信) — for the Shaanxi History Museum mini-program booking and the Terracotta Warriors mini-program booking. The booking functions are both easier inside WeChat than on browsers.

Amap (高德地图) or Baidu Maps — Google Maps is 500m+ inaccurate in China. Amap has an English interface and will route you to the correct Muslim Quarter alley.

Trip.com — HSR tickets, hotels, and Terracotta Warriors entry all in English.

Pleco — the menu translator dictionary. Aim your camera at a paper menu and read it in English.

A China eSIM like Airalo or Nomad keeps all of these working on arrival — activate before landing and you skip the Great Firewall entirely because these eSIMs route through foreign servers. Budget $8–20 for a week of 5GB.
The Shaanxi History Museum and the Terracotta Warriors both book through WeChat mini-programs, and the museum's 17:00 ticket scramble is far easier inside the app than on a browser. Set up a WeChat account before you arrive (foreign phone numbers can now register), and link an international card or save your passport details in advance so you are not typing them during the 30-second window when most slots disappear.
All of it assumes working mobile data, which is its own decision before you land — the connectivity options compares a local SIM against an eSIM that roams.
What goes wrong
Booking windows. Shaanxi History Museum is seven days out, the Warriors are timed entry. Both in WeChat.

The ¥300 "tour" to the Warriors. Bus 5 is ¥7 and takes the same road.

Peak season pricing. The Warriors double to ¥120 from March 1 through November 30.

Arriving at 11am. Pit 1 is calm at opening and after 15:30, and packed in between.

Google Maps. It is offset by hundreds of metres here. Use Amap.

FAQ
How do I get from Beijing to Xi'an?
The G-series high-speed train from Beijing West to Xi'an North: 4h 15m, ¥515 second class. Book three to five days ahead on 12306 to get a fast train rather than an overnight slow one.
Do I need to book the Terracotta Warriors in advance?
Yes — entry is timed. Tickets are ¥120 from March 1 to November 30 and ¥60 outside that. Book through the official mini-program or Trip.com.
How do I reach the Terracotta Warriors on public transport?
Tourist Bus 5, signed 306, from outside Xi'an Railway Station. ¥7, about an hour, stops at the site entrance. From Xi'an North ride metro Line 2 to the railway station first.
Where should budget travellers stay?
Hostels around South Gate — ¥40–100 a night, hot water, English-speaking staff, and you can walk onto the City Wall.
