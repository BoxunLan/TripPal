Macau Has a Free Bus Network Hiding in Plain Sight

M MyChinaGuide Editorial Team · Updated September 14, 2026

Macau has a free bus network and almost no visitor uses it deliberately. The casinos run more than a hundred shuttle routes between the border crossings, the ferry terminals and their own hotels, every ten to twenty minutes, and the overwhelming majority ask for nothing at all — no receipt, no room key, no intention to gamble.
Learn which shuttle goes where and you can cross the territory all day without paying a fare or touching the public bus system, which is the one thing in Macau that genuinely inconveniences visitors.
How the shuttles actually work
The routes connect four entry points to the resorts: the Gongbei border gate (关闸, Portas do Cerco) from Zhuhai, the Hengqin crossing, the Outer Harbour Ferry Terminal from Hong Kong, and the Taipa Ferry Terminal. Between them the big operators — Galaxy, Venetian, Parisian, Wynn, StarWorld, MGM, Sands — cover most places you would want to be.
Walk up and board. You are not asked to prove anything at the vast majority of stands.

Frequency is every 10 to 20 minutes, which means you plan by destination rather than by timetable.

Use them as connections, not as one journey. Border gate to a Cotai resort, then a second shuttle from that resort to wherever you actually want to be, is the normal pattern.

They stop running earlier than public transport, generally late evening. Check the last departure if you are out for dinner on the other side of the territory.

This matters more than it sounds because an Octopus card does not work here. It runs Hong Kong's MTR, buses, trams and ferries, and it is useless on Macau's buses and Light Rail — the Octopus guide sets out what does and does not cross the border. The free shuttles let you avoid the question entirely.

The currency trap
Macau's currency is the pataca (MOP). Hong Kong dollars are accepted almost everywhere at one to one, which sounds convenient and costs you a little each time, because a pataca is worth slightly less than a Hong Kong dollar.
The worse half is the change. Pay in HKD and you will be given patacas back, and patacas are not accepted in Hong Kong. A day of paying in HKD leaves you with a pocket of currency you can only spend before you cross back. If Macau is a day trip from Hong Kong, either pay by card, or spend your patacas deliberately before the ferry.
Two jurisdictions, not one
Macau runs its own immigration, separate from both Hong Kong and the mainland. Most Western passports enter visa-free for a stay set by nationality, and that entry has nothing to do with your Chinese visa status.
The direction that catches people is Macau into the mainland. Crossing at Gongbei or Hengqin is a full entry into China and needs the mainland's own arrangement — a visa, or one of the visa-free schemes if your passport qualifies. The visa-free routes sets out which applies to you, and note that a mainland entry used this way is an entry: a 240-hour transit clock starts when you cross.
Coming from Hong Kong is the easy direction — ferry or the bridge bus, and Hong Kong's own entry rules are separate again. Three jurisdictions, three sets of rules, on a journey you could do in an afternoon.
What to actually see
The casinos are not the point unless you want them to be. What makes Macau unusual is 440 years of Portuguese administration layered onto a Cantonese city, and it is concentrated in a small area you can walk.
The Historic Centre of Macau is a UNESCO site made of about thirty buildings and squares rather than one monument. Senado Square, St Dominic's, and the Ruins of St Paul's — which is a facade and nothing else, the rest having burned in 1835 — are the spine of it.

Coloane village at the southern end is what Macau looked like before the reclamation: two streets, a chapel, and the original Lord Stow's bakery where the Portuguese egg tart was reinvented in 1989.

Taipa Village for the food street, which is where locals eat rather than where the resorts point you.

On food: Macanese cooking is not Cantonese with Portuguese decoration. It is a genuinely separate cuisine built over four centuries — African chicken, minchi, bacalhau treated the Chinese way. The regional cuisine guide covers where Cantonese cooking sits; Macanese sits beside it, not inside it.
A day, or two
A day works from Hong Kong: ferry across, historic centre on foot in the morning, Taipa for lunch, Coloane in the afternoon, back in the evening. Two days lets you do Coloane properly and see the resorts lit at night, which is genuinely a spectacle whether or not you gamble.
If you are combining it with the mainland, the nine-day Hong Kong, Macau and Shenzhen route covers the border logistics in order.
FAQ
Can I ride Macau's casino shuttle buses without gambling?
Yes. The vast majority require no receipt, room key or proof of anything — they are free and open to the public. More than a hundred routes connect the border crossings and ferry terminals to the resorts every 10 to 20 minutes.
Does my Octopus card work in Macau?
No. Octopus covers Hong Kong's MTR, buses, trams and ferries but not Macau's buses or Light Rail. Either carry a Macau Pass and cash, or use the free casino shuttles, which sidestep the local network entirely.
Can I use Hong Kong dollars in Macau?
Yes, at one to one almost everywhere, which costs you a little because the pataca is worth slightly less. The real problem is change: you get patacas back, and patacas are not accepted in Hong Kong. Pay by card or spend the patacas before you cross back.
Do I need a Chinese visa for Macau?
No — Macau runs its own immigration and most Western passports enter visa-free. But crossing from Macau into the mainland at Gongbei or Hengqin is a full entry into China and needs a visa or a visa-free scheme your passport qualifies for.
