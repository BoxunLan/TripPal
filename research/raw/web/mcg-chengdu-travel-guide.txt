Chengdu is the city travelers keep extending. You come for the pandas, you stay for the tea houses, and somewhere around day four you realize you've been eating mapo tofu for breakfast and you're not even sorry. It moves slower than Shanghai and Beijing, eats better than both, and the day trips into the mountains around it are some of the best in China. This 2026 guide gives you a three-day plan, a Sichuan food cheatsheet that won't scorch you by accident, and the logistics to get from the airport to a panda enclosure before the good enclosures get crowded.

Why Chengdu is worth 3-4 days
Chengdu is the capital of Sichuan province and the gateway to western China. About 16 million people live here, but the city center feels smaller than that number suggests, built around a grid of tree-lined streets and teahouses that haven't modernized past the 1980s in any meaningful way. The climate is mild, the pace is gentle, and the food is — for a lot of travelers — the single best reason to come to China.
Three days is the minimum for a proper visit: one day for pandas and the old quarters, one day for a major day trip (Leshan or Dujiangyan), and one day for wandering, tea, and food. Four days lets you fit Leshan AND Dujiangyan without rushing. If you want to climb Emei Shan you'll need to add a day or two more.
Pandas: the Giant Panda Breeding Research Base
Let's get this out of the way first. Yes, you should go. Yes, you should go early. Yes, it's worth it.
Getting there
The Chengdu Research Base of Giant Panda Breeding (熊猫基地) is 10 km north of central Chengdu. Three ways to arrive:
Metro Line 3 to Panda Avenue station, then a free shuttle or a short Didi ride. Cheapest and most reliable, 40-50 minutes door to door from downtown.

Didi : ¥40-70 one-way from downtown, 25-40 minutes depending on traffic

Guided tour : Most hotels can book a half-day panda tour for ¥150-250 per person including transport and guide

Tickets and timing
Entry is ¥55. Book through the "Chengdu Panda" WeChat mini-program up to a week in advance (real-name registration required — use your passport). The base opens at 7:30 a.m. and the pandas are most active from 8:30 to 10:30 a.m. when they're eating breakfast. By noon they're mostly sleeping. If you arrive late you'll see piles of fluffy fur, not moving pandas.
Plan to arrive by 8 a.m. at the latest. Head directly to the Sunshine Nursery and Moonlight Nursery where the baby and sub-adult pandas live — these are the best enclosures. Red pandas are scattered throughout the grounds and are weirdly charming. Plan 3-4 hours total on site. The electric shuttles inside the base run ¥10-20 and save significant walking.
What to skip
The giant panda souvenir stalls at the exit are overpriced. The panda "experience" add-ons (feeding, close contact) that some tour operators sell have been largely discontinued at the main Chengdu base and are now only available through Dujiangyan's Panda Valley at much higher prices. Skip the upsell.

Jinli Ancient Street
Jinli sits next to Wuhou Shrine (武侯祠), a Three Kingdoms-era memorial temple (¥60 entry to the shrine, Jinli itself is free). The street is polished, touristy, and heavy on souvenir stalls, but the snack food is genuinely good — try three-cannon rice cakes (三大炮), chao shou wontons, and spicy rabbit head if you're brave. Go in the evening when the lanterns come on.
Kuanzhai Alley
Three parallel restored alleyways: Kuanxiangzi (wide), Zhaixiangzi (narrow), and Jingxiangzi (well). More upscale than Jinli, with proper sit-down teahouses, coffee shops, and boutique stores. A good mid-afternoon stop. Expect to pay ¥30-60 for tea compared to ¥10-20 at an old-school teahouse.
Wenshu Monastery
The most atmospheric active temple in central Chengdu. Entry is free. The vegetarian restaurant inside (Wenshu Yuan Vegetarian Hall) serves lunch for ¥40-80 per person and is worth the stop. Incense smoke, chanting, old trees — this is where Chengdu's religious tradition lives.
People's Park and teahouses
The real Chengdu experience isn't the polished alleyways — it's He Ming Teahouse (鹤鸣茶社) in People's Park. ¥15-25 for a bamboo chair, a glass of jasmine or bitter mountain tea, and as many refills as you want. People will walk up to clean your ears with metal picks for ¥30-50 (yes, this is a real thing, yes, locals love it). You can sit for four hours and nobody cares. This is the bit of Chengdu that no other Chinese city has.

Day trips from Chengdu
Chengdu is a better base than it gets credit for. Four day trips are all worth doing.
Leshan Giant Buddha
71 meters tall, carved into a cliff face in the 8th century, UNESCO-listed. 130 km south of Chengdu, 55-70 minutes by high-speed train. Entry ¥80. You can either walk the stairs down beside the Buddha (slow, crowded, better views) or take a boat tour from the river (¥70, easier, shorter experience). Most travelers do the walk.
Emei Shan
One of China's four sacred Buddhist mountains. 160 km south of Chengdu. You can do it as a very long day trip, but two days is better — climb or bus up on day one, sunrise at the Golden Summit (3,077 m) on day two, descend. Entry is ¥160 April-December, ¥90 in winter; add ¥120 for the cable car to the Golden Summit. Pair it with Leshan since they're in the same direction.
Dujiangyan
A 2,200-year-old irrigation system, still working, also UNESCO-listed. 60 km northwest of Chengdu, 30-45 minutes by high-speed train. ¥80 entry. You can combine Dujiangyan with Mt. Qingcheng in a single day. Dujiangyan also has its own Panda Valley (¥55 entry) which is quieter and has fewer tourists than the main Chengdu base.
Mt. Qingcheng (Qingcheng Shan)
The birthplace of Taoism. Quiet, forested, walkable. Entry ¥80 for the Front Mountain, ¥20 for the Back Mountain. 1.5 hours from Chengdu by direct train. Pairs perfectly with Dujiangyan for a single day combining water and mountain.
Sichuan food cheatsheet
Sichuan food is famous for being spicy, but that's only half the story. The real signature is mala (麻辣) — numbing and spicy — where Sichuan peppercorns create a tingly, buzzing mouthfeel that's distinct from chili heat. Not every dish is spicy, either. Come hungry and try everything.

Hotpot
Chengdu hotpot is the real deal — nine-grid pots of beef tallow broth loaded with Sichuan peppercorns, dried chilies, and fragrant spices, into which you dunk thin-sliced beef, duck intestine, tofu skin, lotus root, and mushrooms. Budget ¥80-150 per person at a mid-range chain like Shu Jiu Xiang (蜀九香), Xiao Long Kan (小龙坎), or Hai Di Lao (海底捞). Book ahead at dinner — the queue at good spots hits two hours on weekends.
Ask for the yuan yang pot (鸳鸯锅) with half spicy (红汤) and half mild (白汤) if you want to taste the buzz without burning out. The dipping sauce is usually sesame oil with garlic and cilantro — locals use it to cool down each bite.
Where to eat
Chen Mapo Tofu (陈麻婆豆腐) : The original mapo tofu restaurant, founded 1862. The Qinghua Road branch is the one to visit. ¥60-120 pp.

Shu Jiu Xiang (蜀九香) : Reliable hotpot chain, multiple branches. ¥100-150 pp.

Chen's Spicy Rabbit Head (陈记双流老妈兔头) : For the brave. ¥15-25 per head.

Long Chao Shou (龙抄手) : Classic wontons and snacks sampler, tourist-friendly. Chunxi Road branch. ¥40-80 pp.

Yu Zhi Lan (玉芝兰) : Sichuan fine dining, book 4-6 weeks ahead, ¥1500+ pp. Worth it if you can get in.

Most places accept Alipay and WeChat Pay . Small hotpot shops occasionally want cash; bring ¥200-300 in small bills just in case.
Airports, trains, and getting around
Airports
Chengdu has two airports. Know which one you're flying into.
Chengdu Tianfu International (TFU) : The new primary airport, opened 2021, 50 km south of the city. Metro Line 18 runs directly to Tianfu Square and downtown in 40-50 minutes for ¥8-12. Most international flights land here.

Chengdu Shuangliu International (CTU) : The older airport, 20 km southwest of downtown. Metro Line 10 connects to the main network. Still handles domestic and some international flights.

Didi from either airport runs ¥80-180 depending on time and distance.
Metro
Chengdu Metro is clean, English-signed, and cheap. Fares ¥2-9 depending on distance. Pay with Alipay QR or buy a single-journey token from machines. Lines 1, 2, 3, and 4 are the ones tourists use most. Lines 17 and 18 handle long-distance airport routes.
High-speed rail
Chengdu East Railway Station (成都东站) is the main HSR hub. Chengdu South handles some regional lines. Tickets are bookable on the 12306 app (official) or Trip.com (English, small fee). Bring your passport — tickets are real-name.
Payments, apps, and the setup you need
Alipay : Install before you arrive, add a Visa or Mastercard. Works in English. Use it for metro, taxis, restaurants, attractions, almost everything.

WeChat Pay : Backup payment method.

Amap (高德) : Maps and transit, much better than Google Maps (which is blocked anyway).

Didi : Ride-hail, available in English or through Alipay's mini-program.

Meituan and Dianping : For restaurant reviews and reservations. Dianping ratings reflect locals, not tourists.

Trip.com : Hotels, trains, tour bookings, all in English.

12306 : Official rail booking, clunky but works.

eSIM or local SIM before you arrive. Google, Instagram, WhatsApp, Facebook are all blocked — set up a VPN before landing. ExpressVPN and NordVPN work most of the time; travel eSIMs with passthrough save a headache.
Hotels and where to stay
Pick your Chengdu base by how much nightlife and food access you want.
Chunxi Road / Taikoo Li : Central, connected, walkable to food and shopping. Most international chains (The Temple House, Ritz-Carlton, Niccolo, Conrad) cluster here. Best pick for first-time visitors.

Tianfu Square : Central metro hub, slightly less atmosphere. Mid-range chains.

Jinli / Kuanzhai area : More boutique hotels and courtyard-style guesthouses. Picturesque, less convenient to the metro.

Wenshu Monastery area : Quiet, temple-adjacent, a few design hotels.

Budget runs ¥200-400 at chains like Atour, Hanting, or Ji Hotel. Mid-range ¥500-1000. Luxury ¥1500+.
FAQ
How many days do I need for Chengdu?
Three days covers the core: pandas, old town, one day trip (Leshan). Four days lets you add Dujiangyan and Mt. Qingcheng, or slow down. Add one or two more if you want to climb Emei Shan.
Is Sichuan food too spicy for me?
Probably not. The legendary heat comes from specific dishes (boiled beef, rabbit head, certain hotpots). Plenty of classics — tea-smoked duck, fish-fragrant pork, zhong dumplings — are mild. At restaurants you can ask for "bu la" (不辣, not spicy) or "wei la" (微辣, mild). Mapo tofu and kung pao chicken are usually manageable at medium heat.
When should I visit Chengdu?
March to June and September to November are the best windows. Spring is mild (15-24°C), autumn is crisp and dry. Summer is hot, humid, and rainy; winter is cool (4-10°C) and gray but rarely freezing. Avoid Chinese New Year (late January to mid-February) when many restaurants close.
Can I see pandas outside the Chengdu Panda Base?
Yes. Dujiangyan Panda Valley (¥55 entry) is quieter and has pandas in more spacious enclosures. Bifengxia near Ya'an (about 3 hours away) is even more remote. The Chengdu base is still the most convenient and has the biggest variety including red pandas and babies.
Is the Leshan Giant Buddha worth a day trip?
Yes. The scale is impossible to photograph — you have to stand at its feet to get it. Pair it with Emei Shan if you have an extra day, or do it as a quick round trip with an afternoon back in Chengdu.
How do I book a hotpot restaurant?
Walk-in works for early dinner (5:30 p.m.). For prime time (7-9 p.m.), book through Dianping's reservation system, Meituan, or have your hotel call ahead. Popular chains (Shu Jiu Xiang, Xiao Long Kan) run 90-minute queues on weekends.
