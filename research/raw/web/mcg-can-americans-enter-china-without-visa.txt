Can Americans Enter China Without a Visa? What Happens When You Land

Sarah Liu · Cultural Guide, Food Explorer · Updated September 27, 2026

Yes. A US passport gets you into mainland China without a visa on two routes: the 240-hour visa-free transit, which needs a confirmed onward ticket to a third country or region, and Hainan's 30-day visa-free entry, which keeps you on the island. Which route fits your trip, and the conditions for each , is covered in a separate guide. This one covers the day you arrive: the arrival card, the border check, registering where you stay, and what overstaying costs.

Before You Land: File the Arrival Card
Since 20 November 2025 you can fill in China's arrival card online before you fly, on the National Immigration Administration (NIA) website, the NIA 12367 app, or the NIA 12367 mini program in WeChat or Alipay. If you don't do it in advance, you can still complete it at the airport: scan the QR code in the immigration hall, use the self-service machines there, or fill in a paper card.
At the Border
Queue at the counters for foreign passports, or at a visa-free transit counter if the airport has one. Have these ready:
Your passport, valid for at least three months. The US State Department lists six months for China, so renew if you are close.

Your onward ticket to a third country or region, with a confirmed date and seat, leaving within 240 hours. A printed copy or the e-ticket on your phone both work.

Your arrival card, filed online or on paper.

The address of your first night's accommodation, in case the officer asks where you are staying.

If everything checks out, the officer grants a temporary entry permit and stamps your passport. Check the stamp before you leave the desk, then collect your bags and go through customs.
How long is 240 hours in practice
240 hours is exactly 10 days. The clock starts at 00:00 on the day after your arrival. If you land at 16:00 on Friday, your 240-hour window begins at 00:00 Saturday and ends at 00:00 the following Tuesday (day 11). A flight leaving at 23:59 on the final Monday is still legal.
Why people are turned away
The officer will not admit you on visa-free transit if:
Your onward ticket goes back to the country you flew in from. Los Angeles to Shanghai to Los Angeles is a round trip, not transit; build Hong Kong or Tokyo into the return instead.

Your onward flight leaves after your 240 hours run out.

You land at an airport that is not one of the 65 approved ports. Most big ones are on the list; check smaller ones before you book.

Your passport has less than three months of validity left, or you have a record of illegal entry, stay or work in China in the past five years.

Register Where You Stay Within 24 Hours
Hotels register foreign guests with the police at check-in, so in a hotel there is nothing else to do. Anywhere else, such as a rented apartment or a friend's home, you or your host must register the stay with the local police within 24 hours of arriving, and again each time you move.
Since 21 September 2026 you can do this online anywhere in China, through the NIA website, the NIA 12367 app, or its WeChat or Alipay mini program. Online registration has the same legal effect as registering in person, and the local police station still accepts it the old way. Skipping registration can bring a warning and a fine of up to ¥2,000 under Article 76 of the Exit and Entry Administration Law.
If You Overstay
Your 240 hours do not stretch. If illness, a cancelled flight or another emergency will keep you in China past the deadline, apply to the exit-entry office of the local public security bureau for a stay permit before it expires.
Staying on without one is illegal stay, and so is traveling outside the permitted areas. Under Article 78 of the Exit and Entry Administration Law, illegal stay brings a warning and, in serious cases, a fine of ¥500 a day up to ¥10,000, or 5 to 15 days' detention. Anyone repatriated for it can be barred from re-entering China for one to five years (Article 62). Book your onward flight with a day to spare.
Other Ways In
Hainan's 30-day visa-free entry is for arrivals through a port in Hainan, and you must stay in the province; the route guide sets out its conditions. Hong Kong and Macau run their own immigration checks, separate from the mainland's, and US passports get 90 days in Hong Kong without a visa. If neither route fits your trip, you need a visa before you fly: a 10-year multiple-entry visa costs $140 at the current reduced rate.
Before You Leave the Airport
You will want a working payment app and mobile data from the moment you walk out, for a taxi, the metro or your first meal. Set up Alipay with your US card and a way to get online before you fly.
Frequently Asked Questions
If I have a 10-year China visa, can I still use the 240-hour transit?
There is no need. Enter on your visa: the stay allowed on each entry is printed on it, and you do not need an onward ticket to a third country.
Does my 9-year-old US citizen daughter need her own visa or transit status?
She needs her own passport, and the officer checks each traveler, children included, against the same conditions, including an onward ticket. There is no fee for visa-free entry at any age.
What if my visa-free transit is denied at the border?
The border police will order you to return, and you wait in the airport's restricted area until you leave (Article 26 of the Exit and Entry Administration Law). A port visa is only for urgent trips backed by documents such as an invitation letter, so it is not a fallback for a tourist whose ticket does not qualify. Before you fly, check that your onward ticket goes to a different country or region from the one you are flying in from.
What if I lose my passport in China?
Report the loss at the nearest police station and apply for a replacement at the US Embassy in Beijing or the consulate that covers your area (Guangzhou, Shanghai, Shenyang or Wuhan). When you leave, border officers check your identity, your entry record or loss report, and the documents from the embassy; if you have not overstayed, you can exit on the new passport.
Is a dual US-Chinese citizen allowed to use the 240-hour transit?
Not if China considers you a Chinese citizen. China does not recognize dual nationality, and visa-free transit is for foreign nationals. If you enter on a Chinese passport or other Chinese travel document, the authorities treat you as a Chinese citizen only. If you are unsure how China sees your status, ask the Chinese Embassy before you book.
Do I need travel insurance to enter China on a transit stamp?
No. Insurance is not a condition of visa-free entry. It is still worth having: the US State Department notes that Medicare does not cover you abroad, most hospitals overseas do not accept US health insurance, and public hospitals in China ask foreigners to pay before treatment.
What documents should I photocopy before flying?
Bring a paper copy of your passport photo page, your onward ticket and your first hotel booking, and store the copies separately from the originals. A photo of each on your phone helps too. If your passport is lost or stolen, copies make the police report and the replacement easier.
