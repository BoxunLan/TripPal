Does Telegram Work in China? The 2026 Reality Check

Jenny Wang · Local Expert, Beijing Native · Updated September 14, 2026

No. Telegram is blocked in mainland China and has been since 2015, when the Great Firewall cut it off using IP blacklisting, DNS poisoning, and deep packet inspection. Nothing gets through on a local Chinese SIM or hotel Wi-Fi: messages will not send, chats will not load, and calls will not connect. To keep using Telegram in 2026 you need one of two things set up before you land: a VPN with obfuscated servers that still works inside China, or a travel eSIM whose international roaming data bypasses the Firewall entirely, no VPN required. Telegram's built-in MTProto proxies technically exist, but proxy IPs get blocked within days, so treat them as unreliable. WhatsApp, iMessage, and email each behave differently in China - iMessage actually works without any workaround - so pick your family check-in channel before your flight, not after you arrive.
If Telegram is where your group chats, family threads, or work channels live, this needs solving before your plane boards, not after. Here's exactly what happens, what works, and what to tell the people back home.
Why China blocks Telegram
Telegram has been on the wrong side of the Great Firewall longer than almost any other messenger. The block dates to mid-2015, after Chinese human rights lawyers used Telegram to organize - the service was hit by a massive DDoS attack that Telegram traced to Asia, and mainland access was cut around the same time. It never came back. The app's later role in coordinating the 2019 Hong Kong protests cemented its status: from Beijing's perspective, an encrypted messenger with anonymous public channels is exactly what the Firewall exists to keep out.
That's the same core reason WhatsApp and Signal are blocked - end-to-end encryption means Chinese authorities can't monitor content, and platforms that won't localize data or cooperate with content rules get walled off. Domestic apps like WeChat, which do comply, fill the space instead.
Knowing the "why" helps set expectations: this is not a glitch or a temporary outage that might lift during your trip. The block is deliberate, stable, and a decade old. Plan around it.

What actually happens when you open Telegram in China
Worth spelling out, because Telegram fails in a confusing way. The app doesn't show a clean "blocked in your region" banner. Instead:
The connection status sticks on "Connecting..." indefinitely.

Cached chats display normally, which fools people into thinking it half-works.

New messages neither send nor arrive. Outgoing ones sit with a clock icon.

Voice and video calls fail to establish at all.

Telegram's website, where you'd download the app or manage your account, is also blocked.

The cruel part: your family sees your account as simply silent. Messages they send show as delivered to the server but never reach your phone. More than one client has had a mild panic at home because "she read nothing for three days." If Telegram is your main family channel, agree on a backup before you fly.
This behavior is identical on China Mobile, China Unicom, and China Telecom SIMs, on hotel Wi-Fi, and on cafe Wi-Fi. There is no Chinese network that lets Telegram through.
Fix one: a VPN that still works inside China
A working VPN restores Telegram completely - messages, media, channels, voice and video calls, all of it. The catch is the phrase "working VPN." China actively blocks VPN protocols too, so most consumer VPNs fail on the mainland. You need a provider that maintains obfuscated servers designed to pass the Firewall's deep packet inspection, and there are only a handful worth trusting; our tested and regularly updated shortlist is in the best VPN for China guide .
Two rules my clients hear from me every single season:
Install and test the VPN before you land. VPN provider websites are themselves blocked in China, so you often can't download or subscribe once you're inside. Set it up at home, log in, confirm Telegram connects through it.

Have a second VPN as backup. Firewall pressure comes in waves - around major political events, even good providers have rough days. Two independent apps cost little and save the trip.

With a decent VPN through a Hong Kong, Japan, or Singapore server, Telegram text is instant and voice calls are perfectly usable - expect a slight delay similar to any international call, since your traffic makes an encrypted detour out of the country.
What about Telegram's built-in proxies?
Telegram has a native proxy feature (MTProto and SOCKS5) that connects the app through a third-party relay without a full VPN, and you'll find lists of free proxy servers floating around online. Some users inside China do use them.
My honest 2026 assessment for a traveler: don't rely on this. Public proxy IPs are exactly what the Firewall is good at finding, and popular ones get blocked within days - sometimes hours - of being shared. What connects tonight is dead by the weekend, and then you're hunting for fresh server details from inside a network that blocks the places you'd search. The proxies also cover only Telegram, leaving Google, Instagram, and everything else on the blocked apps list unreachable. Treat built-in proxies as a curiosity, not a plan; a proper VPN or roaming eSIM does the same job dependably.
Fix two: the eSIM roaming exception
Here's the workaround I recommend to most short-term visitors because it involves zero fiddling: international roaming data is not filtered by the Great Firewall. When you use a travel eSIM or your home carrier's roaming, your data traffic routes back through a gateway outside mainland China - Telegram, and everything else, simply works the moment you land, exactly like at home.
The practical details:
A 10-20 GB China travel eSIM runs roughly ¥110-180 / $15-25 and covers messaging for weeks; Telegram text and photos sip data, and even voice calls use only about 30 MB per hour.

The exemption applies only to cellular data . The moment your phone joins hotel Wi-Fi, you're behind the Firewall and Telegram goes silent again. Turn Wi-Fi off, or set Telegram to use mobile data.

Buy and install the eSIM before departure - see the best eSIM for China comparison for current options.

For most tourists, eSIM for connectivity plus offline resources for everything else is the whole formula. It's also why the MyChinaGuide app keeps its guides and tutorials available fully offline - the moments you need help most are exactly the moments the network fails you.
Telegram vs WhatsApp: same wall, same fixes
Honestly, 8 out of 10 clients ask some version of "fine, Telegram is blocked - what about WhatsApp?" Short answer: same situation. WhatsApp has been blocked since September 2017, when China sealed it off ahead of a Communist Party congress, and it fails on Chinese networks in the same silent, confusing way. The same two fixes - working VPN or roaming eSIM data - restore both apps equally.
The one practical difference is reach: WhatsApp's enormous global user base means it's likelier your hotel contact, tour guide, or driver abroad uses it, while Telegram skews toward group channels and communities. Either way, don't burn energy choosing between them for China - they live or die by the same workaround. Keeping family updated without Telegram
Build a fallback plan on channels that work in China natively, no VPN needed:
iMessage (iPhone to iPhone) works. Apple services are not blocked in mainland China - blue-bubble texts go through on any network, though large photos and videos can crawl. This is the single most useful fact for iPhone families: your daily "all good, in Xi'an now" needs zero setup.

Email works - unless it's Gmail. Google services are blocked, so Gmail needs the same VPN as Telegram. Outlook, iCloud Mail, and most non-Google providers load normally. If your family runs on Gmail, set up a temporary iCloud or Outlook address and share it before you go.

WeChat works everywhere, obviously. It's China's default for everything, foreign phone numbers can register, and messages to relatives who also install WeChat go through instantly. Worth setting up before arrival for restaurants and guides anyway - just know video calls are not encrypted end-to-end, so keep it to logistics and check-ins.

Plain SMS and phone calls work over roaming. Old-fashioned, slightly pricey, completely reliable for emergencies.

My standard advice: agree on one primary channel and one backup with your family, test both before departure, and tell them the plan - "if I go quiet on Telegram, check iMessage" prevents a lot of transatlantic worry.
Frequently asked questions
Will Telegram work in China on international roaming?
Yes. Roaming data - from a travel eSIM or your home carrier - routes through a gateway outside mainland China, so the Great Firewall never touches it. Telegram works normally on cellular data with no VPN. Just remember hotel Wi-Fi is still filtered, so keep Telegram on mobile data.
Can I download Telegram or a VPN after I arrive in China?
Assume no. Telegram's site, Google Play, and most VPN websites are blocked, and app stores inside China don't carry these apps. Install Telegram, your VPN (plus a backup), and your eSIM before departure, and test that everything connects.
Do Telegram voice and video calls work over a VPN in China?
Yes. With a reliable VPN through a nearby server (Hong Kong, Japan, Singapore), text is instant and voice calls are clear with minor delay. Video calls work but depend on your hotel bandwidth plus VPN overhead - if a call stutters, switching servers usually helps more than waiting.
Is it legal for a tourist to use Telegram over a VPN in China?
China's rules target VPN providers, not individual tourists, and enforcement against visitors checking messages is effectively unheard of. Millions of foreign residents do exactly this daily. Be sensible: it's a gray zone, not an invitation to run public channels commenting on Chinese politics from a Beijing hotel.
