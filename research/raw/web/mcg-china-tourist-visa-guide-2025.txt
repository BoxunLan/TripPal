China Tourist Visa Guide 2026: L Visa, Fees, Processing Time, Documents

The China L tourist visa in 2026 is the same L visa it has been for a decade: a sticker in your passport authorizing single, double, or multiple entries for tourism. What has changed is the paperwork around it. Applications now start online, US applicants no longer submit flight or hotel bookings, and US citizens can get a multiple-entry visa valid for up to 10 years for $140 through December 31, 2026.
Visa-free options have grown too. Citizens of 50 countries, including the UK, Canada, Australia, Japan, and most of the EU, can visit for up to 30 days without a visa, and 57 nationalities, Americans included, can use 240-hour visa-free transit when flying on to a third country. If you are American and flying round trip, you still need the L visa unless you only visit Hainan, which has its own 30-day visa-free policy. This guide walks through each step, the fees, and the mistakes that lead to refusals.

Do you need an L visa at all in 2026?
Before starting an application, check whether you can enter without a visa. Two policies matter most. First, citizens of 50 countries, including the UK, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, Japan, South Korea, and most EU states, can visit for up to 30 days without a visa for tourism, business, or family visits; for most of them the policy runs through December 31, 2026. The United States is not on that list. Second, 240-hour (10-day) visa-free transit, extended from 144 hours in December 2024, now covers 57 countries, including the United States, the UK, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, Japan, Singapore, Indonesia, most of Europe, and Brazil, Argentina, and Chile.
You qualify for 240-hour transit if:
You hold an ordinary passport from one of the 57 eligible countries, valid for at least 3 months

You have a confirmed onward ticket to a third country or region (not your origin), leaving within 240 hours

You enter through one of 65 approved ports and stay within the permitted areas of 24 provinces, which include Beijing, Shanghai, Guangzhou, Chengdu, Xi'an, and Hangzhou

Your total stay is 240 hours or less, counted from midnight after the day you arrive

You need the L visa if any of the following apply:
Your trip is longer than the visa-free stay you qualify for (30 days or 240 hours)

You are flying round trip and your passport isn't on the 30-day visa-free list

You plan to go beyond the transit areas: Tibet and Xinjiang, for example, are outside the 24 provinces

You are American and want to come and go repeatedly without meeting the transit conditions each time

Your passport isn't covered by any visa-free policy

For an American on a two-week vacation across Beijing, Xi'an, and Chengdu returning to the US, transit rules do not work (round-trip) and you need an L visa. A British or Canadian traveler can make the same trip visa-free, since it's under 30 days. For a 7-day Shanghai stopover on your way Singapore-to-Paris, 240-hour transit is perfect.
Step 1: COVA online pre-application
COVA (China Online Visa Application) is the online form you fill in before handing in your passport. In the US, applications go through the new system at consular.mfa.gov.cn/VISA , which the embassy launched on September 30, 2025: you sign up with an email address, fill in the form, upload your documents, and wait for an online pre-review. Elsewhere, start from your embassy's or visa center's website.
Before you start COVA
Gather these items so you do not have to pause mid-form and lose data:
Passport with more than 6 months of validity and at least 2 blank visa pages

Digital passport-style photo meeting China specs (more below)

Exact arrival and departure dates

Flight and hotel bookings, if your checklist asks for them (US applicants don't need them since January 1, 2024)

Employer details: company name, address, phone, your job title

Details of any previous Chinese visa (number, type, validity)

Parent and spouse information (name, date of birth, nationality)

For US applicants: proof of residence, such as a driver's license, utility bill, or bank bill

Filling the form
You can save your progress and come back to it; in the US system, drafts are under Personal Center > My draft. Fill in the form in English or Chinese, section by section.
Sections to complete:
Personal information. Exactly match your passport. The system reads your passport page and checks your photo as you upload it.

Type of visa. Choose the visa type, standard or expedited processing, validity, and length of stay. The consular officer decides what you actually get.

Work information. Your work history for the past 5 years, with full company names and no abbreviations.

Education. Your highest level of education.

Family information. Parents, spouse, and children. If you choose "Not Applicable", you must add an explanation.

Information on your travel. Arrival and departure dates and cities. The US system only accepts trips starting within the next 90 days.

Information on previous travel. Earlier trips to China, previous Chinese visas, and the countries you visited in the past 12 months.

Other information. Criminal record, previous refusals, overstays, military service. Answer truthfully.

Declaration. Say who filled in the form and agree to the statement.

After you submit, staff review the application online. When the status shows "Passport to be submitted", print the application page with the barcode and take it with your passport and original documents to the visa office (in the US) or to your visa center appointment (elsewhere).
Step 2: Photo specifications
The photo standard comes from China's Ministry of Foreign Affairs, and the online form checks your upload against it:
Size: 33mm wide by 48mm tall (not the 35x45 EU standard); digital files 354 x 472 to 420 x 560 pixels

Background: White or close to white, no border, no shadows

Head size: 15-22mm wide, 28-33mm from chin to crown

Expression: Neutral, eyes open, mouth closed, both ears visible

Glasses: Allowed only with untinted lenses, no glare, and frames that don't cover the eyes

Clothing: Not the same color as the background

Paper: Color print with no damage or marks

Digital copy: JPEG, 40-120 KB, uploaded to COVA

If your upload keeps failing the automatic check, you can still finish the form; bring a printed photo that meets the specs when you hand in your passport. Drugstore passport photo services often get the size wrong.
Step 3: Hand in your passport
Where this happens depends on the country. In the US there is no visa center: once your pre-review is approved, you or an agent take your passport to the Chinese Embassy or the consulate that covers your state, and no appointment is needed. In many other countries, including the UK, Canada, and Australia, a Chinese Visa Application Service Center (CVASC) takes in your documents; you book a slot at visaforchina.cn .
Handing in your application
Bring your passport, the printed application page with the barcode, and any original documents your checklist asks for, such as an old passport with a Chinese visa that is still valid.

Staff check your documents and may ask a few questions. A consular officer can also ask you to come in for an interview.

In the US, you don't have to go in person to give fingerprints. At visa centers such as those in the UK, you give your fingerprints when you submit.

You get a pickup form showing the earliest pickup date. Standard processing is 4 business days in the US.

Pickup
In the US, wait until the pickup date has passed and your online status shows "Passport to be collected", then go back (or send your agent) with the pink pickup form. You pay at pickup by Mastercard, Visa, money order, or cashier's check. Check the visa before you leave the counter. Visa centers elsewhere may take payment when you apply instead; the London center charges when you lodge the application.
Can you apply by mail?
Not in the US: the embassy doesn't accept applications by mail, so if you can't go yourself, use an agent. Some visa centers abroad will mail your passport back for a fee (Toronto lists a return-mail service, for example); check your center's website.
2026 L visa fees
Fees depend on your nationality and where you apply. For US citizens the fee follows the principle of reciprocity: it rose to $185 in May 2023, then was cut to $140 from December 11, 2023, and the cut now runs through December 31, 2026.

Visa centers abroad add their own service fees, so check the fee page where you apply.
Express service
Faster processing costs extra:
In the US: express service takes 3 business days and costs $25 more per visa. It's for urgent travel, and you show supporting documents.

Elsewhere: visa centers list their own express levels and fees; Toronto, for example, lists two.

Document checklist
Bring every item on your checklist. The US list is below; other countries' lists differ.
Passport with more than 6 months of validity and at least 2 blank visa pages

Old passport if it holds a Chinese visa or residence permit that is still valid

Printed application page with the barcode, showing "Passport to be submitted"

Photo uploaded with the form (bring a printed one if the upload failed the check)

Proof of residence in your consular district: driver's license, utility bill, or bank bill

Proof of legal status in the US if you're not a US citizen: green card, visa, I-20, or I-94

Chinese passport and naturalization certificate if you used to be a Chinese citizen and this is your first Chinese visa since naturalizing

Name-change document if this is your first application since changing your name

Anything else the officer asks for, such as flight and hotel bookings or an invitation letter; these are no longer required up front for US tourist visas

The UK checklist, by contrast, still asks for a round-trip flight booking and hotel reservations, or an invitation letter.
Common rejection reasons
No official refusal rate is published. These are the problems to avoid; for a fuller list with fixes, see why China visa applications get rejected .
Mismatch between COVA and supporting documents
If you submit bookings, they must match the form: a COVA form listing a Hilton in Beijing from March 5-10 next to a booking for a different hotel invites questions. The form's declaration warns that false, misleading, or incomplete statements may lead to a refusal. Match exactly.
Itinerary extends to restricted regions without special permits
Foreigners heading to Tibet (Xizang) face extra requirements on top of a visa: China's foreign ministry says it applies "regulatory and protection measures" to foreigners entering the region, so check the current requirements with a travel agency before you book. Don't leave planned destinations off the form to make it look simpler: the declaration makes you responsible for accurate answers, and a false statement is itself a ground for refusal.
Previous visa overstays
The form asks whether you have ever overstayed in China, so any overstay, even of a day, comes up again. Explain the circumstances in writing. Under China's Exit and Entry Administration Law, an overstay can bring a warning or, in serious cases, a fine of 500 yuan a day, up to 10,000 yuan, or detention, and people who were repatriated face a one- to five-year entry ban.
Insufficient proof of ties to your home country
The law lets officers refuse applicants who can't cover their expenses in China, and officers can ask for more documents. If your situation raises questions, such as no job or a one-way ticket, supply extra evidence: a rental lease, school enrollment, or an upcoming commitment back home.
Truthfulness errors in travel history
The form asks which countries you visited in the past 12 months, and your passport, stamps and all, goes to the consular officer with your application. Leaving Iran, Syria, or Yemen off the list because you think they will hurt your chances is worse than including them: fraud in an application is a ground for refusal under the Exit and Entry Administration Law. Honesty + explanation beats omission.
After approval
Your passport returns with a glossy L visa sticker showing:
Visa type (L)

Number of entries (1, 2, M for multiple)

Validity period (the "Enter Before" date)

Duration of each stay (the maximum days per entry, counted from the day you enter)

Issue date

The validity period is not the same as duration of stay . For example, a 10-year multiple-entry visa with a 60-day duration lets you enter any time before the "Enter Before" date and stay up to 60 days each time. If you enter shortly before that date, you can stay past it, as long as that stay is within 60 days.
FAQ
How long does a China tourist visa take in 2026?
In the US, regular processing takes 4 business days from the day you hand in your passport, and express takes 3 business days for $25 more, for urgent travel. Some applications take longer. Visa centers elsewhere set their own timelines; the Edinburgh consulate, for example, lists collection on the fourth working day, or the third with express service.
Can I get a China visa on arrival?
Not as a routine option for tourists. China issues port visas on arrival only for urgent cases, such as urgent business or visiting a critically ill relative, and tour groups need a Chinese travel agency to apply in advance. For a regular trip, you either qualify for visa-free entry (the 30-day list, 240-hour transit, or Hainan's 30-day policy) or apply before you travel.
Do I need an invitation letter for a tourist visa?
Not if you apply in the US: since January 1, 2024, tourist-visa applicants there don't have to submit an invitation letter. Some other countries' checklists accept an invitation letter instead of flight and hotel bookings. Business meetings need an M visa, with documents from your Chinese business partner.
Can I apply at a Chinese embassy in a country that is not my citizenship country?
Usually only if you live or are legally staying there. Chinese missions in the US ask non-US citizens for proof of legal residence (green card, visa, I-20, or I-94) and ask everyone to apply in the consular district where they live, and the UK checklist asks non-British applicants for proof of legal stay. Applying in your home country before you travel is simpler.
What happens if my hotel booking is cancelable and I cancel after getting the visa?
US applicants don't face this, since hotel bookings haven't been required for tourist visas there since January 1, 2024. Where a checklist does ask for them, as the UK list does, the bookings document the plan in your application. Nothing in the published rules requires you to keep them after the visa is issued, but everything you submit has to be true when you apply.
