How to Withdraw Cash in China with a Foreign Card 2026

by Robin

August 7, 2026

China runs on mobile payments. WeChat Pay and Alipay are how most things get paid for. But you still need cash. Street food stalls, wet markets, small taxis, temples, some tourist sites, and rural areas either want cash or only take it. If your phone dies or your payment app glitches, cash is what gets you out of trouble. Getting RMB from an ATM with a foreign card is not hard. But if you just walk up to the first machine you see, there is a decent chance your card gets declined or you pay more in fees than you need to. Here is how to do it right.

Which Banks and ATMs Take Foreign Cards in China
Not every ATM in China reads foreign cards. You need to go to the right banks.
ATMs that reliably work with Visa and Mastercard:

Bank of China (中国银行) — The most foreigner friendly. English menu is clear, the machines are everywhere, and foreign card acceptance is the most consistent of all Chinese banks.

ICBC (Industrial and Commercial Bank of China, 工商银行) — The biggest bank in China. Most ICBC ATMs take foreign cards. Look for the Visa or Mastercard logo on the machine.

China Construction Bank (建设银行) — Reliable for foreign card withdrawals. Good coverage in cities.

Agricultural Bank of China (农业银行) — Works with foreign cards at most ATMs. Per transaction limit of 5,000 RMB which is higher than some others.
ATMs that sometimes work:

HSBC — If you bank with HSBC at home, you might skip the local ATM fee entirely at HSBC China machines.

Citibank and Scotiabank — Part of global ATM alliances. Check if your home bank waives fees at these.
What to look for: Before you put your card in, check for the Visa, Mastercard, Plus, or Cirrus logo on the machine. No logo means it probably only accepts UnionPay, China's domestic network. It will decline your foreign card.
Where to find these ATMs: Airports, train stations, bank branches on main streets, hotel lobbies, and shopping malls. ATMs inside actual bank branches are the safest bet because if something goes wrong, you can walk inside and talk to someone.
ATMs to avoid: Small standalone machines on street corners. Machines from local city banks or rural credit unions. Machines without a Visa or Mastercard logo. Any machine that looks old or is not attached to a bank branch. These either reject foreign cards or charge higher fees.

How Much Can You Withdraw
How much you can pull depends on two limits: the one set by the Chinese ATM and the one set by your home bank. You hit whichever is lower first.
Per transaction limit: 2,500 to 5,000 RMB, depending on the bank. Bank of China caps it at 3,000 RMB per pull. Agricultural Bank of China goes up to 5,000 RMB. ICBC and China Construction Bank are usually around 3,000 RMB.
Daily limit at Chinese ATMs: Up to 20,000 RMB across multiple transactions. You can do several withdrawals back to back from the same machine.
Your home bank limit: Most foreign banks cap international ATM withdrawals at $500 to $1,000 USD worth of RMB per day. Check your banking app or call them before you travel.
How to get more: Do multiple withdrawals in a row. Each one counts as a separate transaction with its own fee. If you need 10,000 RMB, you will likely need three or four separate pulls.
How Much It Costs to Withdraw Cash

Every foreign card withdrawal in China costs you money from at least two directions.
The Chinese bank fee: 20 to 30 RMB per transaction. This is what the ATM owner charges. It is the same at Bank of China, ICBC, China Construction Bank, and Agricultural Bank of China. Smaller banks sometimes charge more.
Your home bank fee: On top of that, your own bank charges you. This could be a flat fee (usually $2 to $5 USD per withdrawal) or a percentage (1% to 3% of the amount). Credit card cash advances are even worse. You pay a cash advance fee, often 3% to 5%, plus interest that starts immediately. Do not use a credit card at an ATM unless it is an emergency.
Dynamic Currency Conversion: Some ATMs will ask if you want to be charged in your home currency instead of RMB. Say no. Always choose RMB. Let your own bank do the currency conversion. The ATM's conversion rate is always worse and comes with hidden fees.
How to pay less:
Withdraw larger amounts less often. Three pulls of 2,000 RMB means three Chinese bank fees and three home bank fees. One pull of 5,000 RMB means one of each.
Use a card that waives foreign ATM fees. Wise, Revolut, and some premium bank accounts give you a certain number of free international withdrawals per month.
If your home bank is HSBC, Citibank, or Scotiabank, check if they have an ATM network in China that waives fees.

What to Do Before You Travel
Tell your bank. If your bank does not know you are in China, they might freeze your card the first time you try to withdraw. Call them or do it in the app before you leave.
Check your daily withdrawal limit. Your home bank probably has a daily cap that is lower than the 20,000 RMB Chinese ATM limit. Raise it if you think you will need more cash in one day.
Enable international transactions on your card. Some banks have this turned off by default. Check in your banking app or call them.
Bring more than one card. If your primary card gets declined or eaten by a machine, having a second card from a different bank in your bag saves the day. Do not keep both cards in the same wallet.
How to Actually Use the ATM

The process is straightforward but there are a few things to know.
Step 1: Insert your card. The ATM should switch to English automatically or offer a language button on the screen. If not, look for 英文 (English) or a flag icon.
Step 2: Enter your PIN. Standard six digit PINs work on most machines. If you have a longer PIN, some older machines might not accept it.
Step 3: Choose withdrawal, then select "Checking" or "Savings." If one does not work, try the other. Some foreign cards only register on one option.
Step 4: Enter the amount in RMB. Round numbers work best. 500, 1,000, 2,000, or 3,000 are common denominations.
Step 5: When the ATM asks if you want to be charged in your home currency or RMB, pick RMB. Always.
Step 6: Take your cash. The machine gives you 100 RMB bills in most cases. Some machines also dispense smaller notes but do not count on it.
Step 7: Take your card and receipt. Most Chinese ATMs beep at you until you remove your card. Do not walk away while it is still beeping.

Common Problems and What to Do
Card declined but you know it should work. Try another ATM from a different bank. Some machines just do not like certain foreign cards. Bank of China ATMs are the most reliable. If you are at a small bank, walk to the nearest Bank of China or ICBC and try there.
ATM asks for a six digit PIN but yours is four digits. Try adding two zeros before your PIN. If that does not work, find a different ATM. Most Bank of China machines handle four digit PINs fine.
You picked your home currency by mistake. You just paid about 3% to 5% more than you needed to. For next time, remember to choose RMB. For now, the money is already out and there is nothing you can do about it.
The ATM ate your card. Go into the bank branch immediately and show your passport. Tell them which machine took it. They can usually retrieve it on the spot during business hours. If the bank is closed, call the number on the ATM and report it. Then freeze your card through your banking app.
You got charged in your home currency without being asked. Some ATMs skip the DCC prompt and just charge you in your home currency by default. This is rare at major bank ATMs but more common at independent machines. Another reason to use only ATMs attached to bank branches.
The machine has no English option. Look for these buttons: 英文 (English) or 语言 (Language). If you cannot find either, take a photo with Google Translate and work through it. Or find a different ATM. Most ATMs in cities have English.
You need more cash than the limit allows. Do multiple withdrawals back to back from the same machine. Each one counts as a separate transaction and a separate fee. If you need a lot of cash at once, go into a bank branch and ask about a counter withdrawal with your passport.
Can You Withdraw Cash Over the Counter at a Bank Branch
Yes, but only at major banks and only during business hours. It is slower than an ATM but lets you pull larger amounts in one transaction.
Where to go: Bank of China, ICBC, China Construction Bank, or Agricultural Bank of China branches. Tourist area branches and those near international business districts are more likely to have English speaking tellers.
When to go: Monday to Friday, 9 AM to 5 PM. Some branches open Saturday morning. Closed on Sundays and public holidays.
What to bring: Your passport (original, not a copy) and your card.
What happens: The teller runs your card, checks your passport, asks you to sign a slip, and hands over the cash. Expect a queue and some waiting.
Fees: Debit card withdrawals cost the same as an ATM, about 20 to 30 RMB to the Chinese bank plus your home bank's fee. Credit card withdrawals count as a cash advance and cost more, usually 3% to 5% plus immediate interest.
Why do it: Counter withdrawals let you pull more than the 2,500 to 5,000 RMB per transaction ATM limit, which saves you on repeated fees if you need a lot of cash at once.
Language: Most tellers do not speak English. Bring a translation app or have someone write down that you want to withdraw cash.

When You Actually Need Cash in China
You can pay for most things in cities with WeChat Pay or Alipay. Hotels, restaurants, shopping malls, convenience stores, supermarkets, metro tickets, and ride hailing apps all take mobile payment. But cash is still necessary for:
Street food stalls and night markets
Wet markets and small fruit vendors
Small taxis that do not use a meter app
Temples and some smaller tourist sites
Rural areas and small towns
Buying water or snacks from tiny roadside stands
Tipping (rare in China but some tour guides accept it)
When your phone dies or your payment app stops working
Keep 200 to 500 RMB in cash on you at all times. It does not take up much space and it solves a lot of problems that mobile payment cannot.

How to Withdraw Cash in China with a Foreign Card FAQs

Which Chinese bank ATM is best for foreign cards?

Bank of China. It has the most consistent foreign card acceptance, clear English menus, and ATMs in every major city and airport. ICBC and China Construction Bank are good alternatives.

How much does it cost to withdraw cash in China with a foreign card?

The Chinese bank charges 20 to 30 RMB per transaction. Your home bank charges its own fee, usually $2 to $5 USD or 1% to 3% of the amount. So a 3,000 RMB withdrawal costs you about 50 RMB in total fees between both sides.

How much cash can I withdraw per day?

Chinese ATMs allow up to 20,000 RMB per day across multiple transactions. Individual transactions are capped at 2,500 to 5,000 RMB depending on the bank. Your home bank has its own daily limit which may be lower.

Do I need my passport to withdraw cash from an ATM?

No. You just need your card and PIN. ATMs do not ask for ID. Counter withdrawals inside a bank branch require your passport.

Can I use my credit card to withdraw cash?

You can, but it is expensive. Credit card cash advances charge 3% to 5% upfront plus interest that starts the same day. Only use a credit card at an ATM in an emergency.
