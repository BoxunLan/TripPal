At Beijing Daxing (PKX) arrivals in 2026, the cheapest foreign-card CNY withdrawal is from a Bank of China or ICBC ATM with a card on the Global ATM Alliance (HSBC, Deutsche Bank, Barclays, Westpac, Scotiabank, BNP Paribas). Bank of China says the fees for a foreign-card withdrawal are set by your card's issuing bank, and most partners waive the international fee. Bank of China ATMs pay out up to ¥3,000 per withdrawal.

PKX arrivals ATM layout in 2026
Beijing Daxing International Airport opened in 2019 and has a single consolidated terminal with five check-in "fingers." Arrivals are on level L1 underground, split by international (I) and domestic (D) flows.
International arrivals (L1, level B1) : After immigration and baggage, you exit into the Meet & Greet hall. ATMs are in a row by the east wall of the hall, near the car rental desks. Expect 4-6 machines: Bank of China (BOC), ICBC, China Construction Bank (CCB), Agricultural Bank of China (ABC), and sometimes China Merchants Bank.

Domestic arrivals (L1) : ATMs by the north exit corridor toward the airport express and subway. Usually BOC and ICBC plus a CCB.

Airside (before immigration) : One ATM near each of gates D201, C105, and E108 but these are harder to find and not always restocked.

Signs use both Chinese and English; the universal blue "ATM" icon is posted from the baggage hall.
Typical fees and what to expect
What you pay for a withdrawal depends mainly on your own bank. In 2026:
ATM fees: set by your card's issuing bank, Bank of China says . Check your bank's fee for foreign ATM withdrawals before you fly.

Home bank fees: typically a flat fee of a couple of dollars plus a percentage of the withdrawal, and a 1-3% foreign transaction fee unless your card waives it. On a credit card, the withdrawal counts as a cash advance.

Dynamic Currency Conversion (DCC) prompt: "Withdraw in USD / EUR?" — always pick CNY . DCC adds 3-7% hidden markup.

Per-withdrawal limit: ¥3,000 with a foreign card at Bank of China ATMs.

Daily and annual limits: set by your card's issuing bank.

Fee-free or reduced-fee options
Global ATM Alliance partners
If your home bank is in the Global ATM Alliance, its own fee terms decide what a withdrawal at a partner ATM in China costs.
Bank of China (BOC) in mainland China has a reciprocal partnership with Bank of America, Barclays (UK), Scotiabank (Canada), Westpac (Australia), Deutsche Bank (Germany), BNP Paribas (France), and a few others. Use BOC machines at PKX if your card is with any of these.

HSBC foreign cardholders pay no Chinese surcharge at HSBC China ATMs — but HSBC has limited ATM presence at PKX. There is one HSBC ATM reported near the international arrivals food court corridor; check signage at arrival.

Citibank foreign cardholders can use Citi China ATMs fee-free, but PKX has no Citi ATM as of early 2026 — nearest is in central Beijing.

Charles Schwab and Wise: universal refund / multi-currency
Charles Schwab High-Yield Investor Checking (US residents) refunds all foreign ATM fees worldwide. Use any PKX ATM, and Schwab rebates the ATM fees at month-end.

Wise debit card (formerly TransferWise) converts your home currency to CNY at real mid-market rate and allows 2 free withdrawals up to ~£200/month combined, then charges a small fee. PKX BOC ATMs work fine with Wise.

Revolut debit cards (UK/EU/US/AU) get free withdrawals up to the plan's monthly cap, then 2% + flat fee. Works at BOC.

Paying with Alipay and WeChat Pay: the ATM alternative
Since 2023 both Alipay (支付宝) and WeChat Pay (微信支付) let you link Visa, Mastercard, JCB, Discover, and some Amex cards and pay merchants directly, with no top-up needed. Each app sets the limits for your account and shows them in the app. China's central bank caps overseas visitors' mobile payments at US$5,000 per transaction and US$50,000 per year.
Transaction fees on the foreign-card path:
Under ¥200 per transaction: zero fee.

Over ¥200: roughly 3% charged by Alipay/WeChat.

Your card issuer may still add foreign transaction fees on top.

Why this matters at PKX
For most tourist spending (subway, taxi, food, museum tickets, water-town admissions), QR pay works everywhere and you never touch an ATM. You still want a small CNY cash reserve for:
Tiny street vendors and temple donation boxes.

Rural areas with flaky cell signal.

The "stingy change" problem at some small bus stations and local markets.

Backup when QR payment fails due to network or app glitch.

A reasonable balance in 2026: withdraw ¥500-1000 cash on arrival at PKX and rely on Alipay or WeChat Pay for the rest of the trip.
How to set up Alipay before landing
Link your card to Alipay directly; that is now the main way visitors pay. Tour Pass, the Alipay prepaid card that older guides describe, was withdrawn in 2023. Bank of Shanghai, which issued it, now offers a separate prepaid card called TourCard with its own top-up fee; our Alipay guide explains when it is worth using. Set Alipay up before you fly: Alipay says the identity review can take up to 24 hours.
Download Alipay from the App Store or Google Play before you travel.

Sign up with your phone number; a foreign number works.

Verify your identity with your passport when the app asks.

Add your Visa, Mastercard or other card issued abroad in the app's bank-card section. Your bank may ask you to approve it.

Pay by scanning the merchant's QR code, or show your payment code for the cashier to scan.

WeChat Pay offers a similar "International Card" flow — less polished UI, with its own limits shown in the app. Having both is worth it as a backup because vendor acceptance occasionally glitches.
When to still take out cash
With Alipay and WeChat Pay working for the big 2026 tourist economy, ATMs become a backup tool. Paper CNY still matters for:
Temple donation boxes — monastery stewards only accept cash.

Tipping luggage porters at small hotels (¥10-20).

Remote bus stations and ferry operators in rural Yunnan, Tibet, and Xinjiang.

Network outages — if your phone dies or the venue's POS is offline.

Elderly vendors at morning markets who don't run QR pay.

Paying for a shared taxi in small towns where the driver is old-school.

Allocate ¥500-1000 for the whole trip in ¥10, ¥20, ¥50, and ¥100 notes. Break large notes early — ¥100 can be hard to use at a ¥5 fruit stall.
Currency exchange counters at PKX
Bank of China and ICBC operate staffed exchange counters in the international arrivals hall. They take USD, EUR, GBP, JPY, KRW, HKD, CAD, AUD, SGD, and several other currencies. Pros: no ATM fees, good to change leftover notes at trip's end. Cons: 4-6% total spread versus the mid-market rate, and both counters close between 10 pm and 7 am.
Rates are clearly posted in Chinese and English. Compare with the ATM in the same hall before committing.
Step-by-step PKX arrival cash plan
Clear immigration and baggage. Walk to the Meet & Greet hall on L1.

Skip the money changer touts in the corridor (unlicensed, bad rate).

Find the BOC or ICBC ATM row by the east wall.

Insert card, switch to English, choose "withdraw cash."

Select ¥500, ¥1000, or ¥2000 — stay under ¥3000 cap per transaction.

Decline DCC; take CNY charge.

Take cash, card, and receipt.

If you haven't linked a card to Alipay yet, start now in the app; until the identity review clears, pay with cash.

Proceed to Daxing Airport Express or subway with ¥500-1000 cash in wallet and Alipay/WeChat Pay set up.

Getting from PKX to central Beijing
Once you have cash, the fastest options to central Beijing in 2026:
Daxing Airport Express (草桥站) : ¥25-35 depending on distance, 19-25 minutes to Caoqiao station on Metro Line 10. Transfers give access to the full Beijing subway network.

Beijing Subway Line 19 / New Line : Combined with regular metro fares of ¥3-9 end-to-end.

Licensed taxi : ¥200-260 to central Beijing, 50-80 minutes depending on traffic.

Didi Chuxing : ¥150-200 to central Beijing, similar time.

Intercity shuttle bus : ¥30-40 to multiple city stops including Beijing West Railway Station.

Alipay and WeChat Pay QR codes open subway gates and pay taxis — you do not need to use cash for any of these.
Security and anti-fraud tips at PKX ATMs
Chinese ATMs are generally safe, but apply the same precautions you would anywhere:
Inspect the card slot for skimmers before inserting. A wobbly or extra-thick bezel is a warning sign.

Cover the keypad when entering your PIN.

Decline DCC . When the machine asks if you want to be charged in USD / EUR, always say no. DCC adds 3-7% hidden markup.

Take the receipt — it lists your daily and monthly remaining limits for your Chinese card side.

Don't accept "help" from strangers in the ATM area. Licensed airport staff wear IDs and stand behind counters; any friendly stranger offering to help insert the card is a scam.

Keep your card in sight and do not walk away before retrieving it. The machine will auto-swallow a card left for more than 20 seconds.

Use ATMs inside the bank-branded kiosks rather than free-standing ones in back corridors.

If your card is swallowed, call the bank whose logo is on the machine. BOC English hotline is 95566 from a Chinese number, or +86-10-66085566 from abroad.
Frequently Asked Questions
Are there fee-free ATMs at Beijing Daxing Airport for foreign cards?
Not outright fee-free for most cards, but several options come close. Bank of China says the fees for a foreign-card withdrawal are set by your card's issuing bank, so the cards that come closest are ones whose bank waives or refunds its own foreign ATM fees, such as HSBC Premier, Charles Schwab debit (US), Wise debit (UK/EU), and Revolut debit within their free allowances.
How much cash should I withdraw on arrival at PKX in 2026?
Withdraw ¥500-1000 CNY on arrival. This covers emergency taxi fares, temple donations, street food backup, and small shops in rural areas. Most tourist spending (subway, taxis, restaurants, museum tickets) runs on Alipay or WeChat Pay via your foreign card. Avoid withdrawing large amounts — your bank's daily limit and hotel safe concerns make smaller, more frequent withdrawals safer.
Can I top up Alipay or WeChat Pay with a foreign credit card instead of using an ATM?
You don't need to top up: since 2023 both Alipay and WeChat Pay let you link Visa, Mastercard, JCB, and some Amex and Discover cards and pay merchants directly. Each app sets its own limits and shows them in the app, within China's central-bank cap of US$5,000 per transaction and US$50,000 per year for overseas visitors. Transactions under ¥200 have no fee; over ¥200 are charged roughly 3%. It covers most tourist spending in 2026.
Do currency exchange counters at PKX give a better rate than ATMs?
Usually no. The BOC and ICBC counters in PKX international arrivals post a 4-6% total spread versus the mid-market rate. Most foreign cards at a BOC ATM beat that, unless your own bank's ATM fees are high. Counters are useful only for changing leftover CNY at the end of your trip or for currencies that foreign ATMs struggle with.
Is the Beijing Daxing ATM daily limit ¥3000 or ¥10000?
Neither is a daily limit. ¥3,000 is the most you can take out in one withdrawal with a foreign card at a Bank of China ATM. Daily and annual limits are set by your card's issuing bank, so check yours before you travel; if you need more cash, make several withdrawals within that limit.
Worth asking how much cash you actually need before you queue. For most of a trip the answer is ¥500–1,000 in small notes for rural taxis and temple donation boxes, with everything else on Alipay or WeChat Pay . Where cash still matters is the places mobile payment fails — checkpoints and rural rest stops in Xinjiang , and the first taxi at a small-town station.
