Under ¥200 a night, during shoulder season, in a real Naxi courtyard inn, inside Lijiang Old Town. All four of those things at the same time. It sounds impossible if you have only searched on Booking.com, where most of the listings come back ¥350 and up. But Trip.com (which is Ctrip's international-facing site) and the local WeChat booking ecosystem still surface hundreds of courtyard guesthouses at ¥120 to ¥180 per night in April, May, September, and early October, and a smaller but real number at those prices even during peak weeks if you know how to book.
This is not a list of specific hotel names, because the small courtyard inns in Lijiang turn over ownership and rebrand constantly. A place that was beloved in 2024 might be renovated and renamed in 2026, and any list of "best courtyard stays" more than a year old is already half wrong. What works instead is understanding how the market is structured, where to look, and what to filter for.

Why ¥200 courtyard inns still exist in Lijiang Old Town
Lijiang has thousands of guesthouses inside the Old Town walls. The supply is enormous, and competition has been brutal since the 2020 to 2023 tourism downturn. A lot of older Naxi courtyards, family-run places with five to eight rooms around a central flagstone courtyard, survived by offering rooms below the chain hotel floor price. In 2026, this segment is still alive for a few structural reasons:
Lijiang tourism is recovering but not saturated. Shoulder seasons have spare inventory, and owners would rather fill a room at ¥150 than leave it empty.

The Naxi courtyard inns are run by families, not chains. Many have paid off their property decades ago, so their cost structure is low. ¥180 a night with breakfast still covers their daily operating cost.

The platforms compete. Trip.com, Meituan, and Fliggy all offer "first booking" discounts and coupons that regularly cut the base price by ¥30 to ¥60.

Tourists have been trained to look for 3-star and above. This creates a pricing gap in the under-¥200 segment that independent travelers willing to stay in smaller places can exploit.

The catch: the rooms are simple. You get a wooden bed, a clean duvet, a small private bathroom (usually), a kettle, and wifi that might work well or might not. You do not get a lobby with staff in uniforms. You do not get a buffet breakfast with five kinds of juice. What you get in exchange is a real Naxi wooden courtyard, a host who might be cooking dinner for her family when you arrive, and a quiet lane where the rooftop view is tiled black slate instead of concrete.
Dayan versus Shuhe versus Baisha
Lijiang "Old Town" is technically three connected ancient settlements, not one. Knowing which one you are booking in matters a lot.
Dayan Ancient Town (大研古城) is the main one, the UNESCO site most tourists mean when they say "Lijiang Old Town." It is dense, atmospheric, crowded, and the most photographed. The central square (四方街 Sifang Jie) is the old Naxi market heart of the town. Dayan is where the majority of courtyard inns cluster. This is also where night crowds peak and loud bars along Xinyi Street make light sleepers unhappy. Courtyard inns at ¥120 to ¥180 exist here, but you want to stay in the northern or eastern lanes away from the bar streets.
Shuhe Ancient Town (束河古镇) is 4 kilometers north of Dayan, quieter, with a slower pace and more of a village feel. It is also UNESCO-protected. Courtyard inns in Shuhe tend to be larger, better maintained, and slightly more expensive on average because the Shuhe crowd skews toward travelers who want peace. You can still find ¥150 to ¥200 options, especially in the eastern and southern lanes away from the main square.
Baisha Ancient Town (白沙古镇) is 10 kilometers north of Dayan, a much smaller Naxi village at the base of Jade Dragon Snow Mountain. It has almost no nightlife, very few tourists, and a handful of small family inns. This is where you go if you want absolute quiet and are willing to take a taxi or shuttle bus into Dayan for dinner and sightseeing. Courtyard stays in Baisha run ¥100 to ¥160, the cheapest of the three, but the tradeoff is distance from the main attractions.

How to search Trip.com for real courtyard inns
Open Trip.com (not Booking.com, which has a thinner inventory in Lijiang). Search "Lijiang Old Town" and set the dates. Here are the filters that actually surface the under-¥200 courtyard segment:
Price slider: max ¥200 per night.

Property type: check "Guesthouse" and "Inn," uncheck "Hotel" and "Resort." This is the single most important filter. "Hotel" includes chain properties that have nothing to do with traditional courtyards.

Amenities: check "Free Wi-Fi," "Hot water," and "Parking" if you have a car. Parking is relevant because most Dayan inns cannot offer it, and filtering it out narrows to larger properties that are less authentic. If you are arriving by taxi, uncheck parking.

Review score: 4.3+ minimum. Below this in Lijiang means something is wrong, usually cleanliness or noise.

Location: zoom the map into Dayan or Shuhe. Look for the cluster of pins in the northern Dayan lanes (Wuyi Street, Qiyi Street area) or eastern Shuhe. Avoid pins directly on the main Sifang Jie square, which are the noisiest.

Sort by: "Price from low."

Scroll through the first 30 results. Open each listing and look at the photos for these signs of an authentic Naxi courtyard:
Two or three stories of wooden construction around a central open courtyard

Stone-paved courtyard floor with maybe a plant or a small pond

Carved wooden doors with red or dark brown paint

Black tiled sloped roofs visible from the second floor

Rooms with wood beams exposed in the ceiling

Signs that it is a modern hotel pretending to be a courtyard inn:
Elevator mentioned in amenities

More than 25 rooms

Breakfast served in a lobby restaurant rather than the courtyard

Photos of a "pool" (real Naxi courtyards almost never have pools)

Branded signage out front in English and Chinese

April is usually the sweet spot for budget courtyard stays. The weather is good (12°C to 22°C daytime, crisp evenings), the rain hasn't fully started, and the Golden Week crowds are months away. September has similar weather but more rain risk.
The weeks to avoid completely for a ¥200 budget: Chinese New Year, May 1 to 5 (Labor Day holiday), and October 1 to 7 (Golden Week). During these seven-day stretches, every property in town doubles or triples prices and even the ¥100 hostel beds fill up three weeks in advance.
What ¥180 actually buys you in 2026
Let's set expectations correctly. A ¥180 per night courtyard inn during shoulder season will give you:
Almost always:
Private room with a queen or double bed

Private bathroom with shower (occasionally shared, confirm before booking)

Hot water, usually on a boiler system that is reliable in morning and evening

Free wifi (speed varies from mediocre to fine; expect 5 to 15 Mbps)

A small window or balcony opening onto the courtyard or a side lane

An electric kettle and bottled water or tap water for boiling

Bedding that is clean but not hotel-standard thick

Often included:
Simple breakfast (Naxi flatbread or noodle soup, fruit, tea)

Slippers

A small desk or stool

Heating in winter months (electric blanket or small space heater; central heating is rare)

Rarely included at this price point:
Air conditioning that is modern and quiet (older split units common)

Elevator (almost none; expect stairs)

24-hour front desk (most shut down between 22:00 and 07:00)

English-speaking staff (host may have basic phrases, translation apps are your friend)

Luggage storage before check-in or after checkout (many offer this informally but not guaranteed)

Do not expect five-star cleanliness, but do expect basic cleanliness. If reviews mention bedbugs, mold, or sewage smells, skip. The good ones in this price range have reviews praising the host's friendliness and the atmosphere.
The quiet lanes most tourists miss
Dayan's main streets — Qiyi Street, Xinhua Street, Xinyi Street — are the bar-and-shop areas. Noise carries, especially at night when music and drunk crowds drift across the canals. Stay one or two lanes in from the main thoroughfares and you get the old Lijiang experience without the soundtrack.
Specific areas that tend to stay quiet:
Northern Dayan (north of the Mu Mansion) — fewer bars, more residential, narrow lanes with moss-covered stone walls. Courtyard inns here skew toward local families and quiet travelers.

Eastern Dayan, near the Black Dragon Pool exit — closer to the canals, easier escape routes when you want to walk into Heilongtan Park in the morning.

Southern Dayan, near Wenchang Palace (but not directly on the bar street) — good balance, walkable to dining but one lane removed from the loud areas.

Shuhe's eastern and southern lanes — the western edge of Shuhe is near the main entrance and bus parking, so always walk away from the main square for quieter rooms.

On Trip.com, zoom the map in to 100-meter level and look at the pin clusters. The ones in lanes away from the red-highlighted "main streets" are your targets.
Booking timeline
For shoulder-season dates (April, September, late October to November):
Start checking prices 3 to 4 weeks before your dates

Book when you see a property you like at ¥120 to ¥180 with 4.5+ reviews

Most Trip.com listings have free cancellation up to 48 hours before check-in, so you can book early and adjust

Start checking 5 to 6 weeks out

FAQ
Can I book these courtyard inns without a Chinese phone number?
Yes. Trip.com (the international site) accepts foreign phone numbers and foreign credit cards without any Chinese ID. Your passport is the only ID you need. Hosts will ask to see your passport at check-in and photograph the data page for police registration, which is standard and legal in Lijiang. Keep a digital copy of your passport on your phone as backup.
Is Lijiang Old Town safe to walk around at night?
Yes, genuinely. Lijiang Old Town is one of the safest tourist areas in China for late-night walks. Streetlights are on until at least 23:00 in the busy sections, police patrol the main lanes, and even the quieter northern lanes feel comfortable. The main risks are slipping on wet stone paving after rain and getting lost in the labyrinth of alleys, both of which are easily solved with decent shoes and a map app.
Will the ¥200 courtyard inn host speak English?
Usually not beyond a few phrases like "check-in," "passport," and "wifi password." Bring a translation app (Pleco for offline, or Google Translate with Chinese downloaded) and you will be fine. Hosts who run these inns are used to foreign guests and will gesture, draw maps, and call a friend to translate if needed. The language barrier almost never becomes a real problem; it is awkward for the first minute and then normal.
How do I get to my inn from the airport?
Lijiang Sanyi Airport is about 28 kilometers south of Dayan. Airport shuttle buses run to the Lijiang Bus Station for ¥25 per person and take about 45 minutes. From the bus station, a taxi to the Dayan entrance costs ¥15 to ¥25. Most courtyard inns are a short walk inside the Old Town walls, which do not allow cars. Your host will usually send a hand-drawn map via WeChat before arrival, or meet you at the nearest entrance gate (there are multiple: North Gate, Shuijin Gate, etc.) if you message ahead.
Is the Lijiang Ancient Town entrance fee still in effect in 2026?
The old ¥80 "preservation fee" was suspended during the pandemic period and has not been fully reinstated as a blanket charge in 2026. In practice, foreign tourists rarely get charged at the Old Town entrances these days, though checks are more frequent during peak weeks. If asked, you pay at one of the ticket booths at the main entrances. It is not a gated system like Jiuzhaigou; you can walk in and out freely. Budget ¥80 per person just in case, and check the latest status with your inn host before arrival.
