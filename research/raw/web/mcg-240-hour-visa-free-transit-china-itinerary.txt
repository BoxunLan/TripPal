China 240-Hour Visa-Free Transit Itinerary: Three 10-Day Routes

Sarah Liu · Cultural Guide, Food Explorer · Updated September 27, 2026

This page is about routes: three ways to fill China's 240-hour visa-free window, each with the train legs and a clean exit to a third country or region. Two rules shape every route. Your ten days start at 00:00 on the day after you land, so the arrival day is a bonus, and your onward ticket must go to a different country or region from the one you flew in from.
Every route here also assumes you qualify: a passport from one of the 57 eligible countries, arrival at one of the 65 approved ports, and a confirmed onward ticket. The requirements guide covers each condition, what to show at the counter and what gets people refused.
Hong Kong, Macau and Taiwan all count as third destinations. Mainland China → Hong Kong → home is fully within the rules, and it is the easiest exit to arrange ( the high-speed rail into Hong Kong ).
Where the ten days actually apply
The policy covers the permitted areas of 24 provincial-level regions, and you can move between them. Six of the 24 admit you to named cities only: Shanxi, Heilongjiang, Jiangxi, Guangxi, Sichuan and Yunnan. Check a destination in those provinces against the permitted-city rules before you build a route around it.
Tibet is excluded outright and still needs a Tibet Travel Permit regardless of visa status. Xinjiang, Qinghai, Gansu, Ningxia, Inner Mongolia and Jilin are outside the scheme too, so Silk Road routes to Dunhuang or Kashgar need a visa.

On arrival, and at each stop
Fill in the arrival card online before you fly (possible since 20 November 2025) and keep your onward ticket to hand at the counter. On a multi-stop route, registration comes up at every stop: hotels register you at check-in, but anywhere else you or your host must register within 24 hours of each move, online since 21 September 2026 or at the local police station.
Leave a buffer day
Plan the route so you leave at least a day before the window closes. If illness or a cancelled flight will hold you past it, apply to the local exit-entry office for a stay permit before the deadline; staying on without one is illegal stay, which can mean fines of up to ¥10,000 or detention.
FAQ
When does the 240-hour clock start?
At 00:00 on the day after you clear immigration. Land on Friday afternoon and the ten days run from midnight Friday into Saturday. The day you arrive does not count.
Can I fly home, visit China, and fly back home on this?
No. Your departure country and your onward destination have to be different countries or regions, so a round trip does not qualify.
Can I visit several provinces on one 240-hour entry?
Yes, as long as every place is inside the permitted areas of the 24 regions and you exit through an eligible port inside the window. Six of the 24 restrict you to named cities rather than the whole province, so check the specific destination.
Does Hong Kong count as leaving China?
Yes. Hong Kong, Macau and Taiwan all count as third destinations for this policy, which is why a mainland-then-Hong-Kong route works.
Two places worth knowing about. Harbin Taiping is Heilongjiang's only eligible port, and the permitted area is Harbin rather than the whole province, which is enough for the ice festival. And Tianjin is part of the same permitted area as Beijing, 30 minutes from Beijing South on the fastest trains, so it is an easy day trip inside your window.
