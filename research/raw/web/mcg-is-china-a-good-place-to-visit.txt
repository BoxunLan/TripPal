Yes, China is a good place to visit in 2026, but only if you download three apps before you leave, skip the cities that burned everyone out in 2015, and accept that this is not Japan. International arrivals hit 150 million+ in 2025 , up 17% year on year, and US tourist spending jumped 50% . The food is the best on Earth. The high-speed trains are better than yours. Right now, you might be the only Westerner in a city of eight million. That's the promo.
Here's the part nobody posts on Instagram: the visa is still a pain if you're American. Google Maps doesn't work. Small hotels still refuse foreign guests. If you want a perfectly frictionless trip, go to Tokyo. So is China a good place to visit right now? Yes. Just not for everyone. Let me be honest about who should book and who should stay home.

The Honest Verdict: Is China a Good Place to Visit in 2026?
Yes. With caveats. China is worth visiting in 2026 more than it's been in a decade, but only for travelers who do their homework.
Here's the quick math. In Q1-Q3 2025, almost 21 million foreigners entered China visa-free, up 50% from the year before, now over 70% of all foreign arrivals. The government extended visa-free through December 31, 2026 , added UK and Canada from February 17, 2026 , and brought the total to 50 countries with unilateral visa exemption.
The US dropped China's travel advisory from Level 3 to Level 2 , the same level as France or Germany. Payments with foreign Visa and Mastercards now work through Alipay and WeChat Pay, so you don't need a Chinese bank account anymore. The trains are faster than Europe's, cheaper than Japan's, and go places you can't pronounce yet.
So what's the catch? You still need to prep. China is easier than it was, not easy. And if you're used to Google Maps, Instagram, WhatsApp, and tap-to-pay everywhere, the first 48 hours will rattle you.

What's Genuinely Amazing About Visiting China
People ask whether China has any tourist attractions worth the hassle. It has more UNESCO World Heritage Sites than any country except Italy (57 at last count), a 21,000-km high-speed rail network, and food so regional you need a passport inside the country.
Let me tell you about the food first, because it's the answer to "is china a nice place to visit," and it's not close.
You have not eaten Chinese food until you've had real Sichuan hotpot in Chengdu on a Tuesday night. Numbing peppercorns that make your lips vibrate. 40 ingredients for ¥120 ($17) a person. Hand-pulled biang biang noodles in Xi'an for ¥18 ($2.50). Dim sum in Guangzhou where a cart comes by every 90 seconds and lunch for two costs ¥90 ($13). That's not a tourist price. That's the price.
The scale of the country is the second thing nobody warns you about. You can go from the cyberpunk skyline of Chongqing to the karst mountains of Yangshuo in four hours by train. There's a reason photographers obsess over Zhangjiajie, the sandstone pillars that inspired Avatar's floating mountains, and on a weekday in March you can walk an entire trail alone.
Here's the thing nobody tells you. Outside Beijing and Shanghai, you will often be the only Western tourist in the room . One traveler on Reddit put it perfectly: "At evenings it was just me as Westerner. Thousands of people, only me."
And China is genuinely safe for tourists. Violent crime against foreigners is extremely rare. The US State Department dropped the travel advisory to Level 2 on November 27, 2024 for a reason.
The prices once you're in country are the final punch. A business-class seat on the Beijing to Shanghai G-train is ¥1,748 ($243) for 1,318 km in 4 hours 18 minutes . A four-star hotel in Chengdu is ¥400-600 ($56-84) a night . A full day of pork belly, noodles, tea, and street dumplings will run you ¥150 ($21) if you order like a local.
What's Genuinely Hard (And How Bad It Actually Is)
Now the honest part. I'm not going to pretend China is easy, because it isn't.
The internet will break your brain for 48 hours. Google Maps, Gmail, WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook, YouTube, all blocked. You can fix this with a VPN bought before you arrive (NordVPN and ExpressVPN both still work as of April 2026), but you need to install it at home. Trying to download one inside China from an app store doesn't work. This is the single biggest reason travelers panic on day one.
The language barrier is real outside tier-1 cities. In Shanghai and Beijing, most subway signs, museums, and airport staff speak passable English. In Xi'an, Chengdu, or Chongqing, you'll need Pinyin plus translation. Google Translate is blocked. Use the Translator inside Alipay, or our app's offline translator (it handles voice, camera, and handwriting). One trick: screenshot your hotel's name in Chinese before leaving each morning. Taxi drivers don't read Pinyin.
Payments are fixed but not automatic. Since 2024, Alipay and WeChat Pay both accept foreign Visa and Mastercards for payments up to ¥8,000 ($1,120) per transaction. Set this up before you land , you'll scan QR codes to order food, buy metro tickets, pay for almost everything. One detail that burns people: some small street vendors and mom-and-pop restaurants only take domestic QR codes. Carry ¥500-1,000 ($70-140) in cash as backup. You can withdraw from ICBC or Bank of China ATMs at any airport with your home debit card.
Hotel friction still exists. About 30% of three-star and budget Chinese hotels still refuse foreign guests, technically illegal since 2019, but the "we can't take foreigners" line still gets used. Solution: book international chains (IHG, Marriott, Accor) or use Trip.com, which flags foreigner-friendly properties.
US citizens still need a visa or the 10-day transit exemption. 50 countries can now enter visa-free; the US is not on that list. You're stuck with either a standard L-visa (4-5 business days, around $140) or the 240-hour visa-free transit if you're flying through to a third country. My guide on how Americans can travel to China without a visa in 2026 covers the exact ports and rules.
The last hard thing is sensory. People spit. People smoke in some restaurants. People line-jump. Horns honk at 7am outside your hotel. If you can't let that roll off, you'll be miserable. If you can, none of it matters.
What Changed in 2026: The Visa-Free and Payment Updates
This is the section most "is china worth visiting" articles don't update. Here's what's actually different in 2026.
Visa-free expansion (February 17, 2026) : The UK and Canada joined the 30-day unilateral visa-free list, bringing the total to 50 countries . Germany, France, Italy, Spain, Australia, New Zealand, Japan, and South Korea were already on it. The policy runs through December 31, 2026 . Check the current list on the China National Immigration Administration site.
Americans, you're still transit-only . The 240-hour visa-free transit exemption covers 65 designated ports across 24 provinces. You need a confirmed onward ticket to a third country within 10 days. Hainan Island is separately visa-free for 61 countries (including the US) for 30 days, and you don't need a transit itinerary for that one.
Payment parity for foreigners . Since mid-2024, both Alipay and WeChat Pay let foreign tourists link Visa, Mastercard, Discover, and JCB cards. The transaction cap was raised in 2025. Setup means scanning your passport and adding your card. Our full China payment guide walks through the exact steps.
The numbers back it up . During the 2026 Lunar New Year holiday , 292,000 foreigners entered China under visa-free rules, up 35.8% year on year. American tourist spending grew 50% in 2025. French spending rose 160% . And the US State Department dropped China to Level 2 travel advisory in November 2024, the same level as France or Italy.
Who Should Skip China Right Now
Being honest is the whole point of this article, so let me save some people a miserable trip. If you're asking "should I visit China" and any of the following sounds like you, don't.
Skip China if you refuse to download new apps . You'll need Alipay, WeChat, a VPN, and a maps app that works offline. Non-negotiable. If that sounds like too much, go to Thailand.
Skip China if you want luxury service across the board . The Peninsula Beijing and Mandarin Oriental Shanghai are world-class. Outside those bubbles, service ranges from warm to completely indifferent. If you need polish, stick to Japan or Singapore.
Skip China on a 4-day trip . The country is too big. You'll spend half your trip in transit. Commit to 8-14 days or pick one city and go deep.
Skip China if you're easily overwhelmed by crowds and noise . Beijing on a Saturday afternoon is not for the sensitive. The Great Wall at Badaling will make you question humanity. Go to Mutianyu instead, or skip Beijing and start in Chengdu.
Skip China if you need English everywhere . Outside big-city hotels and Starbucks, most service staff speak zero English. You'll rely on translation apps, pointing, and patience.
Who Will Love China in 2026
Now the good news. These travelers will come back changed.
Food obsessives . If you've spent years chasing regional cuisine, Sichuan peppercorn, Cantonese dim sum, Yunnan mushrooms, Xinjiang lamb skewers, China is the only country that offers all of it in one trip. Nothing else compares. Not even close.
History and architecture nerds . The Forbidden City, the Terracotta Warriors, the Mogao Caves, the walled city of Pingyao, the hutongs of Beijing, Suzhou's classical gardens, this is five lifetimes of UNESCO sites. Japan has temples; China has temple dynasties.
Photographers . Zhangjiajie. Chongqing at night. The Guilin karsts. The Yuanyang rice terraces. Huangshan's sea of clouds. If your Instagram is more than flat lays, book a flight.
Curious, independent travelers . The people who loved India or Vietnam will love China. You have to figure stuff out. You'll get lost. You'll laugh about it later. The learning curve is the experience.
Anyone burned out on over-touristed Japan . Kyoto in April is a human traffic jam. Most of China in April is empty.
How to Prepare If You're Going (The Short Version)
Here's the 10-minute prep list:
Check your visa status for 2026 using the NIA official country list . 50 countries now have 30-day visa-free. Americans use the 240-hour transit exemption.

Download a VPN at home . NordVPN ($4-6/month) or ExpressVPN ($8-13/month) both work as of April 2026. Install and log in before you fly.

Install Alipay and WeChat Pay . Add your Visa or Mastercard. Takes 15 minutes. You'll scan your passport.

Get offline maps . Maps.me works as a backup.

Pack a backup credit card plus ¥500-1,000 ($70-140) in cash. Withdraw at ICBC or Bank of China ATMs on arrival.

Book trains early for peak season. Use 12306.cn or Trip.com. My Beijing to Shanghai train guide covers exact prices and stations.

Screenshot your hotel's Chinese address before leaving each morning. Taxi drivers don't read Pinyin.

And pick your dates carefully, the best time to visit China month by month depends heavily on which region you're hitting. Avoid Golden Week (Oct 1-7) and Chinese New Year unless you love crowds.
So, is China a good place to visit in 2026 ? If you've read this far and you're still nodding, yes. Book the flight. Prep the apps. Skip the cities that don't match your vibe. And know that you'll complain about the first 48 hours and then spend a year wondering why you ever left.
If the answer turns out to be yes, two places make the case better than the big cities do. Zhangjiajie is the sandstone-pillar landscape that Avatar borrowed, and it needs four days rather than the two most people give it. Quanzhou is the opposite kind of trip: one day, almost no foreign visitors, and the port where the maritime Silk Road actually started.
