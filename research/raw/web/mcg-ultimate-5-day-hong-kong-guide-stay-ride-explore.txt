Ultimate 5-Day Hong Kong Guide: Stay, Ride, Explore in 2026

Five days is the right amount of time for Hong Kong. You can cover both sides of Victoria Harbour, ride the Peak Tram, see the Big Buddha on Lantau, and still have a day for dim sum and Mong Kok without sprinting from one MTR station to the next. The trick is where you base yourself and how you use the Octopus card.

Where to stay: pick one base and stick to it
For a 5-day trip, hotel-hopping kills momentum. Pick one neighborhood near a cross-harbour MTR line and you can reach anywhere in 20 to 30 minutes.
Tsim Sha Tsui (Kowloon) — best for first-timers
Tsim Sha Tsui is where most travelers should sleep on a first visit. You are on the Tsuen Wan Line and the East Rail Line, five minutes on foot from the Star Ferry, and directly in front of the Avenue of Stars and the 8:00 PM Symphony of Lights. Budget rooms start around HK$500 per night in a shoebox guesthouse on Nathan Road, mid-range business hotels run HK$900 to HK$1,500, and harbour-view rooms push past HK$2,500.
Central and Sheung Wan (Hong Kong Island) — best for nightlife and hiking
Stay in Central if you want to walk home from SoHo bars at 2:00 AM or catch the Peak Tram without transferring. Rooms are smaller and run HK$1,200 to HK$2,000 for mid-range, and the neighborhood goes quiet on weekends when the finance crowd leaves.
Causeway Bay — best for shoppers
Surrounded by malls, Japanese department stores, and street markets. Good base if half your trip is shopping. Rooms tend to be tight and priced like Central.

The Octopus card and MTR basics
Get an Octopus card on day one
The Octopus is a contactless card that works on the MTR, buses, minibuses, ferries, trams, and almost every 7-Eleven and Circle K in the city. Buy one at the MTR service counter at the airport or any major station. Standard adult card costs HK$150 (HK$50 refundable deposit + HK$100 stored value). You can top up with cash or at self-service machines.
If you prefer your phone, the MTR Mobile app plus Octopus on Apple Pay or Google Wallet works the same way and skips the deposit.
MTR fares that actually matter
MTR fares range from HK$5 to HK$25 depending on distance. A typical Tsim Sha Tsui to Central ride runs HK$11. The Airport Express from Hong Kong Station to Airport is HK$115 one-way, HK$205 return. Avoid 8:00 to 9:30 AM if you can — trains on the Tsuen Wan Line get genuinely crushed.
Star Ferry — the HK$5 experience everyone should take
The Tsim Sha Tsui to Central Star Ferry is HK$5 upper deck on weekdays. Eight minutes across Victoria Harbour. Take it at sunset. Do not overthink this.
5-day itinerary
Day 1 — Kowloon and the harbour
Morning in Kowloon Park, then walk down Nathan Road to the Tsim Sha Tsui waterfront. Late afternoon at the Hong Kong Museum of Art (HK$10 general admission). Dinner in the area — try a cha chaan teng for HK$60 to HK$100, or dim sum at a local spot for HK$150 per person. Stay on the promenade for the Symphony of Lights at 8:00 PM.
Day 2 — Central, SoHo, and Victoria Peak
Take the Star Ferry from Tsim Sha Tsui to Central. Walk up through SoHo via the Mid-Levels escalators (longest outdoor escalator system in the world, free to ride). Visit Man Mo Temple on Hollywood Road. In the late afternoon, queue for the Peak Tram at Garden Road — adult return is HK$88, or HK$108 combined with the Sky Terrace 428 observation deck. Go up at least an hour before sunset for the best photo. Dinner back in Central or SoHo.
Day 3 — Lantau Island and the Big Buddha
Take the Tung Chung Line MTR to Tung Chung station (about 35 minutes from Central). Ride the Ngong Ping 360 cable car to Ngong Ping Village — standard round-trip is HK$270 adult, crystal cabin HK$350. At the top, climb the 268 steps to the Tian Tan Buddha and visit Po Lin Monastery. On the way back, take bus 21 to Tai O fishing village to see the stilt houses and dried seafood stalls. Bus back to Tung Chung, MTR home.
Day 4 — Hong Kong Island south side
Take bus 6 or 6X from Exchange Square in Central to Repulse Bay (around HK$8). Walk the beach, then continue to Stanley Market for lunch and souvenirs. If you have the legs, hike the Dragon's Back trail from Shek O Road — around two hours end to end, ending at Big Wave Bay where you can catch bus 9 back to Shau Kei Wan MTR. One of the best urban hikes in Asia.
Day 5 — Mong Kok, dim sum, and departure
Start in Mong Kok: Ladies' Market, Sneaker Street, and the Goldfish Market. For lunch, queue at Tim Ho Wan Mong Kok branch for baked BBQ pork buns (around HK$30 for three). Use the afternoon for last-minute shopping in Causeway Bay via the MTR, then take the Airport Express (HK$115) or a taxi (HK$300 to HK$400 from Kowloon) to HKG.
If you are traveling with young kids, swap Day 4 for Hong Kong Disneyland (MTR Disneyland Resort Line from Sunny Bay, one-day ticket HK$799 adult). If you prefer marine life and roller coasters, swap in Ocean Park (HK$498 adult, direct bus 629 from Central Pier).
Practical tips for 2026
Visa and entry
Most Western passports (US, UK, EU, Australia, Canada) get 90 days visa-free in Hong Kong. Mainland China visa rules do not apply — Hong Kong runs its own immigration. If you plan a side trip to Shenzhen, you will need a separate China visa or the 240- hour visa -free transit (check the latest rules for your nationality before flying).
Weather and when to go
October to early December is the best window: dry, mild, low humidity. February and March are cool but damp. June through September is hot, humid, and brings typhoons — pack a compact umbrella, and watch the Hong Kong Observatory signals (T8 and above means everything shuts).
Payment and apps
Bring some HK$ cash for wet markets, street food, and the Star Ferry backup, but otherwise Octopus plus a credit card covers you. Alipay HK and WeChat Pay HK both work but are optional for non-residents. MTR Mobile, HKeMobility, and Google Maps are all you need for navigation — the Amap version used on the mainland is not needed here.
Power and SIM
Hong Kong uses Type G (UK 3-pin) plugs. Data SIM cards at the airport 7-Eleven run HK$80 to HK$150 for 5-8 days of unlimited 4G/5G. Most hotels and all MTR stations have free Wi-Fi.
Frequently asked questions
Is 5 days enough for Hong Kong?
Yes, and arguably the ideal length. Three days feels rushed, a week starts to drag unless you add Macau or Shenzhen. Five days lets you cover both sides of the harbour plus one outlying island.
Do I need cash in Hong Kong?
A little. Most places take Octopus or card, but wet markets, dai pai dong street stalls, and some taxis outside the center still prefer cash. HK$500 in small notes is plenty for a 5-day trip.
Is the Airport Express worth HK$115?
For speed, yes — 24 minutes from HKG to Hong Kong Station. If you are on a tight budget, the A21 bus to Tsim Sha Tsui is around HK$33 and takes about 50 minutes. Taxi from airport to Kowloon runs HK$250 to HK$300 plus tunnel fees.
Can I use mainland China apps in Hong Kong?
Alipay and WeChat work through the HK-based versions (Alipay HK, WeChat Pay HK), but most travelers get by fine with Octopus and a credit card. Do not assume you need to set up the mainland apps before your trip — you usually do not.
What should I skip if I only have 4 days instead of 5?
Drop the south side day (Day 4). Repulse Bay and Stanley are pleasant but not essential. Victoria Peak, Lantau, and a night on the Kowloon waterfront are the non-negotiables.
