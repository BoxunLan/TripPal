WeChat Pay for Foreigners in 2026: What It Costs and Where It Breaks

The rule that decides what WeChat Pay costs you is a single number: ¥200. At ¥200 or less, foreign cards pay nothing. Above it, Tencent takes 3% of the whole payment.
A metro ride, a bowl of noodles and most of what you buy from a stall fall under the line. A hotel night and a sit-down dinner for two do not — unless you split the bill, which is the one habit worth forming before you land.
None of this was true before July 2023. Until then a foreign card only worked through a narrow pilot, started in 2019 and tied to specific merchants, and most visitors genuinely did need a Chinese bank account and a cooperative local friend. Tencent opened it up that month, and the ¥200 line has been part of the service since that launch.

Those first two rows are not cherry-picked: a street food meal in China realistically runs ¥15–35, and Beijing's subway fares start at ¥3. Most of a tourist's day sits below ¥200 without trying. ( what street food actually costs )
Splitting is normal here, not awkward. Chinese diners settle bills this way by default, and every restaurant QR code accepts several payers for one table. Two people paying ¥190 each is a fee-free ¥380 dinner.
There is also a window worth using. If you link an international card to WeChat for the first time by 31 December 2026, Tencent waives the fee on up to ¥1,000 of spending a day for 90 consecutive days. The clock starts with your first payment, not with the day you add the card, so adding it before you fly does not use up any of those days.

The People's Bank of China announced that ceiling on 1 March 2024, when it told Tenpay, the company behind WeChat Pay, and Alipay to raise it from US$1,000 per transaction and US$10,000 a year. Your own limits sit inside it. WeChat Pay sets them per transaction, per month and per year, raises them once you have verified your identity, and changes them from time to time, so go by the figures WeChat shows you rather than any printed in a guide. Everyday spending on a two-week trip stays well inside them. If you are staying long enough to hit them, you want a Chinese bank account instead.
Which cards work
Seven card brands bind directly: Visa, Mastercard, American Express, JCB, Discover and Diners Club since Tencent opened this up in July 2023, and overseas-issued UnionPay since November 2024. No Chinese bank account, no Chinese phone number.
Two cases the English guides skip:
If your home bank issues a UnionPay-branded card — common across Asia, the Middle East and Russia — bind that one. UnionPay has run promotions that drop the 3% on larger payments as well, but only for a promotional period, so check the fee on the payment screen before you confirm.

If you already have WeChat Pay HK, you do not need a mainland setup: the Hong Kong wallet pays mainland merchants in Hong Kong dollars. It stays a separate wallet, though, with its own limits, and money cannot move between it and a mainland WeChat Pay wallet.

TenPayGo is a separate app
Tencent launched TenPayGo publicly on 24 September 2026. It is a standalone payment app for visitors, built by the WeChat Pay team, and it pays at merchants that accept WeChat Pay. It is not the wallet inside WeChat, though, and the two work differently:
Sign-up: TenPayGo registers you with an email address. The WeChat wallet needs a WeChat account first, set up with a phone number that can receive SMS codes.

What you can link: TenPayGo takes cards from seven international networks, Visa and Mastercard among them, plus Apple Pay and a growing list of overseas e-wallets. Inside WeChat, you link one of the seven card brands above.

What it covers: TenPayGo's service terms limit it to paying merchants in mainland China, so there are no red packets and no transfers between people. Its terms say any card fee is set out on the payment page.

If you only need to pay for things on a short trip, TenPayGo is worth a look. If you want WeChat for messaging anyway, its wallet pays at the same shops without a second app.
Setup, in four moves
The app walks you through most of this. The parts that go wrong are in the next section.
Install WeChat before you fly. Google Play is blocked in China; on Android you would otherwise be side-loading an APK from wechat.com. Get the Tencent app, not WeCom — that's the business version.

Register with your home phone number and country code. Me → Pay and Services → Wallet turns on WeChat Pay.

Bank Cards → Add Card. Expect a small verification charge that gets refunded, or a 3D Secure prompt from your bank.

Identity Verification → Foreigner / Foreign Passport. Photograph the passport page flat, then the selfie. Tencent says a passport check can clear in seconds, though some take longer.

Until that last step clears, the wallet only lets you spend up to a limited amount. Verification is what raises that cap.
Two ways to pay, and knowing which is which
Chinese merchants use two opposite QR flows, and picking the wrong one is the most common way a foreigner holds up a queue.
Small restaurants, street stalls and taxis: you scan them. Plus icon → Scan, point at the printed code on the counter, type the amount yourself, confirm.

Convenience stores, supermarkets and chains: they scan you. Plus icon → Money, and hold up the rotating code for the cashier's scanner.

Typing your own amount feels wrong the first time. It is normal — the stall owner watches the screen and nods.
When verification rejects you
Almost every rejection is one of four things, and three of them are photography.

Pick the passport's issuing country on the first verification screen, not the phone's.

A card that gets refused is usually your own bank, not Tencent — an anti-fraud hold on an unfamiliar merchant name. Wait five minutes and retry the same card. If it fails again, call your bank and tell them Tencent Pay is legitimate.
If verification still fails after you have matched the name and cleaned up the photo, call WeChat Pay support: 95017 inside China, +86 571 95017 from abroad. One more thing that catches people — a failed attempt sends the retry prompt over data, not SMS, so being offline at that moment stalls you. A travel eSIM installed before departure avoids it; our eSIM comparison covers which ones reach Google and WhatsApp without a VPN.
Red packets and transfers
Red packets — hóngbāo, 红包 — are where a foreign-card wallet differs most from a Chinese user's. Don't plan on a Chinese friend's holiday hóngbāo reaching you.
Paying merchants: fine. That is what the wallet is for.

Red packets and money transfers: not supported with international cards, according to WeChat's help center.

Withdrawing a balance to your card: not possible. Withdrawals only go to debit cards issued in mainland China.

If money does end up in your balance, the practical move is to spend it on your last few meals rather than leave it sitting.
What a passport-verified wallet can and can't do
A foreign card plus passport verification gets you a consumption wallet, not a bank. You can scan to pay merchants, use mini-programs, and buy inside apps. You cannot move money the way a Chinese user does — no red packets, no transfers, no withdrawals to your own card.
In practice that covers everything a visitor does. Shanghai Disney, the Forbidden City ticket mini-program and Trip.com all take it, as does every metro gate.

Most people end up with both: WeChat for daily spending, Alipay as the fallback when a merchant's WeChat terminal is being difficult. The fees match — the same ¥200 line, the same 3% — so switching apps won't save you anything on a large payment. Setting up Alipay takes similar steps, and it is worth doing on the same evening.
For what happens when neither wallet works — and it happens, mostly at small vendors and older taxis — foreign credit cards in China covers the cash fallback.
FAQ
Do I need a Chinese bank account?
No. Foreign Visa, Mastercard, Amex, Discover, JCB and Diners Club cards have bound directly since July 2023, and overseas-issued UnionPay cards since November 2024. No Chinese account, no Chinese phone number.
Is there a minimum top-up?
No. A linked foreign card pays each purchase directly, so there is nothing to top up. The top-ups older guides mention were for Alipay's Tour Pass. That prepaid card was gone from Alipay by May 2023, when its issuer, Bank of Shanghai, launched a new card called TourCard.
What if I lose my phone in China?
Log in from another device with your phone number and password, or freeze the account at weixin110.qq.com, WeChat's security center. A friend can also freeze it for you from their own WeChat. Then call your card issuer.
