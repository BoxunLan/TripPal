You finally decided to book your Beijing trip, you opened the Forbidden City's official WeChat mini-program, typed your passport number into the ID field, and got a red error message you could not read. Welcome to one of the most frustrating puzzles in China travel. Every major attraction now runs on a real-name reservation system built for 18-digit Chinese ID numbers, and passports break the form about half the time.
The good news: you absolutely can book tickets to every major site in China with nothing but your passport. You just cannot always do it on the official Chinese channel. This 2026 guide shows you exactly which platforms work for which attractions, what they cost, and what to do when a booking falls through the day before your visit.

Why Chinese ID is a Problem for Foreign Tourists
Since 2017, nearly every museum, scenic area and theme park in China has moved to real-name ticketing (实名制). The system was built around the Chinese resident ID card, which carries a fixed 18-character format. When the software was upgraded to accept passports in 2019 and 2020, some venues integrated it cleanly. Others bolted it on as an afterthought, which is why the Forbidden City's WeChat mini-program still occasionally rejects a passport that worked yesterday.
The 3 layers of the Chinese ticketing system
Official channels — WeChat mini-programs (微信小程序), official websites and the 12306 rail app. Cheapest prices but patchy English and inconsistent passport support.

Domestic Chinese OTAs — Ctrip 携程 in Chinese, Meituan 美团, Dianping 大众点评. Marginal markup, Chinese-only interface.

Foreigner-friendly resellers — Trip.com (English Ctrip), Klook, KKday, GetYourGuide. A 5 to 15 percent markup buys you English interface, passport-native flow and customer support in your time zone.

When you still need the official channel
A handful of sites, most notably the Forbidden City, sell a limited pool of cheap tickets through their official WeChat only. Resellers package those same tickets with a small service fee. Unless you are on a tight backpacker budget, the fee is worth it.

How to Book the Forbidden City with a Passport
The Forbidden City (故宫博物院) sells 30,000 tickets per day, split across morning and afternoon slots, and often sells out 3 to 5 days ahead in April, May, October and during Chinese public holidays. Tickets open 7 days in advance at midnight Beijing time.
Route A: Trip.com (recommended)
Open Trip.com, search "Forbidden City" under Things to Do

Pick your date and a morning (8:30-11:00) or afternoon (11:00-15:30) slot

Enter each traveler's passport details exactly as they appear on the photo page

Pay with Visa, Mastercard or Alipay Tour Pass

Save the confirmation email and QR code

At the Meridian Gate (午门) entrance, show your passport at the foreigner lane

Trip.com charges roughly ¥75 for the standard summer ticket vs ¥60 official price. You get an English confirmation, a real refund button and 24/7 chat if something fails.
Route B: Official WeChat mini-program
This works if you have WeChat installed, a Chinese friend who can translate, and patience. Search "故宫博物院" inside WeChat, tap 预约购票, create an account, and enter your passport where the form asks for 证件号码. Some passport formats (especially those with leading zeros) fail silently. If it works, you pay ¥60. If it fails, fall back to Trip.com.
What to do if the Forbidden City is sold out
Dongcheng Palace Museum Evening Tour on Klook — includes palace entry on select dates

Skip-the-line with guide on Trip.com — extra ¥200-400 but bypasses the reservation lottery entirely

Hotel concierge — Peninsula Beijing, Rosewood and Waldorf Astoria concierges hold small daily allocations

Great Wall: Mutianyu vs Badaling vs Simatai
The three main Great Wall sections have very different booking rules.
Mutianyu (慕田峪) — the foreigner favorite
Mutianyu is 70 kilometers northeast of Beijing, less crowded than Badaling, with an English-speaking ticket desk and a toboggan run down. In 2026, adult entry is ¥40, cable car round-trip ¥140, toboggan ¥100. You can walk up and buy same-day. Klook and Trip.com both sell the standard combo at around ¥180-220, plus round-trip shuttle from downtown Beijing for ¥80-150.
Badaling (八达岭)
The most famous and most crowded section. Entry is ¥35, no real-time reservation needed for foreigners, but the parking and train lines get brutal during holidays. The Badaling High-Speed Rail from Beijing North Station (北京北站) is ¥30 and takes 30 minutes. Book the train on Trip.com with your passport, not the 12306 app first time.
Simatai (司马台)
Steeper, more atmospheric, and connected to the Gubei Water Town resort. Tickets ¥40, must book online because the scenic area enforces a daily cap. Trip.com handles this cleanly. The nighttime lighting tour (¥90) sells out in peak months.
Shanghai Disney Resort
Shanghai Disneyland (上海迪士尼度假区) is the easiest booking on this list because the official English channel actually works.
Three ways to buy
Official Shanghai Disney Resort English website — Chinese Visa and Mastercard sometimes fail here, so keep a backup

Trip.com — mirrors official prices with a small service fee, takes all foreign cards, supports Alipay Tour Pass

Klook — frequently runs 5-8 percent discount bundles with hotel or with Shanghai metro day pass

Regular one-day tickets in 2026 run ¥475 off-peak, ¥599 regular, ¥719 peak, ¥799 peak-plus. Add ¥400-800 for Premier Access (the skip-the-line option), which is worth it for Tron Lightcycle Power Run and Zootopia land.
Passport entry at the gate
Shanghai Disney reads passports at the turnstile. Bring the actual passport, not a photocopy. Children under 16 need parent-linked reservations — do this in one transaction on Trip.com rather than splitting across accounts.
Terracotta Warriors and Xi'an Sites
The Emperor Qinshihuang's Mausoleum Site Museum (秦始皇帝陵博物院) in Xi'an requires real-name booking and enforces it strictly. Tickets are ¥120 in peak season, ¥100 off-peak.
Booking workflow
Book on Trip.com 3-7 days ahead

Pick a 2-hour entry window

Enter each traveler's passport number; children need their own ticket

At the entrance, go to the "Foreign Passport" lane on the far right

A staff member scans your passport and matches it to the reservation

Pair the Warriors with the Xi'an City Wall (¥54) and the Shaanxi History Museum (free, but must be reserved 3 days out on the official WeChat mini-program, which is the one exception that rarely rejects passports because it was rebuilt in 2024).
Other High-Demand Sites
Zhangjiajie and Avatar Mountains
Zhangjiajie National Forest Park entry is ¥228 for a 4-day pass. The Bailong Elevator is ¥72, cable cars ¥72-118. Book the main entry on Trip.com, and the individual cable cars can still be bought on-site with cash or Alipay. Tianmen Mountain (separate park) ¥258 via Klook — Klook runs the cleanest combo including the glass skywalk.
Chengdu Panda Base
Dujiangyan and Bifengxia research bases both cap daily visitors. Book 3 days ahead on Trip.com for ¥55 (Chengdu Research Base) or ¥90 (Dujiangyan). The panda keeper program at Dujiangyan is ¥700 and sells out 2-4 weeks in advance.
Huangshan (Yellow Mountain)
Scenic area entry ¥190, cable cars ¥80-90 each direction. Book on Trip.com and separately reserve your hotel on the mountain (Beihai Hotel or Paiyunlou) because same-day arrivals in clear weather get turned away.
When Online Booking Fails the Night Before
A reasonable mental model: expect one booking to misfire per 10-day China trip. Have a fallback.
Hotel concierge
Upper-midrange and luxury hotels (Shangri-La, Jin Jiang, Pullman, Waldorf Astoria, Rosewood, any InterContinental) keep small daily inventories or have a ticket agent on retainer. Walk to the concierge desk, say the attraction name, hand over your passport, and pay a ¥20-50 service fee. This saves roughly 40 percent of travelers who thought their trip was ruined.
Same-day window
The Forbidden City releases a small number of unsold tickets at the Meridian Gate from 08:30. Show up at 08:00, bring passport and cash. Great Wall Mutianyu, Temple of Heaven, Lama Temple, Summer Palace and most Shanghai sites sell same-day at the window without reservation.
Last-minute apps
Dianping (大众点评) occasionally lists skip-the-line guided tours at deep discounts the night before. Xiaohongshu (小红书) has crowdsourced real-time availability posts from other travelers — search the attraction name in English plus "passport" and you will find fresh threads.
Payment and Refund Reality
Trip.com, Klook and KKday take any international Visa, Mastercard, Amex or Discover. Alipay Tour Pass and WeChat Pay (once you complete the foreigner setup) also work. Most tickets are refundable up to 24 hours ahead with a 5-10 percent processing fee; within 24 hours you typically get nothing back. Shanghai Disney is non-refundable on purchase day.
Frequently Asked Questions
Can I book Forbidden City tickets the same day I arrive in Beijing?
Only if inventory is not exhausted, which it usually is from April through October and on every public holiday. Book 7 days ahead through Trip.com the moment you confirm your flight.
Do children need their own tickets and passports?
Yes. Every person entering the Forbidden City, Shanghai Disney, Terracotta Warriors and most national parks needs an individual reservation tied to a passport. Even infants need a booking (often free) at Shanghai Disney.
What if my passport number has changed since I booked the ticket?
Contact Trip.com or Klook customer support immediately with both old and new passport numbers. They can update the reservation up to 6 hours before entry for most sites. For the Forbidden City, a name-and-date-of-birth match at the gate is usually enough even if the passport number shifted.
Is WeChat Pay required to buy attraction tickets?
No. Every foreigner-friendly channel listed above accepts a regular Visa or Mastercard. WeChat Pay and Alipay are useful for on-site food and cable cars, but not required for the ticket itself.
Why does Trip.com cost more than the official price?
Trip.com bundles the ticket with English support, refund processing and payment gateway fees for foreign cards. The ¥10-30 premium is cheap compared to standing at a ticket window where no one speaks English at 7 a.m. during a Golden Week crowd.
The same passport-number problem shows up at every real-name gate, and the workarounds differ by system. Train tickets on 12306 take a foreign passport directly with no booking fee, which makes the OTA premium harder to justify there than here. Jiuzhaigou is the hardest case of all — no gate sales, a daily cap, and face recognition at the turnstile. And linking a foreign card to WeChat Pay is what unlocks most of the official mini-programmes in the first place.
